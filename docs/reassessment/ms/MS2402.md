## 1. CRUX

The lecture's real thesis is not "which stocks to buy" but WHY good
fundamentals in small/micro-caps can go unrewarded for years: ownership
composition (who is structurally able to buy a stock) matters as much as
the business's numbers, and that composition changes in discrete steps as
market cap crosses institutional-mandate thresholds -- so "where is the
smart money going" really means "what market-cap/liquidity threshold does
a business need to cross before institutional (FII/DII/mutual fund) money
can even legally/practically enter it, and what promoter/ownership
behaviour should a reader watch as a tell for that transition."

## 2. MECHANISM

- Below roughly a 5,000-10,000 crore market cap, "no institutional mandate
  requires ownership, no index force is passifying, there is no financial
  or reputational cost to ignoring it entirely." (MS2402 NO-TS) A large
  mutual fund cannot build a meaningful position without owning an
  unrealistic share of the free float: "Consider this, for a 5000 crore
  fund, thinking of taking a 2% allocation would mean a 100 crore
  investment." (MS2402 NO-TS) and, for a smaller/more promoter-heavy name,
  "with 75% promoter holding investments amounts to 25% of free float of
  the company." (MS2402 NO-TS)
- Crossing that threshold flips the ownership regime discretely, not
  gradually: "cross the 10, 000 crore threshold and ecosystem changes
  completely. Mutual funds can deploy at scale without moving the price.
  PMS AIF can enter and exit. Research coverage begins." (MS2402 NO-TS)
- The mechanism is illustrated by a real case: "Newland is a good example.
  I think stock was at some 3, 000 crores at like 1500 crore market cap
  then went to be 16, 000, 17, 000 crore market cap with DI ownership
  today." (MS2402 NO-TS) -- "Newland" is the transcript's ASR rendering of
  Neuland Laboratories [likely "Neuland Laboratories"] -- with "White Oak I
  think is the largest institution shareholder there, right?" (MS2402
  NO-TS) named as the specific institutional holder that entered post-rerate.
- Below that institutional floor, the marginal buyer is a different class
  of capital entirely -- "the marginal buyer is not a mutual fund there is
  no DI or FII who is buying there are usually PMSs, AIFs or UH&I's, family
  offices or retail investors who are buying the stock" (MS2402 NO-TS) --
  and this capital "only arrives when sentiment is decisively positive and
  risk appetite is high," at which point re-rating is abrupt: "A stock at
  10x for 3 years can move to 25x in a few months." (MS2402 NO-TS)
- The instructor reframes the "E" (earnings) part of P/E as secondary to a
  liquidity/sentiment variable: "sentiment and liquidity to peer ratio is
  perception upon earnings ratio to perception is the P part of the P
  ratio, that is sentiment and liquidity." (MS2402 NO-TS) [garbled ASR --
  the intended sense is that the "Price" in P/E is driven by
  perception/sentiment/liquidity, independent of whether earnings are
  actually good]
- As sentiment and liquidity flow INTO a name, promoters and price action
  interact reflexively -- both a benign version ("a lot of DIs and Fi has
  entered and promoter started selling because sentiment and liquidity
  entered" (MS2402 NO-TS), i.e. promoters selling into strength once
  institutions arrive) and a warning-sign version, where a bull market
  "creates the incentive" for a subset of promoters to manage the numbers:
  "Sales get stretched, profits get embarrassed and in some cases numbers
  are manufactured outright, bull market creates the incentive, absence of
  institutional scrutiny creates the opportunity" (MS2402 NO-TS).
- A specific promoter-behaviour tell is given as a named worked example:
  "Shivalik by MetalMew all of a sudden they announced some five, six
  institutional meets." (MS2402 NO-TS) [likely "Shivalik Bimetal
  Controls"], followed the next week by "way next week, you know, 15% of
  company has been sold by one of the promoters." (MS2402 NO-TS) -- a
  sudden burst of investor-relations/institutional-meet activity preceding
  a large promoter block sale.
- On the way down, the same reflexivity runs in reverse and the instructor
  names a specific corporate-action tell: "who saw that Pref issues will be
  cancelled in this cycle of companies?" (MS2402 NO-TS) -- cancelled
  preferential-allotment/fund-raise plans as a distress signal in a falling
  cycle.
- Fund-flow direction is also read through fund-house behaviour rather than
  a single flow number: "ICICI potential mutual fund just resumed
  subscription in the small cap schemes" (MS2402 NO-TS) [likely "ICICI
  Prudential"] and new small-cap scheme launches -- "Sunil Singh Hanyaji
  has just recently launched a small cap scheme." (MS2402 NO-TS) [ASR
  garble, instructor name unresolved] and "even if you check about Helios,
  like they also launched a small cap scheme." (MS2402 NO-TS) -- are read
  as a signal that "market veterans" see value precisely when trailing
  small-cap returns look bad.

## 3. SIGNALS

- [SOFT] Institutional-ownership threshold: a company below roughly
  ₹5,000-10,000cr market cap structurally cannot attract mutual
  fund/institutional ownership at scale; crossing ~₹10,000cr flips this.
  Evidence: "cross the 10, 000 crore threshold and ecosystem changes
  completely." (MS2402 NO-TS); Neuland ("Newland") case above.
- [SOFT] Promoter shareholding % combined with fund AUM size determines
  whether institutional capital can realistically enter (free-float math),
  independent of business quality. Evidence: the 75%-promoter / 25%-of-
  free-float example (MS2402 NO-TS) above.
- [SOFT] A sudden cluster of promoter-initiated institutional
  meets/investor outreach, followed shortly by a large promoter stake
  sale, is flagged as a caution pattern (Shivalik example, MS2402 NO-TS).
- [SOFT] Cancellation of a preferential issue / fund-raise mid-cycle is
  named as a distress tell in a falling small-cap market (MS2402 NO-TS).
- [SOFT] Small-cap mutual-fund scheme reopenings/launches by established
  fund houses (ICICI Prudential, Helios, and one ASR-garbled fund house
  name) after a period of poor trailing small-cap returns is read as a
  contrarian institutional-flow signal (MS2402 NO-TS, three citations
  above).
- [SOFT] DII entry alongside promoter selling ("DIs and Fi has entered and
  promoter started selling") is described neutrally, as a marker of the
  sentiment/liquidity phase a stock is in, not as a red flag on its own
  (MS2402 NO-TS).
- [JUDGE] Whether a given re-rating is durable (business quality catching
  up to institutional attention, e.g. Neuland/Persistent) versus a pure
  sentiment/liquidity re-rating that can reverse is left to the investor's
  own business-quality judgement throughout the lecture; no test is given
  to distinguish the two mechanically.
- [HARD] None of the above are computable from the ladder's current
  fetched fields (screener.in ratios, price series). Market cap itself
  IS already fetched (market_cap_floor-001 uses it), but the specific
  ~₹10,000cr institutional-ownership-regime-change threshold discussed
  here is a different number from the ladder's existing ₹1,000cr
  liquidity floor (see Section 4).

activity, institutional-meet cadence, insider buying, or delivery

literally named in the assignment) remains SOFT/unencodable from this
