## 1. CRUX

This lecture is not trying to change one investing decision — it is trying to change a
workflow: it argues that manual, book-derived screening (Minervini-style earnings/price
breakout reading) does not scale to 5,000 listed businesses, and that the instructor's own
"Stock Scans" tool exists precisely to mechanize the same qualitative pattern-spotting he
teaches from the book, at speed, so the researcher's time goes into reading the shortlisted
business rather than manually hunting for it: "why these tools exist, you can do this work
quickly, this tool is leveraged and you can do it in a minute, Then you invest your time on
doing that business." (MS1005B 00:01:24)

## 2. MECHANISM

- Start from an industry/index-level read, not a stock-level one: use a custom index (a
  basket of names in one theme) to see whether the INDUSTRY is breaking to new highs or
  sitting in a channel/stage-4 base first — "if industry is having a relative strength and
  if industry is hitting stage 2 or in an all-time high territory, then the businesses that
  will be considering it will be doing the same thing." (MS1005B 00:09:51)
- Fundamental "earnings breakout" scan: find businesses whose PAT was flat/range-bound for
  the trailing several quarters and then broke out in the latest quarter — "Now the magic
  begins. Now what I do here is, I put the latest quarter." (MS1005B 00:16:34-00:16:40)
- Confirm with a technical breakout scan (price making a new high after a long base) so the
  fundamental and technical stories corroborate each other, then read the concall/transcript
  to find the causal story behind the number (margin guidance, capacity, volume growth) —
  demonstrated live on Usha Martin's concall notes. (MS1005B 00:36:32-00:37:19)
- Margin-expansion scan: look for OPM rising for 3-4 consecutive quarters, not one quarter,
  because "only one quarter is not expansionary some structure or story is changing there"
  (MS1005B 00:42:58). Read the concall to find the product-mix/premiumization cause (his own
  analogy: an Americano at 10% margin vs. a "Redhead Frappuccino" the business can price at
  50% margin — MS1005B 00:39:20-00:39:47).
- Turnaround / "loss to profit" scan: screen recent-IPO/beaten-down names whose PAT flips
  from negative to positive quarter-on-quarter, on the theory that post-IPO "peak optimism"
  names round-trip through a stage-4 base before a real re-rate — "After that base, you can
  see stage 4 or stage 3 consolidation" (MS1005B 00:52:41-00:52:54), and "The business becomes
  profitable and the story of the business is changed on paper." (MS1005B 00:53:03)
- Qualitative/event scans (warrant issues, acquisitions, promoter/institutional buying,
  who's asking questions on concalls) are read for WHY, not taken at face value: a warrant
  issue or FII/DII stake increase is a prompt to go read the business, not a buy signal by
  itself — "we are not saying you have to apply in every warrant issue what we are saying is
  you have to look two things that the use of the warranty issue is going to be and who is
  participating in that." (MS1005B 00:29:07-00:29:20)
- Explicit backtest discipline on the institutional-buying scan: he ran it, got 54 names,
  then checked relative strength a year later and found only about half actually worked —
  "the related strength came out to be only 25 business are outperforming after FI, DI, Z
  have increase your stake when you were scanning and rest are under performing." (MS1005B
  01:01:36-01:01:50) His own conclusion is that industry/stage context, not the FII/DII flag
  alone, explains the difference (PCBL/BCBL case below).
- Close with combination/peer-comparison ("P2P") scans that stack technical relative
  strength with fundamental ratios across an industry so relative — not absolute — strength
  drives the shortlist. (MS1005B 01:19:15-01:20:09)

## 4. WHAT THE LADDER MISSES

(a) canslim_sales-001 / canslim_pat-001 (G0, sales YoY >=15% / PAT YoY >=20%) are QoQ growth-
rate gates. This lecture's headline fundamental scan is structurally different: a level-shift
"earnings breakout" test (multiple flat trailing quarters, then a single quarter with PAT
>30% above that flat base — MS1005B 00:14:52), explicitly aimed at names just beginning to
inflect, not names already sustaining a 15-20% run-rate. It is a genuinely distinct, ladder-
uncovered screening pattern (a level-break vs. a growth-rate floor), not a restatement of
canslim at a different number.

(b) canslim_market_direction-001 already captures the idea of reading a CANSLIM pass against
overall market/index direction, as an observation. This lecture adds concrete, ladder-uncovered
mechanics for a related but distinct judgement call: read the SCAN RESULT against industry/
index STAGE (2 vs 4) before trusting
it, using a custom index of peer names as the diagnostic tool — "is this industry in stage 2
or stage 4?" is asked explicitly as the gate before any institutional-buying signal is
trusted (MS1005B 00:57:56-00:59:27), and is corroborated by the instructor's own backtest
showing the raw FII/DII signal alone worked on only ~46% of flagged names (25/54, MS1005B
01:01:36-01:01:50). No rule or observation in the current rulebook encodes an industry-stage
precondition at all — canslim_market_direction-001 only covers broad index direction, not
sector/industry-relative staging, which this lecture treats as the primary filter, stock-
level scans as secondary.

(c) The rulebook has no rule or observation for institutional-ownership change
(FII/DII stake delta) as a signal at all, despite it being one of this lecture's five named
scan categories and carrying an explicit, sourced reliability caveat (b, above) that would
make a good observation if ever added.

(d) The rulebook has no rule or observation for warrant issues, acquisition announcements,
or "who is asking concall questions" as signals — all three are named, worked scan
categories here, each with the same instructor caveat: the event itself is not the signal,
its stated USE/rationale (read from text) is. These are consistent with the CLAUDE.md
project's own SOFT-signal category (annual-report/concall text this ladder does not fetch)
and would need new fetches to encode, not a rulebook change to an existing rule.

(e) The margin-expansion scans (10% OPM QoQ improvement for 3-4 straight quarters; separately,
>20% margin expansion + 10% sales growth + >6% latest OPM) and the 4-quarter PAT-consistency
scan (>10% each quarter) are distinct, numerically specific momentum screens the rulebook
has nothing resembling — they test consistency/direction of margin and profit trend, not a
point-in-time growth-rate threshold the way canslim_pat-001 does.

(f) Nothing here conditions or contradicts G1 (leverage-001), G3 (capital_efficiency_gate-001
/ roe_lender-001), G8 (entry_rsi-001 / entry_adx-001), or any valuation observation
(pe_context-001, ev_to_ebitda_context-001, etc.) — none of those metrics, thresholds, or
even the words RSI-as-an-entry-signal, ADX, ROCE, or debt-to-equity are used as SCREENING
BARS anywhere in this transcript. "RSI" appears only once as a conceptual analogy (RSI vs.
relative strength, a classroom exam-scores illustration, MS1005B 00:07:29-00:08:08) and "ROC"
appears once as a company-specific concall figure (Usha Martin's own reported 20.6% ROC,
MS1005B 00:36:42), not a stated universal bar. On the ladder's existing gates, this lecture
adds and conditions nothing — say so plainly.

(g) The 1,000 crore market-cap floor used twice in this lecture (MS1005B 00:11:25, 00:17:50)
is consistent with, and does not add new information to, market_cap_floor-001 (already an
observation, already sourced from a different lecture at the same >=1000 anchor). Not a new
finding.
