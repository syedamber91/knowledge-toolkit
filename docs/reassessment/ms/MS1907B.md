## 1. CRUX

This lecture is not about picking good businesses -- it is about timing entries and exits around one specific corporate-action catalyst (a demerger/scheme of arrangement), teaching that the catalyst's own information effect is priced in within about two weeks, after which the trade must be re-judged as an ordinary fundamental bet or dropped.

## 2. MECHANISM

- The demerger process itself is a fixed corporate-action sequence -- board approval, stock-exchange filing, NCLT/creditor/shareholder meetings, NCLT approval, then a record date that fixes who receives the resulting-company shares -- and "from the announcement of demerger is anywhere between 12 to 18 months it takes a long time" (MS1907B 00:01:40), restated later as "D-Mergers will take almost 12-18 months to be done, if not more" (MS1907B 01:07:05); one case (Orient Refractories / RHI Magnesita) took 3 years because the restructuring happened at the promoter level first (MS1907B 01:07:47-01:08:29).
- Two entry styles, chosen on the investor's own risk/mandate profile, not a formula: pre-demerger means buying the pre-split company purely on fundamentals with no announcement trigger required and a 12-18 month wait, best when the SOTP gap versus the current market cap is large (the Jubilant Industries math: implied value 2000-3000cr vs. an actual market cap of 700cr, MS1907B 00:13:23); post-demerger means buying the newly-listed small entity specifically to catch the mechanical forced-selling dip.
- The forced-selling mechanic is mandate-driven, not fundamentals-driven: institutions holding the pre-merger parent for one segment cannot legally or practically hold the small spun-off piece (market-cap or sector mandates), so they dump it "especially if the market cap is below 2-3,000 crores because there are certain mandates" (MS1907B 00:13:51) -- this creates a temporary, mechanical mispricing window, separate from the parent's shareholding structure (Jubilant Industries itself had promoters at "75%" with low institutional float, so no forced-selling opportunity existed there at all, same citation).
- "That's why I said that special situations are special for 2 weeks" (MS1907B 00:05:18) -- restated as "special situation is a special situation for only 10 to 15 days" (MS1907B 00:03:54) and "Generally, within 10-15 days, the announcement will discount" (MS1907B 00:04:31): the announcement-driven price move (pre-listing) and the forced-selling-driven move (post-listing) each resolve inside roughly two weeks; after either window closes, "you will expect something reversal in two weeks because the sellers have sold everything, so there is no seller left" (MS1907B 00:21:18) is the confirming signal, and once that reversal either happens or fails to happen the position is purely a fundamental holding from then on.
- Confirmation before/after entry leans on volume and technical structure (breakout, "runaway gap") alongside corroborating signals such as bulk-deal buying by known special-situation investors (Neel Bahel's AIF, MS1907B 00:34:17) and promoter/insider buying -- "if an insider is riding, then you have odds of success improves" (MS1907B 00:31:36) -- but these are described as odds-improvers, not standalone triggers.
- If price does not re-rate within the expected window, Dhruv's own discipline is to "reassess your thesis" (MS1907B 00:26:42) -- explicitly distinguishing whether the position was ever a genuine fundamental view or only a technical/forced-selling trade -- and to always build one's own SOTP valuation rather than relying on brokerage models, because "always do your own valuation, you are not dependent on brokerage at all" (MS1907B 00:46:59), since "when D-Majai is only a catalyst, the Fundamentals need to follow through" (MS1907B 00:47:58) -- i.e., the demerger re-rating and the long-term stock outcome are governed by two different mechanisms.

- [JUDGE] Sector/cyclical exposure of the demerged entity (oil & gas, power, steel, aluminium) -- explicitly a personal-allocation call, not a rule: "I generally avoid cyclicals" (MS1907B 00:51:45), and Vedanta's Oil & Gas spin-off is discussed as a pure cyclical fundamental bet with "I won't give you any recommendation per se" (MS1907B 00:51:53-00:51:57).

## 4. WHAT THE LADDER MISSES

**Special situation / demerger investing as a whole category has zero representation in the rulebook.** The ladder has no data source that identifies a company as being IN a demerger, merger, scheme of arrangement, or any other corporate action at all -- so every mechanism this lecture teaches sits entirely outside what the ladder can currently see, screen for, or reason about. That is the honest headline finding, not a list of missed thresholds on existing rules.

(a) Rules this lecture conditions/qualifies:
- `market_cap_floor-001` (observation, `market_cap >= 1000`, default liquidity screen, "generally a lot of liquid names come"). This lecture states a mechanism that runs in the OPPOSITE direction on the same metric: a market cap "below 2-3,000 crores" is specifically where mandate-driven institutional forced-selling creates the best special-situation entry window (MS1907B 00:13:51) -- i.e., for this strategy, being small and under-institutionally-owned is a source of opportunity, not (as the observation currently frames market cap) simply a liquidity floor to clear before a name is worth researching. The rulebook has no notion of "market cap low enough that institutions are structurally forced sellers" as its own signal.
- No conditioning found for `turnaround_context-001`, `cash_conversion-001`, `peg_ratio-001`, or the CANSLIM/technical gates -- this lecture does not touch quarterly growth screens, cash conversion, or PEG at all.

(b) Central points with no rule for them at all:
- The demerger/corporate-action calendar itself (announcement date, record date, NCLT approval, listing date) as a screenable event -- entirely absent.
- The mandate-driven institutional forced-selling mechanic post-listing as a distinct, mechanical (non-fundamental) source of temporary mispricing -- entirely absent; this is arguably the single most repeated, most concrete idea in the whole lecture (Jubilant Ingrevia, Arti Surfactants, RTI/Arti Industries, Kesoram Industries, Quess Corp's "founded" spin-off are all built around it).
- The "special situation decays into a plain fundamental story after ~2 weeks" timing rule -- a regime-classification concept (is this still a special-situation trade, or now just a stock) that the ladder has no analogue for at all.
- Bulk/block-deal disclosures and insider/promoter buying as corroborating evidence during a live special situation -- absent, consistent with this reassessment's already-known promoter-holding gap.
- Independent SOTP valuation of demerger legs as the stated valuation method -- absent; this is separate from (and does not conflict with) the rulebook's existing, deliberate decision to leave G6 (valuation) empty, since the header there already excludes F19's SOTP as a "dated one-company worked example." This lecture reinforces that SOTP is central to the strategy but is explicitly non-formulaic and judgment-heavy (see Section 3, [JUDGE]/[SOFT] SOTP items) -- consistent with staying unencoded, not evidence that it should become a hard rule.

(c) A dated, one-company worked example being treated as universal: none found being imported into the rulebook. The lecture itself contains several dated, one-company anecdotes (Jubilant Industries' 4-5x EBITDA entry, Kesoram's 18% merger-arbitrage spread, Rosaltec's 10-20x-to-100x re-rating) that Dhruv presents explicitly as illustrations of a mechanism, not as thresholds to reuse -- and none of these numbers appear anywhere in the current rulebook. Nothing to flag here; this is a genuine "nothing here."
