## 1. CRUX

This is a screening / pattern-recognition lecture (a live "SOIC Market Signals" webinar) teaching the audience one mental model — how to spot a company entering a "J-curve" (a multi-year step-change from flat/losing to hyper-growth profits) *before* it is obvious in the numbers — and then handing them a literal StockScans screen (quarterly PAT growth > 30%) to operationalize it (MS0803A NO-TS).

## 2. MECHANISM

- Three growth tiers, stated explicitly as a framework: normal/steady compounding growth, described as "the low growth that is in your FMCG companies" (MS0803A NO-TS) running steady 10-15% growth; fast growth, where "you have to look at companies" (MS0803A NO-TS) growing 20-25% as well; and hyper growth, which is where a J-curve lives — "Hyper growth is this growth of 30 to 40" (MS0803A NO-TS) percent plus, sometimes reaching much higher: "The growth rates of these companies are 50" (MS0803A NO-TS) to 80 percent or more, on the PAT or sales level.
- A J-curve is caused by a discrete trigger, not organic drift: "usually it is something that is new" (MS0803A NO-TS), whether a new segment or new product, or "corporate restructuring or new demand tailwinds" (MS0803A NO-TS) that no one forecasted.
- Before the inflection, there is often a visible investment/pain phase: "investment phase where profit falls as cash is burned" (MS0803A NO-TS) on raw material, machinery, hiring, and "marketing and distribution" (MS0803A NO-TS); margins can compress first, as with CCL Products, where "sometimes near term margins compress" (MS0803A NO-TS) and then, "when utilization is increased" (MS0803A NO-TS), profit rides the J-curve up.
- The instructor treats capacity expansion / capex announcements as a *leading* signal to pre-position on, not a neutral fact: on Gravita's capacity build-out he asks whether "a potential that this company might go through a J curve" (MS0803A NO-TS) exists because "existing capacity shows 15-20" (MS0803A NO-TS) percent growth already — capex is explicitly framed as bullish foresight, not hindsight: "Jacob is not talking about hindsight" (MS0803A NO-TS), "he is talking about foresight" (MS0803A NO-TS).
- The actual operational screen taught: "dedicatedly look for companies quarterly results" (MS0803A NO-TS) with more than 30% PAT growth, where the companies also "which have minimum 15" (MS0803A NO-TS) percent plus revenue growth — restated later as the literal StockScans filter built live: "companies whose pad broke 30" (MS0803A NO-TS) [percent]. ["pad"/"pat" is the transcript's recurring ASR rendering of "PAT."]
- Once a numeric hit is found, the instructor says the job is not done — you then have to read the conference call to find the *cause*, asking "what is the new segment" (MS0803A NO-TS), "what is the market share company is gaining" (MS0803A NO-TS), or whether "capacity utilization is increasing" (MS0803A NO-TS). A confirming technical/price overlay is also part of the recipe: the companies are "showing higher relative strength" (MS0803A NO-TS), and "when these companies are inflecting" (MS0803A NO-TS) they "keep going into those stage 2 trends" (MS0803A NO-TS) where "base formation starts happening" (MS0803A NO-TS).
- The instructor is explicit that hyper-growth is inherently short-lived and this is a trading-style, not buy-and-hold, thesis, lasting 1-3 years because "have this type of growth non-stop is tough" (MS0803A NO-TS); as he puts it, this fast growth does not "last for infinite number of years" (MS0803A NO-TS) [i.e. does not last forever], and along the way "accidents also happen" (MS0803A NO-TS).
