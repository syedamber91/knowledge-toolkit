## 1. CRUX

Stop pricing companies off trailing P/E and start hunting for the specific, nameable "new" event -- a new capacity, product, geography, regulation, management, acquisition, or capital-structure action -- that is about to change a company's rate-of-change of earnings, because that rate-of-change (not the earnings level itself) is what the market re-rates; then use StockScans' pre-built fundamental/technical/announcement scans to industrialize the hunt for those events instead of reading annual reports cover to cover.

## 2. MECHANISM

- Returns come from three multiplying factors, not one: "perception of a stock or a theme earnings with and also the rate of change of perception about earnings growth" (MS1207A 00:09:33) -- i.e. sentiment x earnings growth x the rate of change of perception about that growth. A cheap-looking stock with no rate-of-change catalyst is not what this framework is built to find.
- A "growth catalyst" is explicitly defined with a duration test: "the growth catalyst is any company that has been doing abnormal growth for two years beyond last" (MS1207A 00:05:34) -- i.e. sustained (2-year-plus) abnormal growth, not a single good quarter.
- The instructor's single organizing heuristic is the word "new": "First framework is new based creation, new industry cycle, new management change, New corporate action, new capex, new age of business, like new way of doing business, new market creation, new regulations, new value added products, new business verticals, new acquisitions, new warrant issues and much more." (MS1207A 00:15:55). Every worked example in the lecture is slotted into one of these buckets.
- Trailing valuation is framed as actively hostile to finding these setups: "J curve mindset is a mindset which is attached to what will be the futures growth will be." (MS1207A 00:23:05), and "trailing 12 month P ratio kills the J curve mindset while it is like taking birth." (MS1207A 00:23:10).
- The practical workflow the instructor states for himself: read con-calls/PDFs for the "new" trigger -> filter with StockScans' scans (fundamental growth scans, technical/relative-strength scans, or the combined "scan match") -> "then I will sit there and work, then I will make a bull bear based scenario, then I will see the technical, I will see that my entry criteria is meeting" (MS1207A 01:07:31-01:07:35) -> size the position with a fixed conviction-based allocation grid.
- Corporate-action signals (warrants, promoter pref issues, M&A, management change) are treated as a distinct, backtested category of "new," not just an anecdote: a promoter putting in capital via warrants is read through incentives -- "you have a warrant, a promoter pays 10% of the capital upfront, logs the share price of today and 18 months down the line, he can convert it" [ASR garble, likely "locks"] (MS1207A 00:50:21), followed by "There are only two scenarios in which a promoter will" (MS1207A 00:50:29): balance-sheet repair, or locking in today's price ahead of a J-curve.

## 4. WHAT THE LADDER MISSES

(a) Conditions/qualifies an existing rule:
- None of this lecture's numeric thresholds are framed as universal screening bars the way canslim_sales-001/canslim_pat-001 are -- they are StockScans UI-tool defaults ("pre-made scans"), explicitly offered as swappable templates the user can re-configure ("let's make another scan." This is a genuine qualification worth recording against canslim_sales-001/canslim_pat-001: the instructor's own StockScans "explosive growth"/"buoyant growth" presets use a flat 20%/20% top-and-bottom-line bar (MS1207A 01:08:44-01:08:51), not the ladder's split 15%/20% (sales/PAT) bar from MASTEC 00:09:35. Two different SOIC-sourced numbers for a similar-sounding screen -- worth a note next to canslim_sales-001 that a StockScans-shipped default uses 20% sales growth, not 15%.

(b) A central point with NO rule at all:
- The entire "new" taxonomy (new base creation, new industry cycle, new management, new corporate action, new capex, new regulation, new value-added products, new business verticals, new acquisitions, new warrant issues, new balance-sheet deleveraging -- MS1207A 00:15:55) is the actual subject of this lecture and has zero representation in the 7-rule/21-observation rulebook. Every one of these is qualitative/event-driven and not resolvable from screener.in ratios; the ladder is structurally a ratio-and-technical screener with no slot for detecting that something new just happened. This is not a small gap -- it is the entire thesis of the lecture, judged by the instructor to matter more than conventional fundamentals: "Fundamental analysis is not about being an accountant." (MS1207A 00:15:00), it is "about business analysis and how this business will shape out to be in the future." (MS1207A 00:14:52).
- The promoter-warrant/preferential-issue backtest (686 companies, >₹500cr, 6-year window, stated hit rates and returns at 2/3/5-year horizons -- MS1207A 00:48:36-00:49:48) is a specific, numerically-grounded corporate-action signal the ladder has no observation or rule for at all. This is a different, independently-sourced number from the market_cap_floor-001 observation's ₹1,000cr liquidity default and should not be conflated with it -- it defines the backtest's universe, not a stock-picking floor.
- The "scan match" concept (intersection of a fundamental growth scan and a technical relative-strength scan) is a structurally distinct idea -- combine two independently-passing screens rather than gate on either alone -- that nothing in the rulebook's G0/G1/G3/G8 gate structure expresses (the gates are ANDed sequentially, not intersected as independent scan populations).
- Annual-report red-flag scanning (qualified/disqualified/disclaimer audit opinions) is presented as a forensic screen StockScans automates; the rulebook's G2 (forensic veto) is explicitly empty per CONTEXT.md's own header, and this lecture shows exactly the kind of signal that could eventually occupy it -- worth flagging as a candidate direction for G2, not a ready-made rule (no threshold or resolvable-attribute mapping is given here).
