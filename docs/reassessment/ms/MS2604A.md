## 1. CRUX

This lecture is not a stock-screening lecture at all -- it is a market-access
and tax-structure lecture whose job is to convince a domestic Indian investor
to allocate a slice of their portfolio abroad and then walk them through
*how* to do it (which legal/tax wrapper -- direct broker, mutual fund/FoF,
or GIFT City PMS/AIF -- and what each costs in tax), using global mega-caps
purely as motivating illustrations, never as an NSE-applicable screening
method.

## 2. MECHANISM

- Diversification pitch: global investing gives access to entire industries
  that "there isn't a single" comparable company for in India -- GPUs,
  aerospace engines, semiconductor chip testing, robotics manufacturers,
  drone sensors -- so it is framed as incremental exposure, not a
  replacement for domestic investing: "There isn't a single GPU company in
  India like dominances with Nvidia, for example." (MS2604A 00:12:33)
- Comparative-valuation motivation: mega-cap global businesses are shown
  trading at growth-adjusted multiples that look cheap next to a comparable
  Indian name, to argue globally-dominant moats can be bought more cheaply
  per unit of growth than some domestic alternatives -- Google's "It trades
  on 30 times" P/E against "Around 30.4% H, the company grew its EPS" is set
  against "TVS motors the answer is 57 times almost" (MS2604A 00:20:05).
- Currency/home-bias argument: INR depreciation against USD/GBP/EUR is
  framed as both a headwind that erodes FII returns from India and a source
  of extra rupee-terms gain for an Indian investor holding US assets (walked
  through numerically later in the Apple worked example); the instructor
  states a comfort allocation, not a rule: "if 10 to 15 percent be at the
  asset allocation globally At least there will be a bull market somewhere
  or the other which keeps happening and that gives you an advantage"
  (MS2604A 00:13:20), echoed later as "I'm not saying put 100% global"
  (MS2604A 00:18:35).
- Three access routes, each with a different regulatory ceiling: direct
  overseas broker account (no ceiling, highest minimum ticket flexibility);
  retail mutual funds/FoF into index or active global funds, throttled by a
  fund-level RBI overseas-investment cap -- "there is a full fund cap limit
  of $7 billion ... and then $1 billion for AMCs" (MS2604A 00:30:31) --
  which forces AMCs to periodically close fresh subscriptions; and GIFT
  City/IFSC-regulated PMS or AIF vehicles, which sit outside that cap and
  offer a lower minimum ticket to an "accredited investor" (either an income
  test, "the root A is that my spouse's income should be greater than 1.7
  crores" (MS2604A 00:39:24), or a net-worth test, "your total wealth should
  be around 10 lakh dollars that convert into around 8.5 CR" (MS2604A
  00:39:43)).
- Taxation is wrapper-dependent, not company-dependent: PMS and direct
  investing are taxed at the investor level on realization -- short-term
  (<24 months) "at the investor's lab[slab]" (MS2604A 00:42:28) or long-term
  (>=24 months) "it will be 12.5% Along with CES" (MS2604A 00:43:14) --
  while retail FoF/AIF units are taxed at the fund level on redemption.
  Direct investing alone carries extra US-specific frictions the fund
  wrappers avoid: a withholding tax on dividends offset via the India-US
  treaty, and a US inheritance-tax exposure on US-situs assets above a
  stated threshold: "When you invest in the US More than $60, 000 If you
  have a asset ... You have to give taxation to the US government." (MS2604A
  00:44:40).
