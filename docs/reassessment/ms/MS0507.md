## 1. CRUX

This lecture is not a stock pitch; it is a demonstration of the *reading habit* itself -- track weekly corporate-announcement/order-flow and sector-rotation signals to notice a theme before it shows up in a financial ratio, then use global (mostly US-listed) comps as a preview of where a still-nascent Indian sub-sector -- here, the "attention deficit economy" of leisure, live entertainment and sport -- is headed, with Saregama and TIPS offered as India's early, small-scale domestic analogues.

## 2. MECHANISM

- Read corporate announcements and order wins every Saturday because a fresh order or a changed tone in an annual report moves before the financial statement does: "You can just come towards Announcements Scans and you can just read the announcements" (MS0507 00:27:18).
- Use top-down sector technicals (weekly moving averages, % of stocks above the 200-DMA, a "score change" metric) to flag when a hot sector is overheated and due to rotate, independent of whether its fundamentals are actually broken: "now is a point of time where you have to watch out for sectoral rotation" (MS0507 00:50:26), and concretely, "the manufacturing sector which was the outperforming sector has started going through consolidation" (MS0507 00:52:07).
- Treat the rate of change of a narrative driver -- not its absolute level -- as the real timing signal: the AI/data-center capex boom's "narrative" is only expected to turn once 2-3 hyperscalers visibly cut capex, at which point "the rate of change will be negative on the narrative" (MS0507 00:39:46).
- A low headline P/E is not itself a buy signal -- it can mark a business the market has already priced for terminal decline rather than a bargain: "growth drives valuation and not valuation which drives growth" (MS0507 00:55:28), illustrated with TCS trading at "14.5 times" (MS0507 00:54:55), a multiple "seen in 2009" (MS0507 00:54:59), because the market treats it as a "newspaper stock" "at a terminal value" (MS0507 00:55:12, 00:55:15).
- Global (largely US-listed) comps in an emerging theme are read as a preview of a domestic sub-sector 5-10 years out, on the premise that India's consumption pattern lags the US: Live Nation's 2026 results are read out at length ("2026 is off to a powerful start with first quarter revenue climbing 12% to 3.8 billion dollars", MS0507 01:06:18) before pivoting to the domestic analogues, "both Saregama and TIPS fall into this category" (MS0507 01:20:16).
- Business/capital cycles (new entrants chase high returns, capacity floods in, prices collapse, players exit, supply tightens, prices recover) are used to keep a "hot" cyclical -- here semiconductor memory -- in context rather than extrapolating one quarter's move: "Capital cycles is something that you can never forget" (MS0507 01:00:15), following the observation that "Each and every cycle of shortage is followed by a glut of oversupply" (MS0507 00:59:50).
