## 1. CRUX

This lecture is not a valuation or buy/sell framework session -- it is a live,
practitioner walkthrough of the roughly dozen independent, mostly mechanical
SCREENS (thematic/regulatory triggers, corporate-announcement scans, most-
searched/volume scans, sector-rotation "Market Scans," mutual-fund portfolio
tracking, technical base/V-Stop scans, and a margin/capex fundamental scan)
the instructor personally runs every week to turn "where do I even look" into
a shortlist of names worth independent research -- teaching the sourcing
funnel, not the final decision (MS1503B verbatim, no timestamp in source).

## 2. MECHANISM

- The funnel always runs broad-to-narrow: a structural/thematic trigger (a new
  regulation, an acquisition, a capacity announcement) is used to build a tiny
  peer set, which is then narrowed by a quantitative filter (PAT/revenue growth
  rate), narrowed again by a quality check (cash conversion), and only then
  handed to an AI-assisted peer-to-peer research prompt -- "let me show you one
  more idea... let's see the growth rates of these three companies... These 3
  companies are above 20% Pad Roth" then "let me just put this... you are a
  world class equity analyst who specializes in doing P2P analysis of
  companies" (MS1503B verbatim, no timestamp in source).
- Growth alone is explicitly NOT sufficient to survive the shortlist: Gen
  Recycling was growing fast but was dropped from the recycling peer set
  purely on a cash-quality defect -- "one problem which I encountered was of
  cashlose. The company is not able to convert its CFO to like CFO to PAT...
  So this is why this one got like basically deleted versus the others"
  (MS1503B verbatim, no timestamp in source).
- Several of the screens are explicitly designed to catch a different failure
  mode of the others and are run in parallel on a routine (often weekly)
  cadence rather than any single one being "the" screen -- corporate
  announcements catch M&A/capex before quarterly results show it, "most
  searched" catches crowd attention shifts, Market Scans catches sector-level
  rotation, mutual-fund tracking catches "smart money" conviction, and
  technical base/V-Stop scans catch stocks about to re-rate: "new capacity and
  ensure announcements, new like corporate announcements and technical scans.
  Also some of the ways that one can look at basically looking at different
  businesses and stocks" (MS1503B verbatim, no timestamp in source).
- Institutional conviction is read through POSITION SIZE, not mere presence: a
  single fund holding roughly 8-10%+ of a company is treated as a de facto
  private-equity-style, multi-year bet worth investigating on its own --
  "remember this mutual fund which gets into like which buys 8 to 10
  percentage of a company It's very rare to find this but if they do so that's
  interesting" (MS1503B verbatim, no timestamp in source).
- The instructor explicitly privileges what fund managers DO over what
  commentators SAY: "the real Truth is in what you are doing about those
  companies right?" (MS1503B verbatim, no timestamp in source) -- used to
  justify tracking specific fund portfolios (Trust MF, Invesco, PPFS/BPFS,
  DSP, SBI) rather than opinions.
- Liquidity is checked as an actual rupee traded-value figure, not company
  size: "if a company doesn't have trading of Rs. 15 crore in a day, then
  there is a free float [problem] in that company" (MS1503B verbatim, no
  timestamp in source) -- demonstrated by contrasting Varun Beverages
  (crores traded per day) against Arman Finance (barely tens of thousands of
  shares on 13th March).
