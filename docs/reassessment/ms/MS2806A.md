## 1. CRUX

This lecture is not a new stock-selection framework -- it is a demo of HOW to use AI (Claude, Perplexity, Google notebook/Gemini) to produce the same TVGP (Theme-Valuation-Growth-Promoter) research the instructor already teaches, faster and at scale, while warning members that the analytical framework must live in their own head first or they cannot catch the AI's mistakes: "Learning is speciality, AI is commodity beyond the point" (MS2806A 00:58:59) and "Framework is a must, you cannot automate learning" (MS2806A 00:58:59).

## 2. MECHANISM

- TVGP stays the organizing spine: Theme (sector/macro tailwind and rotation), Valuation (a sentiment gauge, not a precise number), Growth (why the company is growing, mapped via a "new" framework), Promoter (forensics + qualitative read of intent).
- Theme is framed as dominant and cyclical: "60% of a stock's move is attributable to the theme, 20% is attributable to the liquidity and last 20% is attributable to your stock picking" (MS2806A 00:03:05), illustrated with named leading sectors per multi-year window, e.g. "But generally, if you check 1991 to 1996, right?" (MS2806A 00:03:54) through "95 to 2000, they will see all IT companies, these are the best performing stocks in every 5 years." (MS2806A 00:04:04), citing William O'Neill: "the leading sector in one bull market usually does not lead the next" (MS2806A 00:02:40).
- Valuation is explicitly anti-DCF: "How many of you agree with me that DCF sheets rarely work in real life?" (MS2806A 00:07:17) and "Reverse DCF is far better" (MS2806A 00:07:22); valuation is treated as a read of market perception/froth, not an intrinsic-value calculation.
- Growth is diagnosed through a "new" catalyst checklist -- new margin base, new products, new management, new capacity, new institutional owners, new supply-demand setup: "New products, new management, new highs." (MS2806A 00:11:26), "The catalyst is a new product, new leadership, new capacity, new adjustable market" (MS2806A 00:11:55), "a new supply demand set up, a new scarcity in the flow bybacks, low shrinking share count, tightening supply against rising demand" [likely "float via buybacks"] (MS2806A 00:11:59), "Then there are new institutional owners." (MS2806A 00:12:34).
- Promoter/forensics is a short named checklist: CFO/PAT and a second cash ratio, past promoter actions toward minorities, related-party transactions, and contingent liabilities -- see Section 3 for exact quotes.
- The AI workflow itself: ask the LLM for a report-generating prompt rather than write one from scratch; for an INDUSTRY report demand TAM (global vs domestic, base year + 5/10-year projection), growth rate and its drivers, end customers, a granular value chain (raw material to finished good, both India and global), and a competitive/specialization matrix ending in SWOT; for a COMPANY report demand business model, latest con-call summary, a 5-year "walk the talk" guidance-vs-delivery check, industry tailwinds and how the company sits on them, a growth-trigger map, risk analysis, peer-to-peer comparison, and bull/bear/base napkin-math valuation with stated assumptions.
- Sourcing discipline against hallucination: require citations, restrict the model to uploaded/approved sources (annual reports, con calls, investor PPTs, DRHPs, screener/stock-scans pages) and explicitly flag assumptions when data is unavailable, triangulating from global benchmarks. A reusable Claude "skill" is built to encode exactly which sites/filings to pull from and in what format.
- Iteration is expected and fallible: the instructor repeatedly has to re-prompt the AI to fix wrong or unreadable outputs (e.g. flowcharts), underscoring that the human must audit the AI's work rather than trust it outright.
