## 1. CRUX

This is a weekly market-commentary broadcast whose actual persuasion job is
narrower than its title suggests: get the SYC audience to stop pattern-matching
the whole Indian CDMO/pharma-outsourcing space as one trade and instead track
each company on its OWN CDMO-mix-as-percent-of-sales trajectory and molecule
pipeline — because the same sector label (CDMO) currently contains names at
wildly different stages (already re-rated and expensive vs. "yet to inflect")
and the re-rating itself is a slow, multi-year structural shift, not a
quarterly catalyst (MS0103 NO-TS).

## 2. MECHANISM

- The instructor's core CDMO investing thesis is a repeatable staged pattern,
  stated as his own mental model: a company's base low-margin API/CMO business
  "takes three to five years for that to develop," and only once CDMO becomes
  "a high profit margin business" that "becomes substantial part of the
  profit pool," does "the re-rating happen" — he names Neuland
  ("Newland"), and "to some extent" Laurus ("Lauras"), as the pattern he's
  already watched play out (MS0103 NO-TS).
- He explicitly rejects the "China plus one" framing the market uses to
  justify the whole sector: "let's delete the dictionary China plus one," his
  stated reason being that China's incumbents are moving up the value chain
  and becoming innovators themselves rather than staying pure contract
  manufacturers — evidenced by one specific competitor's book of business
  shrinking ("Vushi has almost 5 times sales" of the whole Indian industry
  combined, but "the pipeline of Vushi, it is de-growing for last 3 years")
  (MS0103 NO-TS).
- His stated holding-period discipline for this sector is explicit and
  precedes any stock discussion: "you have to take a horizon of 2 to 5 years"
  because supply is lumpy by nature — "one quarter of supply then four
  quarters of no supply," and "sometimes it will take 3-4 years to get
  launched" (MS0103 NO-TS).
- Per company, he walks a repeatable checklist: what % of revenue is
  currently CDMO vs. legacy API/CMO/other, is that % rising, what molecule(s)
  are driving it, is there a *named*, dated capacity/capex trigger
  (a numbered plant, a numbered kiloliter capacity, an FY-dated revenue
  target), and — separately, as a valuation overlay he treats as a distinct
  question from the business quality call — what multiple is already priced in.
- He treats "lumpiness" (a bad multi-quarter earnings patch) not as a red flag
  but as the entry opportunity the whole thesis depends on: for Neuland
  specifically he says the current problem is the opposite — it is priced for
  perfection ("margin of safety is missing") — and states the trade is to
  "wish and pray" for the next lumpy patch so the market temporarily
  disbelieves the story again (MS0103 NO-TS).
- Valuation discipline is explicitly PEG-based, not a flat P/E cutoff: for
  Anthem Biosciences he does the arithmetic live — "what's the PEG ratio
  folks? If you do 81 above 20" — to argue the stock is expensive relative to
  its own growth rate, and states a personal ceiling ("I won't pay two times
  peg ratio") rather than a sector-wide hard number (MS0103 NO-TS).
