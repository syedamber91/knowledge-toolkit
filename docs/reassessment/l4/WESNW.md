LECTURE: When & How to Enter a Stock    REF: WESNW    (transcript: 178263 chars, 2348 lines, covered: yes)

## 1. CRUX

The lecture teaches a repeatable framework for deciding WHEN to enter, re-enter, or
pyramid into a stock you already like fundamentally -- not what to buy -- built by
inverting the problem first (define what NOT to buy) and then mapping a fundamental
earnings-expectations cycle onto a technical stage-analysis/moving-average price
structure, so a retail investor stops being led by price-anchoring, chasing highs, or
buying into downtrends and peak margins.

## 2. MECHANISM

- Invert first: before deciding when to buy, define when NOT to buy -- "avoid the
  downtrend or write it down avoid downtrends avoid stocks which are down trending"
  (WESNW 01:02:15), and fundamentally avoid stocks with "a clear downgrade of the
  earnings cycle" (WESNW 00:52:11) where analyst EPS estimates are being revised
  downward.
- Classify your own investor type first (deep value, GARP, quality, momentum, special
  situation) because the entry framework differs by type: "Deep value investor focus
  on entry valuations" and doesn't require growth confirmation (WESNW 01:22:07),
  whereas a GARP investor "He'll buy a reasonable P and he'll focus on EPS growth"
  (WESNW 01:22:31) -- applying one entry rule to every investor style is,
  in the instructor's own framing, a category error.
- Fundamentally, returns are decided by "there are only two things which decide your
  returns" (WESNW 01:20:54) -- entry PE/EPS and the EPS growth trajectory during the
  holding period -- and stocks re-rate or de-rate on analyst estimate revisions,
  because "stocks work in anticipation of earnings" (WESNW 01:44:24), not on
  earnings already reported.
- Technically, the same cycle is read off price structure and a moving average:
  price forming lower highs/lower lows = downtrend to avoid; a sideways base with no
  fresh lows = base formation; price forming higher highs/higher lows with the
  30-weekly EMA sloping up = early stage 2, the buy zone.
- Volume is used throughout as a confirming filter on any of the above signals:
  "if the price is going high and the volumes are going high, it means strong
  uptrend and smart money is getting into it" (WESNW 02:47:45) versus rising
  price on falling volume, "a weakening uptrend lack of further demand" (WESNW
  02:47:53).
- Fundamentals and technicals are explicitly sequenced, not interchangeable: "without
  studying fundamentals, I can't understand the chart of the company" (WESNW
  03:05:05) -- technicals decide entry/exit timing, fundamentals decide allocation
  size and conviction to hold through a base or pullback.

## 3. SIGNALS

[HARD] The 30-weekly EMA turning upward (on rising volume) is stated as a primary
entry trigger: "Generally, if the 30 weekly m is start saving upwards on back of
higher volumes, It means that the trend has changed and it will go into an up move"
(WESNW 02:41:26). Also recommended by name as the default indicator: "I go 30
weekly MA" (WESNW 02:18:23). This is a WEEKLY-timeframe EMA, applied to any stock
regardless of sector, and is framed purely as an ENTRY signal (trend turning up),
not an exit condition.

[HARD] A "golden crossover" -- the 10-weekly EMA crossing above the 30-weekly EMA --
is offered as a second, more precise entry trigger, also weekly-only: "10 weekly EMA
crossing the 30 weekly EMA or 30 weekly EMA start shaping upwards on high volumes."
(WESNW 02:24:05). The instructor is explicit these two moving averages are read on
WEEKLY charts only, not daily: "We lo daily charts peh nahi karhe because daily
charts me bhat noise crossing 30 VMAs, golden crossover" (WESNW 02:21:21) [likely:
"we don't do it on daily charts because daily charts have too much noise"].

[JUDGE] Base formation (sideways consolidation with no fresh lows, ideally spanning
roughly 8-12 weeks) validates a subsequent high-volume breakout as a genuine entry
point: "any stock which goes through a sideways movement of anywhere between 8 to 12
weeks just signals a base formation happening" (WESNW 02:42:52), and "by the way the
longer the base the better it is" (WESNW 02:42:52). Tagged JUDGE rather than HARD
because identifying a base is explicitly taught as a pattern-recognition exercise
the instructor quizzes students on chart-by-chart -- "Is this a base formation?"
(WESNW 02:35:46) -- not a single closed-form computation.

[JUDGE] A "multi-year breakout" -- price taking out a resistance that has held for
several years -- is framed as a stronger, longer-conviction entry signal: "Generally,
3-4 years is considered to be a multi-year breakout" (WESNW 02:52:17).

[SOFT] Avoid entering at "peak margins" even when valuation looks reasonable, because
margins mean-revert: "if you get into a stock with peak margins, maybe the margins
have to persist for 2-3 years for you to make money" (WESNW 01:03:13), and "peak
margins and peak order book both come together" (WESNW 01:03:27) -- illustrated with
GMM Pfaudler's ("GMM Fodler") capex-order-book cycle. This needs long-term margin
history read in context (is this a cyclical peak?) and order-book/concall
commentary the ladder does not fetch.

[SOFT] Volume confirmation on breakouts/base-completion ("smart money" buying vs.
weak demand) -- the ladder's current rulebook has no volume metric at all.

[JUDGE] Price-anchoring bias correction: "price anchor is the worst type of anchor
that you can develop price anchor this is a completely wrong framework" (WESNW
00:12:24) -- psychological discipline, not a screenable rule, but a repeated,
central teaching point (framed as the very first thing addressed in the webinar).

[JUDGE] Investor-style self-classification (deep value / GARP / quality / momentum /
special situation) as a precondition for which entry rules even apply -- a pure
judgement call about the user's own strategy, not a per-company fact.

[SOFT] The SOIC LTI screening tool's live reading on a specific stock (Good Luck
India, in the demo) shows "So I think ADX is 20" (WESNW 03:00:39) and "We actually
like to RSI that hasn't gone above 55, but the weekly we stop has just gone
positive" (WESNW 03:00:47) -- this is a live narration of a proprietary,
undocumented composite indicator ("SOIC LTI" / "V-STOP"), not a stated universal

## 4. WHAT THE LADDER MISSES

(a) Rules the ladder encoded that this lecture conditions:

- `entry_rsi-001` / `entry_adx-001` (weekly RSI >= 50, weekly ADX >= 20, sourced from
  HOWB 00:01:55-00:02:23) are NOT independently restated or corroborated by WESNW.
  The only RSI/ADX language in this lecture is the live tool-reading quoted above
  (WESNW 03:00:39-03:00:47), which names a DIFFERENT RSI figure ("hasn't gone above
  55") attached to one specific stock's screening-tool output, not a stated general
  entry bar. This is not a contradiction of the ladder's rule, but it means the
  ladder's G8 gate has only single-source support (HOWB) -- WESNW does not add a
  second, independent citation for the specific 50/20 thresholds, and it shows those
  numbers are actually read off a proprietary composite tool rather than computed
  from raw RSI/ADX by the instructor by hand.

(b) Central points the ladder has NO rule for at all -- and this is the largest gap:
the ladder's entire technical layer is the single G8 gate (weekly RSI/ADX). This
lecture's actual technical entry framework is built almost entirely on moving-average
structure and price-pattern recognition that G8 does not capture:
  - The 30-weekly EMA turning upward as the primary entry trigger (WESNW
    02:18:10-02:18:23, 02:41:26-02:41:33) -- no rule in the 16-entry rulebook checks
    the direction of any moving average.
  - The 10-week/30-week EMA "golden crossover" as a second entry trigger (WESNW
    02:24:05-02:24:12) -- no crossover rule exists.
  - Downtrend avoidance via price structure (lower highs/lower lows, price below a
    falling 30-weekly EMA) as an entry FILTER, independent of and prior to any
    RSI/ADX check (WESNW 01:02:15, 01:13:17) -- the ladder has no rule tied to price
    structure/trend at all, only the RSI/ADX oscillator gate.
  - Base-formation length (roughly 8-12 weeks) and breakout-on-volume as a
    validating signal (WESNW 02:42:52) -- no base-detection or breakout-volume rule
    exists, and the ladder has no volume metric at all.
  - Multi-year breakout (3-4+ year resistance break) as a stronger entry signal
    (WESNW 02:52:17) -- absent.
  - Investor-style classification (deep value vs. GARP vs. momentum vs. quality vs.
    special situation) as a precondition that changes which entry criteria are even
    relevant -- the ladder applies one uniform rule set to all 38 candidates
    regardless of style, which this lecture explicitly treats as a category error
    (WESNW 01:22:07-01:22:37).
  - Peak-margin/peak-order-book avoidance at entry (WESNW 01:03:13-01:03:27) is a
    distinct check from the ladder's existing `growth_trap_flag-001` observation
    (which is PE-multiple-focused); this lecture's version is margin-LEVEL-focused
    and independent of PE.

(c) No dated one-company threshold is being smuggled in as a universal bar here: the
"8 to 12 weeks" base-length heuristic and the "3-4 years" multi-year-breakout
heuristic are both explicitly framed by the instructor as general rules of thumb
("any stock which goes through...", "Generally, 3-4 years is considered...") and
illustrated across multiple different tickers (MCX, Prestige, Glenmark, IndraPrastha
Medical, Aditya Birla Sunlife AMC as homework), not derived from and pinned to a
single worked example.
