LECTURE: How to use Trading View! (Part1)    REF: TVPDT    (transcript: 126505 chars, 1337 lines, covered: yes)

## 1. CRUX

This lecture is a platform-and-indicator setup tutorial, not a screening framework: it teaches a fundamentals-first investor how to configure TradingView's charts, volume indicators, RSI, Volatility Stop and ADX/Parabolic SAR so that entry and exit TIMING for stocks already chosen on fundamentals can be made mechanically and unemotionally instead of by gut feel, with the instructor stating outright: "We will not buy anything like a technical analyst" (TVPDT 01:03:50-01:03:53) and "buying basis of fundamentals selling basis of mixture of fundamentals and volatility stop" (TVPDT 01:35:15).

## 2. MECHANISM

- Stage analysis (from Stan Weinstein's "Profiting in Bull and Bear Markets") splits a stock's life into four repeating states: stage 1 is "basically known as a basing area" (TVPDT 00:03:37) i.e. consolidation; stage 2 is "also known as a markup phase" (TVPDT 00:05:01) confirmed by high-volume breakout; stage 3 is topping/distribution; stage 4 is decline. He uses this as the organizing lens for every other indicator that follows.
- Always chart on log scale and auto y-axis — anything else "will always lead to the bad representation of a chart and whatever technical analysis you are doing, all of it will go down the drain" (TVPDT 00:25:01), and he separately calls skipping log scale "This is your unpardonable offense." (TVPDT 00:20:29). Weekly/monthly timeframes are the norm for long-term investors, not daily.
- Volume is read through three indicators: volume moving average, On-Balance Volume (OBV, a cumulative running total that rises on up-days and falls on down-days), and (paid-tier) Visible Range Volume Profile to locate high-volume price levels as support/resistance. He states the underlying logic as "volume is the weight of the evidence and price gives you an evidence" (TVPDT 00:53:30-00:53:37, exact tail: "Volume is the weight of the evidence.").
- RSI crossing 50 (not the textbook 30/70) is his entry trigger, learned from a contact: "Whenever a stock crosses RSI of 50 with very high volumes, he ends up buying the stock with a fundamental analysis." (TVPDT 00:57:41). He also treats 50 as a support level for a stock already in an uptrend, and treats a comprehensive break below 50 as a prompt to "re take a look at your fundamentals closely" (tail of TVPDT 00:59:51). Separately he flags an upper danger zone: crossing "80 to 85... I typically call that the situation has become extremely overrated [likely "overbought"] and here stock should not be purchased now." (TVPDT 00:56:14), later pinning it more loosely at "75 or 80" (TVPDT 01:02:19).
- The Volatility Stop (V-Stop) is presented as his actual selling framework: "Volatility stop is nothing but the measurement of average volatility." (TVPDT 01:29:16), and when volatility increases against the position "it will stop you out from that particular investment" (tail of TVPDT 01:28:35). Standard settings: "length 10 right 10 months right multiplier is Either you can use 2 or 2.5" (TVPDT 01:29:59) — 2.5 reserved for higher-conviction names, monthly charts for long-term compounders, weekly charts for deeply cyclical businesses (earlier, tighter exits). The stated discipline: "Because the selling decisions always have to be made on the basis of objectivity and not emotions." (TVPDT 01:30:28).
- A composite "buying setup" is described as several signals confirming together — weekly RSI above 50, weekly ADX above 20, V-Stop green, Parabolic SAR trending positive, volume above its moving average, OBV making higher highs, and positive relative strength versus a benchmark index — which he frames as a probabilistic institutional-entry signal that should send the investor back to check fundamentals, not as an automatic buy: "adx, the moment adx crosses 20, It means that the direction of the stock has started becoming very very positive." (TVPDT 02:02:43) combined with "if your ADX crosses 20 and if your volatility stop is also become green" (TVPDT 02:04:30). Parabolic SAR settings given as "increment 0.05, start 0.02 and max value 0.2" (TVPDT 02:08:15).

## 3. SIGNALS

- [HARD] Weekly RSI crossing/holding above 50, on high volume, is his buy-side entry trigger — "Whenever a stock crosses RSI of 50 with very high volumes, he ends up buying the stock" (TVPDT 00:57:41).
- [JUDGE] A weekly RSI break below 50 "very comprehensively" is not itself a sell signal but a trigger to "re take a look at your fundamentals closely" (TVPDT 00:59:51) — a qualitative re-underwrite prompt, not computable.
- [HARD] RSI above roughly 75-85 is a "don't buy now" zone he calls "extremely overrated" [likely "overbought"] (TVPDT 00:56:14; restated at 01:02:19 as "75 or 80"). Note the instructor himself hedges the exact number ("what I've seen"), so it is closer to a rule-of-thumb band than a precise bar.
- [HARD] Weekly ADX crossing above 20 signals "the direction of the stock has started becoming very very positive" (TVPDT 02:02:43).
- [SOFT] The Volatility Stop (length 10, multiplier 2 or 2.5, monthly for compounders / weekly for cyclicals) as an exit trigger — computable from price data in principle (it is an ATR-style trailing stop), but the ladder does not currently fetch/compute it, and the multiplier choice (2 vs 2.5) is explicitly conviction-based: "that is typically for stocks where you have much higher conviction" (TVPDT 01:33:49).
- [SOFT] Parabolic SAR turning positive (settings: increment 0.05, start 0.02, max 0.2) as a secondary buy confirmation (TVPDT 02:08:15) — computable from price series but not currently fetched/used.
- [SOFT] Relative strength / comparative strength versus a sector or benchmark index (e.g. Nifty FMCG, Nifty Bank) crossing zero or making higher highs, as a corroborating "outperformance has started" signal (TVPDT 01:59:53-02:00:00 area) — needs an index price series the ladder does not currently fetch.
- [JUDGE] Volume/OBV confirmation of a breakout or a decline — "volumes are also more than the average moving average volumes" (TVPDT 02:06:04) — requires reading a chart, not reducible to a single ratio.
- [JUDGE] Distinguishing a "genuine" red V-Stop/sell signal from a promoter-driven, non-fundamental sale: his own example on Gujarat Fluorochemicals ("Gujarat Flora Chemicals" in the transcript [likely "Gujarat Fluorochemicals"]) — "the promoter sold his stake to pay off the debt in the group companies" (TVPDT 01:34:36) — required reading the company's own BSE disclosure, not a chart signal alone.
- [JUDGE] Whether a stage-3 top resolves into stage-4 decline or a fresh stage-2 base (his Narayana Hrudayalaya example, "Narayana Hodyalya" in the transcript) is explicitly visual/pattern-based, not a threshold.

## 4. WHAT THE LADDER MISSES

(a) `entry_rsi-001` (G8, weekly_rsi >= 50) is corroborated as a floor but this lecture attaches two qualifications the ladder does not encode: first, a break back below 50 is framed not as a fail-state but as a trigger to "re take a look at your fundamentals closely" (TVPDT 00:59:51) — i.e. re-underwrite, not just exclude; second, and more consequentially, the lecture states an UPPER bound the current rule has no counterpart for — RSI above roughly 75-85 is "extremely overrated" [likely "overbought"] and "should not be purchased now" (TVPDT 00:56:14). The rulebook's G8 gate treats any weekly_rsi >= 50 as a pass with no ceiling. This matters concretely for the 38-name shortlist: LAURUSLABS carries weekly_rsi = 80.87 and is CANDIDATE — squarely inside the "don't buy now" zone this lecture describes, on the instructor's own admittedly approximate threshold.

(b) `entry_adx-001` (G8, weekly_adx >= 20) is directly corroborated with no new qualification — same 20 threshold, same weekly-chart framing (TVPDT 02:02:43, 02:04:30). Not a miss, a confirmation.

(c) The ladder has NO rule at all for the Volatility Stop, despite the instructor presenting it as his primary, named selling discipline ("this is a selling framework which I will be teaching you," TVPDT 01:29:02) and the thing that actually produces his objective sell decisions across every worked example in this lecture (Laurus Labs, Dixon, RHI Magnesita, APL Apollo, KEI Industries, HDFC Bank). The 38-name table's "ExitTriggers" column (values 0/1/2) is not sourced to this lecture's V-Stop logic anywhere in CONTEXT.md's 16-entry rulebook — nothing in the rulebook computes a V-Stop. This is the single largest gap: the ladder currently has no computable exit/sell signal of the kind this lecture treats as central, even though the underlying math (a volatility-based trailing stop off the price series) is in principle fetchable from data the ladder already has.

(d) The ladder also has no rule for Parabolic SAR (settings given explicitly: increment 0.05, start 0.02, max 0.2, TVPDT 02:08:15) or for relative/comparative strength against a benchmark index — both named as components of his composite "buying setup" alongside RSI and ADX, which the ladder currently gates on RSI+ADX alone with no volume/OBV/V-Stop/relative-strength corroboration requirement. This is a real qualification of how G8 should be read: the instructor never treats RSI>50 + ADX>20 as sufficient on its own — "adx, the moment adx crosses 20... adx and if your volatility stop is also become green... and your parabolic SAR it starts trending, that's the point of time to buy" (TVPDT 02:02:43-02:04:30) is presented as a multi-signal convergence, not a two-metric gate.

(e) The multiplier choice (2 vs 2.5) and the monthly-vs-weekly V-Stop timeframe choice (TVPDT 01:33:49, 01:35:15) are explicitly conviction- and cyclicality-dependent judgment calls in the instructor's own words, not fixed universal thresholds — if a future V-Stop rule is ever added to the ladder, it should not hard-code a single multiplier as a universal bar; this is a case of (c) in the brief-writing sense — a dated, business-specific setting risk, flagged pre-emptively since no rule yet exists to misapply it.

Nothing in this lecture bears on G0 (CANSLIM sales/PAT growth), G1 (leverage), G3 (ROCE), or any of the observation-only entries (PE band, cash conversion, PEG, CFO/PAT, fixed-asset turnover, capex, growth trap, growth durability) — it is purely a technical-charting tutorial and does not touch fundamentals screening at all beyond repeating that technicals should follow, not replace, a fundamental thesis.
