## 1. CRUX

This lecture is trying to replace an emotional, price-anchored exit decision (holding by hope, anchoring to buy price, averaging down a broken thesis) with an objective, pre-committed exit checklist — fundamental triggers (thesis breakdown, peak-margin/peak-sentiment, valuation, governance/cash-flow red flags), a technical system (30-week EMA + relative strength + volatility stop), and portfolio-hygiene triggers (time stop loss, position trimming) — so the investor sells on a rule rather than on a feeling.

## 2. MECHANISM

- The instructor frames selling with a wheel diagram he calls "the exit Chakar view" [likely "exit chakravyuh view," a Mahabharata reference to a trap one enters but cannot easily exit] (FSSMF 00:42:45): the outer ring is a set of named traps investors fall into, the inner rings are exit tools, and the center mantra is "portfolio should be going on, compounding should be going on, exit to preserve, redeploy to grow" (FSSMF 00:44:59), paired with practicing "emotional detachment" (FSSMF 00:44:38) and accepting "that there will be mistakes" (FSSMF 00:45:10) as the behavioural precondition for using any of the rules below.
- Fundamental exit reasons are given as a named list: "Fundamental reasons to exit." / "Thesis broke down." (FSSMF 01:02:26-01:02:27), followed by poor PNL/earnings growth and what he calls the "three lethal P's" — peak margin, peak profitability, peak sentiment — illustrated with PG Electroplast (promoter relatives selling into a 130-140 PE on a one-off AC-summer demand spike) and Trent (a 152% YoY growth quarter mathematically guaranteed to decelerate against its own high base, "the company has become triple P" (FSSMF 01:06:54)).
- Valuation is checked with a bull/base/bear reverse-DCF-style scenario builder (revenue/EBITDA growth at three probability-weighted rates, applied to an exit P/E or EV/EBITDA multiple) rather than a single static ratio — he runs this live on Trent and Dixon as demos (FSSMF 01:11:35-01:13:21).
- A parallel technical system is offered for investors who don't want to rely on fundamentals alone: sell when three signals all turn negative together — price closes below the 30-week EMA (Dow-theory trend breakdown, lower high vs. previous high), relative strength vs. Nifty 500/50 rolls over, and the volatility-stop (V-Stop, ATR length 10, multiplier 2) flips red — stated explicitly as "30 weekly MN negative, RS negative, and V-stop negative." (FSSMF 01:46:56).
- Two portfolio-hygiene triggers sit alongside the fundamental/technical ones and are explicitly not about being "wrong": time stop loss (a position that hasn't moved for a defined number of quarters gets sold regardless of thesis) and trimming an overweight position back down once it clears roughly 12-15% of the portfolio, independent of whether the thesis is still intact.
- He is explicit that the technical system is a choice, not a universal law: "in my satellite PF stocks but in my core holdings, I am not following this because my business thought process is closed." (FSSMF 01:56:49).

## 4. WHAT THE LADDER MISSES

(b) No rule for exit/sell logic at all. Every rule in CONTEXT.md's 16-entry rulebook is framed as an entry/screening gate or a corroborating observation — there is nothing in G0/G1/G3/G8 or the observations list that encodes when to SELL a name already held. This entire lecture's core content (time stop loss, position trimming above 12-15%, the 30-week-EMA/RS/V-Stop system-sell trigger, peak-margin/peak-sentiment, thesis-breakdown, auditor/governance red flags) is structurally absent from the current rulebook. This is the single largest gap this lecture surfaces.

(b) The RSI usage in the ladder (entry_rsi-001, gate G8, "weekly RSI at or above 50," sourced from HOWB) is an entry-momentum-confirmation rule, not a sell rule, so it is not directly contradicted by this lecture. But this lecture explicitly and specifically warns against using RSI overbought readings to trigger a SELL ("for buying selling this is a worst indicator," FSSMF 01:53:46) — if the ladder or any future extension of it is tempted to add an RSI-based exit rule, this lecture is a direct, named objection (CDSL, MCX cited as stocks whose RSI stayed overbought for years while compounding). Worth recording as a guardrail even though it doesn't condition an existing rule.

(a) capital_structure_trend-001 (debt_to_equity_delta_3y <= 0, "is the overall balance sheet getting stronger or weaker") is a pure balance-sheet-direction observation sourced from MASTEA. This lecture adds a sharper, cash-flow-paired version of the same idea as an active SELL trigger, not just an observation: rising debt combined with negative operating cash flow together (not debt direction alone) is the red flag — quoted in full in section 3 (FSSMF 00:55:01). The ladder's existing rule only reads D/E direction in isolation; this lecture's condition is compound (debt rising AND CFO negative together) and is framed as a hard sell trigger on names like Gensol, not a soft directional read.

(c) The "1.1-1.2 times the peg ratio" figure (FSSMF 00:11:04) is a dated, single-business-story remark made in passing while discussing an unnamed "auto-anx" component stock's earnings growth (~30-35%), not a stated universal PEG bar — it should NOT be read as competing with or tightening the ladder's existing peg_ratio-001 (<=1.5x, from INSIGN). Flagging this explicitly so a future pass doesn't mistake it for a second, stricter PEG threshold.

(c) The 70% distance-from-30-week-EMA "parabolic/Eiffel Tower" trigger (FSSMF 02:01:34) and the "12-15%" position-concentration trim trigger (FSSMF 01:31:27) are both given as clean numeric thresholds and are genuinely repeated (not single-company examples) — these read as intended-general rules, not one-off worked examples, and are candidates for encoding if/when the ladder gets a sell-side module.

Everything else in this lecture (the pre-webinar market-overview segment, sector calls, mutual-fund picks, the SOIC-membership pitch) is out of scope for the ladder's own logic and not counted as a gap.
