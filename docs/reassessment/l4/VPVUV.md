## 1. CRUX
The lecture is teaching that volume (raw volume, a 20-period moving average of volume, and On Balance Volume/OBV) is a confirming "weight of evidence" layer to be added ON TOP OF the already-taught entry framework (Vstop + ADX) — the decision it changes is not whether to buy, but how confident to be that a stage-2 breakout is real institutional accumulation rather than a false or unsupported move, per "price will give you what is happening in a company but volume is the weight of the evidence" (VPVUV 00:02:00).

## 2. MECHANISM
- Volume's role is framed by stage analysis: "how volume analysis can help you to figure out if stage 2 trend is starting or if a stage 3 or stage 4 decline is starting" (VPVUV 00:00:04); ideally the maximum volume accompanies "taking something stationary to a position of flying" i.e. the stage-1-to-stage-2 breakout (VPVUV 00:00:49).
- Volume itself is simply defined: "volume simply indicates the number of shares that have been bought and sold in a particular time period" (VPVUV 00:02:38). Rising volume in the direction of a price breakout = bullish, smart-money/institutional entry; worked example: modern beverages [likely a garbled company name — real identity not confirmed by the reader] volume spiked because it "got included in MSCI index," and "the stock went from 1249 to 1410 in no time" (VPVUV 00:03:15-00:03:19).
- A moving average of volume is plotted on the chart with period 20: "this is the ML nth is 20 right so it will be measuring the last 20 bars" (VPVUV 00:06:03); when the volume bar sits "substantially above the moving average" (VPVUV 00:06:03) it "points to the entry of some smart money or a large investor or institutional money" (VPVUV 00:06:17), shown across weekly and monthly chart views in the same walkthrough (VPVUV 00:06:17-00:06:53) — no fixed timeframe is asserted as correct; the instructor demonstrates the same 20-period volume MA on both weekly and monthly charts of the same stock in sequence.
- On Balance Volume (OBV) is a cumulative running total: "on days where prices went up, that is volume is added to the OBV total" (VPVUV 00:09:33), formally "current closing price is greater than the prior closing price then previous OBV plus current volume" (VPVUV 00:10:24), and subtracted on down days. The instructor repeatedly insists the SLOPE, not the level, is what matters: "Always focus on the slope" (VPVUV 00:11:12).
- Volume/OBV is explicitly positioned as an addition to an existing rule, not a replacement: "we have learned that entry should be taken when we stop is positive" (VPVUV 00:12:30), "ADX is above 20 pyramid up" (VPVUV 00:12:35), and "you can also add on balance volume or volumes to your sound like to your framework as well" (VPVUV 00:12:36) — "sound like to your" is ASR-garbled, most plausibly "own" framework.
- A worked exit-timing nuance on Laurus Labs: a promoter/PE block sale ("Warburg pink is sold this on 66 rupees on fourth May" (VPVUV 00:12:58), likely "Warburg Pincus") made the OBV slope dip, but Vstop had "already given you an exit much before your on-balance volume... starts pointing towards down and because slow, slow distribution can happen" (VPVUV 00:13:25) — i.e. price-based Vstop can lead OBV's slope-turn as an exit signal.

## 4. WHAT THE LADDER MISSES

(b) The ladder has zero rule of any kind — HARD, SOFT, or observation — for volume, volume moving average, or OBV. All three of this lecture's core indicators (raw volume direction, the 20-period volume MA, and OBV slope) are completely absent from the 16-entry rulebook and the 12 observations in CONTEXT.md. This is the central content of the lecture and the ladder has no rule for it at all — not even as an "observation" (context-only, non-gating) entry, the weakest tier CONTEXT.md defines.

(b) The existing entry_adx-001 / entry_rsi-001 rules (G8: weekly ADX >= 20, weekly RSI >= 50, both sourced to REF HOWB) are consistent with what this lecture calls "we have already learned" (VPVUV 00:12:30-00:12:35: Vstop positive, ADX above 20), but this lecture explicitly says to layer volume/OBV confirmation ON TOP of that entry condition ("add on balance volume or volumes to your... framework as well," VPVUV 00:12:36) — the ladder has never encoded that additional confirming layer, so a company can pass G8 on RSI/ADX alone with volume actively contradicting the breakout (e.g., low or falling volume, or volume below its own moving average), and the ladder would not know.

(c) The "ML nth is 20" period-20 volume moving average (VPVUV 00:06:03) is demonstrated once, on one stock's weekly-then-monthly chart, as a default/example indicator setting inside the trading-view walkthrough — it is not stated as a universal rule that ALL volume analysis must use a 20-period MA, just the setting shown in this particular demo. Any future rule built from this number should not be over-generalized into a strict "20-week/20-month volume MA crossover" gate without re-checking whether the instructor treats 20 as a fixed convention elsewhere in the course.

Nothing in this lecture qualifies or conditions any of the fundamental-ratio rules (sales/PAT growth, D/E, ROCE, PE, PEG, CFO conversion, etc.) — it is purely a technical/price-volume layer and does not touch fundamentals.
