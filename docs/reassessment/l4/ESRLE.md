LECTURE: Exclusive SOIC Ranking Sheet + Long Term Indicator    REF: ESRLE    (transcript: 123957 chars, 1594 lines, covered: yes)

## 1. CRUX

This lecture is trying to change how a student SEQUENCES and WEIGHS a stock
decision: it hands over an objective, points-based ranking sheet (industry →
fundamentals → technicals → valuation, in that order) plus a companion
trading-view indicator, so that "very very objective" ranking replaces
narrative-driven buy/hold/sell calls (ESRLE 00:02:41).

## 2. MECHANISM

- The ranking sheet scores a stock across four gates in a fixed order:
  industry structure (max 45 points, worked live on Narayana Hrudayalaya
  and Balaji Amines), then ~6-8 fundamental line items, then technicals,
  then valuation -- "our technicals, valuation of forensic parameters are
  there there is no simple 20 point checklist" (ESRLE 00:35:41-00:35:46,
  transcribed as heard).
- Industry gate asks four yes/no questions -- growing industry?, is it
  concentrated ("If one company controls the industry or if top 7 players
  are less than 50% it means the industry is crowded" ESRLE 00:07:50-00:07:56),
  regulatory interference (an automatic zero-out: "So here it will be
  penalized and here it will be yes, there is a regulatory interference."
  ESRLE 00:13:43, followed immediately by "So by penalizing it, the score
  will be zero here." ESRLE 00:13:48), and near-term tailwind/headwind.
- The technical layer replaces classic EMA-based "stage analysis" with two
  homemade indicators built from V-stop + relative-strength-vs-Nifty: a buy
  trigger fires when "V stop is positive, ADX is above 20 and RSI is above
  45." (ESRLE 01:50:37), "and when it is relatively outperforming the
  index." (ESRLE 01:50:43), and this same V-stop turning negative is the exit
  signal -- weekly V-stop for satellite positions, monthly V-stop for core
  positions (ESRLE 01:03:03-01:03:16).
- Position sizing/pyramiding is done in tranches gated by Parabolic SAR
  flags turning positive, not in one shot (ESRLE 02:08:47-02:09:07).
- Portfolio construction is a second, separate layer on top of the
  per-stock score: 10-25 total names, split into a "core" bucket (6-8%
  each, via a "2468" framework) and a "satellite" bucket capped at 20% of
  total portfolio (ESRLE 01:55:40-01:59:22).
- The instructor frames selling as mechanical, not narrative: "What is
  there in your hands?" (ESRLE 02:03:01), "Buying, selling and holding the
  stock." (ESRLE 02:03:03), "What is not in your hands is MD continuing."
  (ESRLE 02:03:05) -- illustrated by exiting South Indian Bank on an
  MD-resignation headline alone (ESRLE 02:03:11-02:03:38).

## 4. WHAT THE LADDER MISSES

(a) Rules the ladder encoded that this lecture conditions/qualifies:

1. `entry_rsi-001` (weekly_rsi >= 50, sourced from HOWB) is directly
   contradicted by this lecture's repeated statement of RSI above 45 as the
   buy-trigger default: "We talked about RSI above 45" (ESRLE 01:16:04);
   "I will leave it at 45" (ESRLE 01:49:39). This is a genuine cross-lecture
   number conflict (45 vs. 50), not a rounding difference, and the ladder
   currently resolves it silently by picking one source.

2. `entry_adx-001` (weekly_adx >= 20) is qualified, not just corroborated:
   the instructor explicitly frames ADX=20 as his own optional default that
   can legitimately be set to 0 ("we made it so flexible for you that you
   will do ADX 0" ESRLE 01:27:22; "you can experiment with ADX as
   0" ESRLE 01:49:29-01:49:34) -- it is a toggle, not an absolute gate.

3. `cash_conversion-001` (~70% B2C / ~60% B2B split) is qualified by a
   flatter, undifferentiated band from this lecture: "CFO to EBITDA
   conversion between 50 to 70 percent or above 70 percent try" (ESRLE
   01:09:08-01:09:13) -- no B2C/B2B split stated here.

4. `peg_ratio-001` (<= 1.5) is qualified by an explicit graded secondary
   band this lecture states but the ladder's hard cutoff does not capture:
   "peg somewhere between less than 1.5 or between 1.5 to 2.2" (ESRLE
   01:08:59-01:09:00).

(b) Central points this lecture makes that the ladder has no rule for at
    all:

1. The industry-structure gate (growing vs. stagnant, concentration/
   duopoly-or-crowded, regulatory-interference override-to-zero,
   near-term tailwind/headwind) is explicitly the FIRST and largest single
   component of the instructor's own scoring rubric (up to 45 of the total
   points), run before fundamentals, technicals, or valuation are even
   looked at. The ladder has no representation of industry concentration
   or regulatory risk anywhere in its 16 rules.

2. G2 (forensic veto) is stated in CONTEXT.md to have no rule at all --
   this lecture supplies a concrete, named forensic checklist that is a
   direct candidate to fill it: auditor resignation in the last year,
   contingent liability under 15% of net worth, and promoter pledge status
   (ESRLE 01:09:18-01:09:21). This is the single most load-bearing gap:
   the ladder cannot currently REJECT anything on forensic grounds, and
   this lecture names exactly the kind of checks that gate would need.

3. The V-stop + relative-strength-vs-Nifty "stage" framework (Stage 1-4)
   is the entire technical backbone of this lecture and the Level 4 course
   it summarizes. The ladder's technical rules are two single-point gates
   (RSI, ADX); it has no concept of trend stage, relative strength vs.
   benchmark, or a V-stop-based trailing exit, even though these are all
   derivable from price series the ladder already fetches.

4. Portfolio-construction rules (10-25 total names, core sized 6-8% via a
   "2468" framework, satellite capped at 20% of total portfolio, tranche-
   based pyramiding on Parabolic SAR signals) are a whole layer of
   position-sizing logic the ladder does not attempt -- the ladder produces
   a per-company verdict only, with no allocation guidance at all.

5. A total-score philosophy with a stated pass bar ("if it crosses 200,
   then you know it's a very good score... crossing 230 is also one of the
   key criteria" ESRLE 01:38:40-01:38:53) is fundamentally different from
   the ladder's gate-based PASS/FAIL/ABSTAIN/CANDIDATE/WATCH verdict
   scheme -- an additive scoring model vs. a gating model. Worth flagging
   as a structural mismatch, not just a missing threshold.

(c) Dated, one-company thresholds risk being read as universal:

- The P/E > 50 "penalize" line is illustrated entirely through Navin
  Fluorine's specific 2021-era situation (31.5x PE, 252-275% export
  growth, yet "since July 2021, it has just given 20% return" ESRLE
  01:07:37-01:07:49) -- a single dated example of a fast grower still
  getting de-rated. The number 50 itself is stated as a general rule, but
  the corroborating story is one company at one point in time and should
  not be read as a sector-agnostic calibration.
- The "score 248" and the "200 good / 230 gold standard" bar come from the
  instructor's own real, undisclosed "company zero" and live portfolio
  scores at the time of the session -- these are illustrative anchors from
  his own scoring exercise, not a published, reproducible cutoff.
