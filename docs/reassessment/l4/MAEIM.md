## 1. CRUX

The lecture builds a weekly-chart entry/exit timing system (30-week EMA direction, EMA+Volatility-Stop confirmation, and golden crossover/cross-down) whose stated purpose is to give a mechanical exit discipline specifically to the SATELLITE portfolio bucket — cyclical, lower-conviction, higher-risk bets — as distinct from the core portfolio's fundamental screen (MAEIM 00:18:10-00:18:29).

## 2. MECHANISM

- A moving average is the rolling average price over a chosen lookback; exponential moving average (EMA) weights recent prices more than a simple moving average (SMA), so EMA "will bake in the recent price action much, much sooner as compared to a simple moving average" (MAEIM 00:10:46).
- Apply this on weekly charts, not monthly. Instructor's suggested set is "EMA 10, EMA 30 and EMA 40 max to max" (MAEIM 00:12:53), but the specific one carried over from stage analysis is the 30-week EMA: "as we learned in stage analysis... which moving average to be used is 30 weekly moving average" (MAEIM 00:14:08).
- Direction of the 30-week EMA is the core signal: "once the moving average starts pointing upwards it means that it goes into a bullish trend" (MAEIM 00:14:38); when the EMA later "starts pointing downwards" that is described as "the start of stage four" and "can be used as a selling pointer" (MAEIM 00:17:57).
- The signal is regime-dependent, by the instructor's own caveat: "if you're following it as an entry and exit signal, then it will only work in an upwards market" and "It won't work in a sideways market" (MAEIM 00:15:15-00:15:20).
- For entries, EMA direction is combined with a weekly Volatility Stop (VStop, 2x multiplier): "both of them should be giving an entry... your EMA should be pointing upwards and your volatility stop should be green" — one signal alone is insufficient (MAEIM 00:24:21-00:24:28). For exits, VStop alone is used because it fires earlier than EMA: "for exits, exits you use Vstop on your weekly charts... it will give you much better exits when it's... especially [for] your basically satellite portfolio" (MAEIM 00:24:38-00:24:43).
- Golden crossover/cross-down (short EMA, e.g. 10-week, crossing the long EMA, e.g. 30-week) is a separate, secondary signal the instructor uses mainly as a screening filter rather than a trading trigger: "golden crossover means is that the shorter duration moving average crosses the longer duration moving average from the bottom" and cross-down is the reverse (MAEIM 00:25:43); "I particularly like this strategy to just to filter stocks or screen for stocks" (MAEIM 00:27:51).

## 3. SIGNALS

- [HARD] Golden crossover (short-EMA crosses above long-EMA) / golden cross-down (crosses below) on weekly EMAs is a bullish/bearish screening signal (MAEIM 00:25:43, 00:28:20). Computable from weekly price series; no rule for it exists in the rulebook.
