## 1. CRUX

This lecture is not a fundamentals checklist -- it is a workflow for turning
5000 tradeable names into a short, high-probability watchlist using layered
screens (quarterly growth, volume/accumulation, relative strength,
institutional ownership) as a triage step, then handing the survivors to a
manual reading process (con calls, investor presentations, management
interviews) before any decision is made; the decision it targets is which of
5000 stocks deserve reading time, not which stock to buy.

## 2. MECHANISM

- Filtration exists because 5000 stocks generate noise (WhatsApp tips,
  Twitter calls); a mental model narrows the field to where "your
  probability of winnings is more" (TFELT 00:02:16).
- The organizing principle for every screen is the idea of something new --
  a company doing something it wasn't doing before (new capex, new product,
  new geography, new client, new segment, new acquisition) is what produces
  the earnings inflection a screen is trying to catch: "always remember that
  new is a very powerful word when it comes to filtration" (TFELT 00:07:19).
- He layers CANSLIM (William O'Neil) as the base quantitative screen, then
  augments it with volume/accumulation signals, a relative-strength (not
  RSI) screen he calls "the holy grail of all screens" (TFELT 01:11:03), and
  institutional/promoter shareholding-change screens -- each screen is a
  different lens on the same underlying idea, that money and information
  flow toward a company before its story is fully priced in.
- "See fundamentals have a momentum" (TFELT 00:09:59): a company doing well
  for one quarter tends to keep doing well for several more, "60-70% of the
  time" (TFELT 00:11:06), and this is his stated justification for why a
  quarterly-growth screen has predictive value at all, not just descriptive
  value.
- A screen firing is explicitly the START of work, not the end: after a
  quarterly-growth screen fires, he asks "are you understanding that after
  screening once, we have to do something?" and answers "Once we have a
  strong growth rate, we will start reading after that" (TFELT
  00:32:35-00:32:50) -- con call, press release, DRHP, management YouTube
  interviews -- to determine if the growth trigger is real or fake, cyclical
  or structural (TFELT 00:32:19-00:32:33).
- Relative strength (price performance vs. the index) is framed as being as
  much a fundamental signal as a technical one, because outperformance
  implies the market is pricing in earnings growth faster than the index's
  own ~12-14% long-run return (TFELT 01:14:54-01:15:03) -- this is his
  bridge between the quant screens and the chart-based screens.

## 3. SIGNALS

## 4. WHAT THE LADDER MISSES

(a) canslim_sales-001 / canslim_pat-001 (sales >=15%, PAT >=20%) are
    provenanced to a quote and timestamp (00:09:35) that is identical, word
    for word, to what appears in THIS transcript at the same timestamp
    (TFELT 00:09:35) -- this lecture is very likely the true source (or a
    re-run of the same script) under a different REF mnemonic. Two things
    the ladder's encoding of these two rules drops that this lecture states
    in the same breath: (i) the screen also always carries a ROC>=15% leg
    AND a market-cap floor (>=1000 cr default) -- sales/PAT growth alone
    was never run as a two-variable screen in this lecture, it is always a
    4-part query (TFELT 00:23:47, 00:42:00-00:42:15); (ii) the ROC leg is
    explicitly waivable for turnaround companies (TFELT 00:42:09), a
    qualifier the ladder's own capital_efficiency_gate-001 (G3, ROCE>=15,
    not applied to lenders) does not carry at all -- it exempts lenders but
    says nothing about turnaround/inflection non-lenders, which this
    lecture explicitly treats as a second, separate exemption class.

(b) No rule anywhere in the 16-entry rulebook enforces a market-cap floor.
    This lecture states one as a load-bearing, non-optional leg of its
    headline screen, with a stated reason (sub-100cr names carry "very
    negative cash loads or poor business models," TFELT 00:42:15) -- this
    is a central, repeatedly-used criterion (also implicit in "I will
    ignore the small market tab companies because sometimes there is no
    information," TFELT 01:16:46) that the ladder has no equivalent of at
    all.

(c) The ladder has no rule at all for CANSLIM's N (new
    product/geography/segment/acquisition/capex), which this lecture treats
    as the single most important organizing idea of the entire session --
    "always remember that new is a very powerful word when it comes to
    filtration" (TFELT 00:07:19) -- illustrated with roughly a dozen worked
    examples. This is [SOFT] (needs con-call/investor-presentation reading)
    so it cannot become a HARD gate, but its complete absence from the
    rulebook -- even as a documented, non-gating observation -- means the
    ladder has zero representation of what this instructor calls the
    actual causal mechanism behind every screen hit.

(d) The ladder has no rule for CANSLIM's L (leader/laggard) or I
    (institutional sponsorship) -- FII/DII/promoter holding-change screens,
    which this lecture spends a full segment on (TFELT
    01:24:03-01:35:25) with a specific, reusable numeric recipe (FII change
    >2-3%, DII change >2-3%, optional promoter change >1-2%, market cap
    >1000cr). [SOFT] -- needs shareholding-pattern time-series data the
    ladder does not fetch -- but again, entirely unrepresented, not even as
    an observation.

(e) The ladder's entry_rsi-001/entry_adx-001 pair (weekly RSI>=50, weekly
    ADX>=20, sourced to a different lecture, "HOWB") is NOT corroborated or
    conditioned by this lecture -- ADX is never mentioned here at all, and
    RSI is mentioned only to explicitly disclaim any relationship to
    relative strength: "relative strength and RSI are two different terms"
    (TFELT 01:52:40). This lecture's own preferred entry screen is relative
    strength making a 52-week high, "the holy grail of all screens" (TFELT
    01:11:03), a completely different, unfetched metric (stock-price-
    ratio-to-index series, with 13/26/52-week backtested lookbacks) that
    the ladder does not encode under any rule or observation. If a future
    rule is ever added for this lecture's entry screen, it must not be
    conflated with the existing RSI/ADX pair.

(f) The pe_context-001 observation's 15-35 reference band is not what this
    lecture states -- this lecture's own stated comfort band is narrower
    (15-30x, TFELT 00:33:51-00:34:07) and is explicitly scoped to growth
    names in an expensive market, with a separate, looser, explicitly-
    optional 40x cutoff used only as a stringency dial on the base screen
    (TFELT 00:42:15). Neither of TFELT's two PE numbers is the
    observation's 15-35 band; if this lecture is ever cited as a source for
    that observation, the exact number and its scope (personal-excitement
    threshold vs. optional dial, not a universal band) would need

(g) One dated, single-company anecdote worth flagging as NOT a universal
    bar if it were ever promoted to a rule: Sarda Energy's SKS Power
    acquisition is used at length (TFELT 00:44:43-00:48:20) purely as a
    worked "new segment" example (thermal power added to a coal-blocks

    attached to it, so there is no risk of it being mistaken for a rule,

Everything else in this lecture -- the CANSLIM framing itself, the
screen-fires-then-read sequencing, sector-rotation-via-relative-strength,
and stage analysis -- is [SOFT]/[JUDGE] material about HOW to search and
