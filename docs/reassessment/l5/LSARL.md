LECTURE: Learn Stage Analysis to Avoid Falling Knives    REF: LSARL    (transcript: 29895 chars, 327 lines, covered: yes)

## 1. CRUX

Teach a purely technical (price + volume, weekly chart) classification of a
stock into one of four Weinstein "stages" so a viewer can screen FOR stocks
entering stage 1 (base) / stage 2 (markup) and screen OUT stocks sitting in
stage 4 (markdown) -- the "falling knife" -- before any fundamental work is
even attempted; the decision it is trying to change is which stocks get
studied at all, not whether to buy a specific one.

## 2. MECHANISM

- Price and volume together signal intent, not price alone: price up +
  volume up is bullish ("if the price is going up but the volumes are also
  increasing it's a bullish pattern" (LSARL 00:04:30)); price down + volume
  up means "someone is basically dumping" (LSARL 00:04:48) -- distribution.
- After an up-move, sideways consolidation on shrinking volume is read as
  bullish, not neutral: "the selling force is missing" (LSARL 00:08:11) and
  "the buyers have taken up the counter and this is also known as the
  tightening pattern" (LSARL 00:08:17) -- existing holders refuse to sell,
  so the float tightens ahead of the next leg up.
- The whole cycle is a 4-stage loop sourced from a named book, not an SOIC
  invention: "this is what Stan Winston in his book, Profiting from Bull
  and Bear Markets says" (LSARL 00:22:09) [presenter says "Stan Winston";
  the book "Profiting from Bull and Bear Markets" is actually by Stan
  Weinstein -- likely an ASR/speaker slip, not a different author]. Stage 1
  = ignored base ("At first, it will be ignored" (LSARL 00:10:27), cricket-
  pitch flat consolidation); Stage 2 = markup (earnings beat estimates,
  "analysts have low B report" [likely "a low-ball report"] (LSARL
  00:11:04-00:11:15), institutional accumulation, breakout above the
  30-week EMA); Stage 3 = topping/distribution (assessment phase); Stage 4
  = markdown (loss of EPS momentum, guidance misses, price below the
  30-week EMA), eventually cycling back to a value/ignored stock.
- The 30-week EMA on a weekly chart is the mechanical stage divider: "the
  price action in the stock usually oscillates above and below the 30
  weekly moving average while forming a horizontal base" (LSARL 00:23:09);
  price above a rising 30wk EMA reads as stage 2, price below (with prior
  down-trend and overhead supply) reads as stage 1 or stage 4.
- "Overhead supply" explains why a chart stays capped rather than
  breaking into stage 2: repeated selling into every rally near a prior
  purchase level means "the first thing they bought, which is a poor
  forced long-term investor, is selling the stock at this time" (LSARL
  00:19:02) -- trapped longs recycling supply into strength.
- Screening intent is explicit and asymmetric: "identify these stocks in
  screeners using stage one, stage two, stage three, basically stage one
  and stage two we'll identify" (LSARL 00:14:57) while "Stage four is
  something that we'll steer clear of, but value screens can the stage
  four go be taken" (LSARL 00:15:07) -- stage 4 is excluded from the
  growth/momentum screen but not banned outright; a separate value-style
  screen may still pick it up.

- [SOFT] Stage 3-to-4 exit discipline for traders vs. investors differs

## 4. WHAT THE LADDER MISSES

(b) The entire stage-analysis / 30-week-EMA technical framework has NO
rule anywhere in the 16-entry rulebook. The only existing technical gate
is G8 (entry_rsi-001, entry_adx-001: weekly RSI >= 50, weekly ADX >= 20,
sourced from a different lecture, HOWB). This lecture's stage
classification is a distinct, prior technical filter -- "identify these
stocks in screeners using stage one, stage two" (LSARL 00:14:57) -- meant
to run BEFORE an RSI/ADX-style entry trigger, not instead of it. Nothing
in the rulebook encodes "price above/below its 30-week EMA" or "stage 4 =
avoid," even though the instructor states this as the single load-bearing
computable signal in the whole lecture (LSARL 00:26:07) and it is genuinely
[HARD] -- a 30-week EMA is directly computable from the weekly price
series the ladder can already fetch. This is the most concrete, missing,
data-cheap addition this lecture surfaces.

Important scoping note on G2: this lecture's "falling knife" avoidance is
NOT the same territory as G2's forensic-accounting veto, despite the
title's language. Nothing here concerns related-party transactions,
promoter pledging, auditor resignations, or accounting red flags -- every
signal in this lecture is price-and-volume technical (stage, 30wk EMA,
volume-on-consolidation). A stage-4 technical filter would be a genuinely
new, separate gate (a technical pre-screen), not a way to populate the
empty G2 forensic gate. Do not conflate the two when deciding where a
30wk-EMA rule should live in the ladder.

(a) None of the 16 rules is directly conditioned or qualified by this
lecture -- there is no overlap in metric between the existing rulebook
(CANSLIM sales/PAT growth, D/E, ROCE, weekly RSI/ADX) and this lecture's
stage/EMA vocabulary, so there is nothing here to narrow an existing rule's
scope.

(c) The Laurus Labs stage-4 confirmation at "a price of 600 to 620 at 520"
(LSARL 00:25:26) is exactly the kind of dated, one-company anecdote that
must never become a universal price bar -- it fixes a historical INR level
for one stock at one point in time, not a percentage or ratio that
generalizes.
