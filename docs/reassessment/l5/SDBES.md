LECTURE: 10.05.26 Screen Domestic Businesses    REF: SDBES    (transcript: 86451 chars, 777 lines, covered: yes)

## 1. CRUX

This lecture is not really about any single threshold — it is a tool-tutorial teaching a repeatable
WORKFLOW for cutting a 5000-stock universe down to a small reading watchlist inside the "Stock
Scans"/StockEdge platform: first confirm the industry/index is in an uptrend (stage 2), then run a
battery of named scans (earnings breakout, technical breakout, margin expansion, PAT consistency,
turnaround, institutional buying, warrant issues, acquisitions, volume), and — the point he repeats
most — treat every scan hit as a prompt to go READ the business (concalls/transcripts), never as a
buy signal by itself. The decision he is trying to change is "stop trying to manually eyeball 5000
stocks" and "stop treating a scan output (or FII/DII buying, or a warrant issue) as an answer instead
of a question to research."

## 2. MECHANISM

- Precondition, applied first: build/check a custom industry index and see whether that industry is
  in "stage 2" (uptrend/relative strength) or making new all-time highs, independent of whether the
  broad market (Nifty 50 / Nifty 500) is doing the same — he shows Nifty 50 stuck "in a channel"
  (SDBES 00:03:06) while his custom Fluorine chemicals index is "making all time" (SDBES 00:04:45), and states
  the causal chain: "if industry is having a relative strength and if industry is hitting stage 2 or
  in an all-time high territory, then the businesses that will be considering it will be doing the
  same thing" (SDBES 00:09:53-00:10:00).
- Second, run a fundamental "earnings breakout" scan: prior quarters must look range-bound (flat
  PAT), and the LATEST quarter must break out; scope and exact numbers below in SIGNALS.
- Third, run a technical breakout scan to confirm price is doing the same thing the earnings are
  doing — two time windows (long-term "after 200 days" up to "1000 days", or short-term "greater
  than 40" but "less than 200" days).
- Fourth — the step he calls out explicitly as the payoff step, not optional — read the con
  call/transcript of whatever the scan surfaces to find out WHY the number moved: "your three step
  framework is done, you find out business where earnings are being break out, second you see
  technical break out and third you read this business" (SDBES 00:39:01-00:39:09).
- In parallel/supplementary, he layers qualitative and ownership-based scans (margin expansion
  consistency, PAT consistency, loss-to-profit turnaround, warrant issues, FII/DII stake increase,
  investor question-tracking, shareholding-pattern tracking, acquisition tracking, volume) — each one
  explicitly framed as a lead to investigate, not a verdict, and several come with an explicit warning
  that the raw scan hit can mislead if read alone (see WHAT THE LADDER MISSES + the PCBL example).
- Everything is built and re-run as a saved, alertable scan in the tool ("stock scans"/StockEdge) so
  it becomes a recurring watch, not a one-time filter.

## 3. SIGNALS

  stage 2 or in an all-time high territory, then the businesses that will be considering it will be
  doing the same thing" (SDBES 00:09:53-00:10:00). This is checked FIRST, before any per-stock scan,

  crores" AND "more than 1000 crores" — i.e. a small/mid-cap window, explicitly because "I am putting

## 4. WHAT THE LADDER MISSES

This lecture is almost entirely about a technical/momentum + qualitative screening WORKFLOW built in a
third-party scanning tool (StockEdge/"stock scans"), not about the ladder's fundamental gates (G0
sales/PAT growth, G1 leverage, G3 ROCE). Only two direct overlaps with the existing 16-rule ladder
exist, and both are conditioned/qualified rather than contradicted:

(a) canslim_pat-001 (G0, "Quarterly profit growing at least 20% year on year", ref MASTEC 00:09:35) is
NOT the same pattern this lecture teaches. This lecture's earnings-breakout scan requires PAT to be
FLAT/RANGE-BOUND across the prior 3-4 quarters and then break out with "today's earnings are more
than 30%" (SDBES 00:14:52) — a shape-of-curve (consolidation-then-breakout) test, not a plain
"grew ≥20% YoY" threshold — and it is explicitly scoped to a market-cap window ("less than 20,000
crores and more than 1000 crores", SDBES 00:14:52-00:17:50), a small/mid-cap-only window the ladder
rule carries no such restriction for. A company clearing canslim_pat-001's 20% YoY bar every quarter
consistently (steady grower) would NOT be what this scan is hunting for — the scan is specifically
hunting for the INFLECTION from flat to breakout, which is a different, narrower, and unrepresented
pattern.

(b) entry_rsi-001 / entry_adx-001 (G8, weekly RSI ≥50 and weekly ADX ≥20, ref HOWB) have no
counterpart or contradiction here — this lecture's technical scans use entirely different mechanics
(breakout-window-in-days since a prior high, 52/26-week high proximity, volume vs. 50-day average,
relative-strength %), so there is nothing to condition; it is simply a disjoint technical toolkit the
ladder does not encode at all (point b below covers this properly).

Central points this lecture makes that the ladder has NO rule for at all:
- The industry/custom-index stage-2 precondition — checking whether the relevant industry itself is
  in an uptrend BEFORE screening individual names within it (SDBES 00:09:53-00:10:00). This is the
  lecture's single most repeated idea and the ladder has no sector/industry-momentum gate whatsoever.
- The institutional-interest (FII+DI stake +2%) scan, and — more importantly — his own backtest
  showing it is a WEAK standalone signal ("the related strength came out to be only 25 business are
  outperforming after FI, DI, Z have increase your stake when you were scanning and rest are under
  performing", SDBES 01:01:10), illustrated by the PCBL counter-example where FIIs/DIs bought and
  "the stock corrected" (SDBES 00:58:26) because "the market cycle is the earnings are bad" (SDBES
  00:58:36) — a rule the ladder doesn't have, paired with an explicit warning against treating it
  simplistically that the ladder also doesn't have.
- The warrant-issue "why + who" qualitative checklist (SDBES 00:27:12-00:27:23) — entirely absent.
- Margin-expansion consistency (both variants) and PAT-consistency-over-4-quarters scans — entirely
  absent; these are close cousins of existing observations (cash_conversion-001, capital_efficiency
  observations) but test trend-consistency of margins/PAT, which nothing in the ladder currently does.
- The turnaround/loss-to-profit scan scoped to recently-listed "bombed out IPO" names (SDBES 00:52:32,
  00:53:03) — a cohort-specific pattern absent from the ladder.
- The explicit "screen fires → now go read the business" discipline itself (the "three step
  framework", SDBES 00:39:01; also "I got a problem with a scan" and, separately, "I got a problem
  that I should read this business", both SDBES 00:17:26-00:17:28) — this is a process rule, not a
  data rule, but it is the lecture's central instruction and the ladder (a numeric gate system)
  structurally cannot represent "go read the concall next," which is exactly why it belongs here as a
  gap, not a rule to encode.
- The Car Trade illustration of a scan's blind spot: margin genuinely expanded (27.2% to ~35%) but
  "why this didn't come in our screen" (SDBES 00:45:41) is answered as "This is a technical, this is
  a, this is a match's scan" (SDBES 00:46:02) and "So if the stocks fall in that scan, then the
  business will not come" (SDBES 00:46:06) — a direct warning that a real positive can be excluded by
  a neighboring filter in a multi-scan combination, which is structurally what the ladder's
  multi-gate design also risks and has no rule expressing.

No dated one-company threshold in this lecture is being mistaken for a universal bar in the existing
16-entry ladder — none of the numbers above (30% PAT breakout, 1000-20000cr window, 10%/20% margin
steps, 2% FII/DI stake, 200-1000 day breakout window, etc.) appear in the current ladder at all, so
there is nothing here to flag as an over-generalized worked example already baked in.
