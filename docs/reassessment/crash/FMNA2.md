## 1. CRUX

Teach the mechanics of turning management guidance (from con-calls, presentations, broker reports) into a forward 2-3 year P&L, then convert that forward PAT estimate into bull/base/bear exit-multiple scenarios to get an expected CAGR — i.e. this lecture is trying to change how a student *builds* a forecast, not which stock to buy; the worked companies (SJS, Interarch, Gravita, Manorama, Credit Access Grameen) are teaching props for the method, not verdicts.

## 2. MECHANISM

- Walk the income statement chain step by step — Sales (top line) minus COGS/raw material = Gross Profit; minus SG&A = EBITDA; minus depreciation = EBIT; minus interest = PBT; minus tax = PAT — using a samosa-shop toy example before touching real numbers (FMNA2 00:00:32-00:04:19).
- Source every forward input from the con-call/management guidance itself, read live on screen, not invented: sales growth guidance, EBITDA margin guidance, capex plan, cash balance, tax rate (FMNA2 00:05:45, 00:13:00-00:14:47).
- Before trusting a company-stated number, check it against what was actually reported and understand the gap. For SJS the company claimed 30.5% EBITDA margin while reported margin was 29.3%; the instructor traces this to a one-off gratuity/labour-code provision before deciding "So I should work with what the company is saying" (FMNA2 00:08:34-00:09:04).
- Sanity-check the forward growth assumption against the company's own worst-case historical growth floor: "even in the worst of the auto cycle has grown at 17%" (FMNA2 00:10:50). Cross-check against management's own outperformance claim, guided at "2.5x" industry growth (FMNA2 00:09:39), and deliberately dial the assumption down to stay conservative rather than assume the company keeps outperforming at the higher rate it was actually tracking: "I will be conservative and not give 3x growth" (FMNA2 00:29:14).
- Build individual line items as simple ratios rather than a full balance-sheet forecast, explicitly framed as approximate: depreciation as a historical percentage of sales — "5.5% which is historically a number between 5.3 to 5.5%" (FMNA2 00:15:46) — and other income as projected cash balance times an assumed yield — "we put a yield on this, that the company is earning almost 7-8% today, we will take 7% yield" (FMNA2 00:33:44) — plus tax rate from the guided effective rate. The instructor repeatedly names this posture explicitly: "on a finance is more important than be 100% right" (FMNA2 00:05:45), and later, reflecting on the finished SJS model, "this is being roughly right" (FMNA2 00:40:11).
- Cross-check the self-built model against an independent broker's published assumptions (Monarch) on the same company before trusting either (FMNA2 00:13:00-00:40:01).
- Convert the forward PAT into a forward market cap and forward P/E/PEG using an assumed exit multiple, then build three parallel scenarios (bull/base/bear) that vary BOTH the PAT growth rate and the exit P/E multiple together, and read off the resulting CAGR for each case (FMNA2 00:44:56-00:46:18).
- Demonstrate an emerging AI-assisted variant of the same workflow: a custom Claude prompt fed the latest results + con-call transcript builds a formula-linked 3-year Excel model automatically. The instructor validates the tool's output against his own manual assumption rather than trusting it outright: "Actually, it's very close to my own assumptions." (FMNA2 00:58:23) — "My own assumptions are 210 to 20 crore plus pad." (FMNA2 00:58:25) — "It's got to 191 crore pad almost." (FMNA2 00:58:29). He also shows a bank/NBFC-specific variant ("ROA tree") for Credit Access Grameen (FMNA2 00:52:41-01:02:43).
