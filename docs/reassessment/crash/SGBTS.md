## 1. CRUX

This lecture is not a growth-screening checklist at all — it is a discipline lecture arguing that growth is inherently temporary and mean-reverting (base effect, cyclicality, "nothing is permanent"), so the decision it is trying to change is not "does this company clear a growth bar" but "do you know WHY a stock is growing, and will you sell the moment that specific reason breaks, regardless of price" (SGBTS 00:36:33-00:38:15).

## 2. MECHANISM

- PE is "perception upon earnings" (SGBTS 00:16:23), and perception is driven mainly by the growth rate and the CHANGE in growth rate — an earnings surprise causes the market to say "this company's P rate has started to be re-rated" (SGBTS 00:16:49).
- The base effect is the causal reason supernormal growth cannot persist: "if you're growing at a super normal pace, what happens is that your base starts becoming bigger" (SGBTS 00:18:30-00:18:39), worked through decade-by-decade for HDFC Bank (50-60% in the 1990s down to "14 to 15%" by the 2020s) and Bajaj Finance (50-60% in 2010-15 down to "22-23%" now) (SGBTS 00:18:41-00:19:23).
- Sales/earnings growth itself decomposes into distinct drivers he treats as different in quality, not interchangeable: volume growth vs price/mix-led growth ("volume growth and pricing mix are very much in sales growth" (SGBTS 00:52:58)), then operating leverage/economies of scale, backward-integration cost efficiencies, and AI/automation-driven cost efficiencies as separate, non-sales levers on the P&L (SGBTS 00:53:35-00:56:30).
- At the industry level, growth comes from demand-supply mismatch, regulatory/norm changes, new-industry creation, and consolidation (SGBTS 01:07:44-01:07:51), each illustrated with a live worked example (hotels 2003-08 pricing power, memory-chip shortage, cement consolidation).
- Growth is empirically cyclical and non-repeating across market leaders: "There wasn't even a single business which is repeated in every 5 year time frame in the next time period" (SGBTS 00:28:03).
- The behavioural payoff of all this: once you've identified the specific growth trigger you bought for, "if that growth trigger goes away, should I sell or sit?" (SGBTS 00:36:42) — his answer is sell on thesis-break, not on price: "stop loss can be applied, thesis break can be applied" (SGBTS 00:36:48).
