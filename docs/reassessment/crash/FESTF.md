LECTURE: 15.12.24 Class 4 How to Filter Epic Stocks    REF: FESTF    (transcript: 129544 chars, 1203 lines, covered: yes)

## 1. CRUX

This lecture is not about picking or valuing stocks at all — it teaches the
INPUT stage that happens before any of the ladder's gates run: how to build
your own repeatable screens (out of 5000 listed names) so that a small,
high-probability shortlist surfaces itself weekly, and then what to do next
with a name that clears a screen (read the con call, presentation, DRHP,
management interview) — the decision it targets is "stop reacting to
WhatsApp/Twitter noise and build a personal, mechanical filtration habit."

## 2. MECHANISM

- Filtration exists to cut noise: "there is a lot of noise in the market" out
  of "5000 stocks" (FESTF 00:01:50), and a stated criteria set narrows that
  down to where "your probability of winnings is more" (FESTF 00:02:16).
- The organizing idea is CANSLIM's "C" — Current/big/accelerating quarterly
  earnings and sales growth, sourced explicitly from William O'Neill's book,
  named in the transcript as "How to make marine stocks" [ASR garble; the
  real title is "How to Make Money in Stocks"] (FESTF 00:08:59): "C stands
  for Current Bigger Accelerating Quarterly Earnings and Sales Growth"
  (FESTF 00:09:08). In Indian-market
  terms he operationalizes this as "companies which are showing more than
  15% sales growth and more than 20% profit after tax growth" (FESTF
  00:09:35).
- The live screener query he builds combines FOUR criteria at once, not
  independent gates: "year on year quarterly sales growth of more than 15%
  Year on year quarterly profit growth of more than 20% ROC of more than 15%
  and market cap of more than 1000kroners" [ASR garble, likely "1000 crores"]
  (FESTF 00:23:47), optionally tightened with "price to earnings ratio as
  company That should be below 40 mark" (FESTF 00:42:15).
- A pure numeric hit is not enough — it must be paired with "new": new capex,
  new product, new geography, new client, new segment, new management, or new
  acquisition. "Always remember that new is a very powerful word when it
  comes to filtration" (FESTF 00:07:19); he later names the same idea
  directly: "We are going through a screening thought process." (FESTF
  00:53:28), followed immediately by "That something new is happening."
  (FESTF 00:53:31).
- Once a name clears the numeric+"new" screen, the ladder's job (per this
  lecture) stops and human research begins: "I will come to the conference
  call and see the company's press release If it is a recent type, I will
  study the company's DRHP If it is an interview for management, I will find
  an interview on YouTube" (FESTF 00:32:35) — explicitly framed as the very
  next step, not optional: "Once we have a strong growth rate, we will start
  reading after that, yes or no?" (FESTF 00:32:50).
- Two further independent screening lenses are taught on top of the earnings
  screen: (1) volume/accumulation ("your average 10 day volume is 1.5 times
  higher than the last 20 days of volume" (FESTF 01:01:08) signals
  institutional accumulation before news breaks), and (2) relative strength
  vs the Nifty index making 52-week highs — his "blue dot list" (FESTF
  01:11:00), which he calls "the holy grail of all screens" (FESTF
  01:11:03): "Stocks where relative strength is 52 weeks high, not the
  stock price, relative strength" (FESTF 01:11:19).
  He explicitly separates this from RSI: "Relative strength is a study of a
  securities performance versus the index whereas RSI is a momentum
  oscillator RSI does not confuse you with relative strength, RSI is a
  different thing So relative strength and RSI are different" (FESTF
  01:45:10).
- A third independent lens is institutional/promoter ownership change (FII,
  DII, promoter holding, tracked via screener), built live with shifting
  thresholds (3% → 2% → 1% → 0.5% depending on how many names the query
  returns) — "Here I'll come to this FII plus DI lapping up screen" (FESTF
  01:27:58).

[JUDGE] "New" catalyst test — a name must show a genuinely new capex,

reading conference calls/investor presentations — not computable from

[JUDGE] Post-screen research protocol — conference call, press release, DRHP

[SOFT] Volume/accumulation screen: "your average 10 day volume is 1.5 times
higher than the last 20 days of volume" (FESTF 01:01:08), which he elsewhere

series (which the ladder already fetches for RSI/ADX) but not currently used
this way.

[SOFT] Relative strength vs Nifty at a 52-week (or 26-week) high — his
single highest-conviction screen, framed as fundamentally meaningful because
"a stock follows a earnings road trajectory" (FESTF 01:14:39), so
outperformance implies superior earnings growth (FESTF 01:15:36). Computable
from price-series ratio to an index series (which the ladder does
not currently fetch), and explicitly NOT the same thing as the ladder's G8
weekly-RSI gate (FESTF 01:45:10).

[SOFT] Institutional/promoter ownership change (FII%, DII%, promoter% change
over 1yr/1qtr, thresholds ranging 0.5%-3% in his live demo) as a distinct
screening lens (FESTF 01:27:58-01:34:25). Needs shareholding-pattern data the
ladder does not fetch.

[JUDGE] Real vs fake/cyclical growth trigger — must be checked via
conference call before trusting a screen hit; example given is IndiaMart,

specifically "markets are concerned about churn rates and renewal rates"

quality.

[JUDGE] Sector-level relative strength / rotation — comparing a custom
sector index's outperformance vs Nifty, used to find which sector to hunt in
before picking individual names (FESTF 01:59:04-02:00:19). No company-level
gate captures this; it is a pre-screen step, not a per-company one.

## 4. WHAT THE LADDER MISSES

(a) Rules the ladder encoded that this lecture conditions or qualifies:

- capital_efficiency_gate-001 (G3, ROCE >= 15%, `requires_attribute:
  {is_lender: "false"}`): this lecture corroborates the 15% number but states
  a SECOND carve-out the ladder's `requires_attribute` does not encode at
  all: "ROC criteria can be removed from some people where the company is
  inflecting from the turnaround" (FESTF 00:42:09). The ladder only exempts
  lenders — it has no notion of exempting a turnaround company from the ROCE
  gate, so this lecture directly conditions capital_efficiency_gate-001 with
  a scope the current rule lacks.
- canslim_sales-001 / canslim_pat-001 (G0, sales >=15% / PAT >=20%,
  `requires_attribute: {}`): this lecture reaffirms the exact numbers but
  never presents them as a standalone independent pair — every time he
  states them he immediately adds ROC>=15% and market-cap>=1000cr as the
  SAME combined query (FESTF 00:23:47, 00:42:02-00:42:15). The ladder gates sales and
  PAT growth with no market-cap floor at all; this lecture's own screen would
  never surface a sub-1000cr name regardless of growth.
- pe_context-001 (observation, 15-35 band, `ref: null`): this lecture
  supplies an actual sourced quote this observation currently lacks (its
  provenance ref is null) — but the number it gives, "between 15 to 30
  times" (FESTF 00:33:51), is narrower than the ladder's stated 15-35 band.
  Worth reconciling: is 30 or 35 the intended upper bound.

(b) Central points this lecture makes that the ladder has NO rule for at all:

- The "new" catalyst/rate-of-change test (new capex/product/geography/
  client/segment/management/acquisition) as a required companion to numeric
  growth — arguably the single most repeated idea in the whole lecture
  (FESTF 00:05:44, 00:07:19, 00:45:33) — has no ladder analogue whatsoever.
- The mandatory post-screen research step (conference call / press release /
  DRHP / YouTube management interview / AGM) that follows every single
  worked example in this lecture — the ladder's pipeline ends at a gate
  verdict and has no concept of anything downstream.
- Market-cap floor (>=1000cr default), paired with an explicit caution below
  100cr that "The businesses have very negative cash loads or poor business
  models" (FESTF 00:42:15) — entirely absent from the ladder.
- Volume/accumulation screening (10-day-avg-vol >= 1.5x 20-day-avg-vol) — no
  ladder rule, despite OHLCV data likely being on hand.
- Relative-strength-vs-index screening at 52-week highs (the "blue dot
  list," FESTF 01:11:00, his single most emphasized tool) — no ladder rule.
  Note specifically
  that this is NOT the same signal as the ladder's existing G8 gate
  (weekly RSI >=50, weekly ADX >=20, sourced from a different lecture,
  HOWB): this lecture explicitly warns listeners not to conflate relative
  strength with RSI (FESTF 01:45:10), so an implementer should not assume
  G8 already covers this idea.
- Institutional/promoter ownership-change screening (FII/DII/promoter %
  change) — no ladder rule; needs shareholding-pattern data not currently
  fetched.
- Sector-level relative-strength rotation, done BEFORE individual-company
  screening — the ladder only ever evaluates one company at a time with no
  sector-relative context.

(c) Thresholds that read as one-off illustrative numbers rather than a fixed
universal bar: the specific FII/DII/promoter ownership-change percentages
(3%, then relaxed to 2%, then 1%, then 0.5%) are visibly being live-tweaked
on screener purely to control how many names the query returns ("we can
relax a little more" / "let's do a" — FESTF 01:28:01-01:33:42 region) — none
of them is presented as a fixed bar the way the 15%/20%/15%/1000cr screen is.
Treating any single one of these percentages as a universal threshold would
misrepresent what he actually did on screen.

If a check were being written against this lecture alone, the growth-plus-
market-cap-plus-ROC combined screen (00:23:47 / 00:42:02-00:42:15) and the "new"
qualitative test would be the two load-bearing additions; everything else
(volume, relative strength, ownership change) is presented as a separate,
optional idea-generation channel rather than a gate a candidate must clear.
