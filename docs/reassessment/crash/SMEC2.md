## 1. CRUX

This lecture is trying to change how the student assigns a multiple to a
business — teaching a per-sector "which valuation metric applies and what
level is expensive" map (P/E, PEG, EV/EBITDA, price-to-cash-flow,
price-to-sales, SOTP), so the student stops asking "is this cheap" in the
abstract and starts asking "cheap on the right yardstick, for this kind of
business."

## 2. MECHANISM

- Re-rating and de-rating are driven by whether earnings turn from volatile
  to consistent (re-rating) or consistent to volatile / falling (de-rating);
  the instructor states plainly: "whenever a volatile asset class turns into
  a consistent asset class, the maximum amount of re-rating happens over
  here" (SMEC2 00:33:47-00:34:03).
- No single multiple works for every business — the correct metric is
  selected by business shape: asset-heavy → EV/EBITDA; multi-segment
  conglomerate → SOTP; cyclical with volatile margins → price-to-sales or
  price-to-book instead of P/E (P/E is called a "misleading metric" at the
  bottom of a cycle, SMEC2 02:31:17); platform/exchange businesses →
  volume growth is the real driver, not a valuation multiple at all;
  contractor/EPC businesses → P/E, price-to-cash-flow, or market-cap-to-order-book.
- SOTP works by valuing each business segment on its own multiple (peers/EV-EBITDA)
  and summing — "problem well defined is a problem half solved" (SMEC2
  01:40:01-01:40:03) — then comparing the implied multiple on the residual/core
  segment to what peers get, to see if the market's assumption is realistic.
- PEG ratio functions as a growth-adjusted valuation check: divide P/E by the
  EPS growth rate, computable three ways (trailing, quarterly, forward)
  (SMEC2 01:05:19-01:05:50); paying too much PEG on entry means the buyer
  needs the growth AND a re-rating both to work out, so overpaying is framed
  explicitly as inviting trouble.
- The instructor closes the lecture with an explicit, sector-general
  valuation checklist as a takeaway, distinct from mid-lecture single-name
  observations, explicitly framed with the words `"Our framework is"` when
  he states the analogous price-to-cash-flow bar earlier in the same lecture
  (SMEC2 02:25:15) — signalling these are standing rules he applies, not
  one-off single-company remarks.

## 4. WHAT THE LADDER MISSES

(a) Rules the ladder encoded that this lecture conditions/qualifies:

- `peg_ratio-001` (F38, `<= 1.5`) is directly conditioned by two different,
  looser numbers stated in this lecture: a mid-lecture general ceiling of
  `2x` PEG scoped as "for any businesses apart from the consumer sector"
  (SMEC2 01:07:54-01:08:07), and the closing checklist's range "Below 1.5-2
  times PEG" (SMEC2 03:01:29-03:01:45). The existing rule's hard `<=1.5`
  bar is the tight end of a range this instructor states elsewhere as
  going up to 2x — and critically, the existing rule has no sector carve-out
  for consumer businesses, while this lecture explicitly treats consumer
  as a different (implicitly looser) bucket. This is exactly the kind of
  scoping gap G6 needs.
- `pe_context-001` (observation, whose display_text reads against a 15-to-35
  reference band, no gate) is corroborated on the upper end — this lecture
  repeats a 35x P/E ceiling three separate ways
  (00:49:04, 00:50:24-00:50:30, 03:01:29-03:01:45) — but the lecture never
  states a 15x floor; the lower bound in the existing note appears to come
  from a different source. Worth flagging that the 35x ceiling is now
  triple-sourced within this lecture alone, strengthening it as a G6 rule
  candidate, separate from any lower-bound claim.

(b) Central points this lecture makes that the ladder has NO rule for at all:

- G6 (valuation) is completely empty, and this lecture is essentially a
  worked syllabus for exactly that gate: explicit numeric ceilings for PEG
  (1.5-2x), price-to-sales (3x), P/E (35x), price-to-cash-flow (22-23x), and
  EV/EBITDA (20-22x) — all five stated together in one closing sentence
  (SMEC2 03:01:29-03:01:45). None of these five appear
  in the current rulebook except the loose PE observation and the tighter
  PEG rule discussed above. Price-to-sales, price-to-cash-flow, and
  EV/EBITDA have zero ladder presence whatsoever.
- The lecture's central organizing idea — that the CORRECT valuation metric
  depends on business type (asset-heavy → EV/EBITDA, conglomerate →
  SOTP, cyclical → price-to-sales/book, platform/exchange → volume growth,
  EPC → order-book/cash-flow, real estate → NAV) — has no ladder analogue
  at all. The ladder currently applies one blended screener P/E to every
  company uniformly (`pe_context-001`), which this lecture would call a
  category error for cyclicals, hospitals-vs-general, real estate,
  exchanges, and EPC names alike.
- Sector-specific EV/EBITDA sub-bands (hospitals ~20-22x reasonable/30x
  expensive; cement ~17-21x) are stated as distinct from the general 20-22x
  band and would silently misclassify a hospital or cement name if the
  ladder ever added only the general band.
- The "remove one-off costs before computing P/E" practice (SMEC2
  01:11:01-01:11:08) has no ladder equivalent — the ladder's screener P/E
  is presumably raw/unadjusted.

(c) Dated, one-company worked examples that must NOT be treated as universal
