LECTURE: 29.12.24 Class 5 — Research a Stock from Scratch    REF: RSSER    (transcript: 167100 chars, 1598 lines, covered: yes)

## 1. CRUX

This lecture is not a screen — it is the instructor teaching the entire
downstream research process a stock enters AFTER it clears a screen: build
ROE/ROC intuition, then work through four sequential checklists (fundamentals
→ management → technicals → valuation) with live worked case studies, so a
student stops treating a screener hit as a buy signal and starts treating it
as a research candidate. "Alright, so we will start the webinar with scratch,
step by step, investing process and investing checklist." (RSSER 00:08:07)

## 2. MECHANISM

- He builds ROE/ROC as the causal foundation before the checklist, because
  every later checklist item (margins, asset turns, leverage, cash
  conversion) is explained as one of the levers that move these two ratios,
  not as an independent metric: "The first place where whenever there is an
  improvement in the business or in the industry, the first place where it
  will have an effect is on the return on equity for a business." (RSSER
  01:01:08)
- ROE is decomposed via DuPont into exactly three levers, and every
  fundamentals-checklist item downstream is framed as which lever it moves:
  "ROE has 3 parts If ROE is improving or not improving in any company, then
  it is only for 3 reasons" (RSSER 00:21:20) — "the net profit margins"
  (RSSER 00:21:50), "asset turnover" (RSSER 00:22:00), and "assets upon
  equity" (RSSER 00:22:07, i.e. financial leverage).
- The checklist itself is explicitly four parallel tracks, run together, not
  a single pass/fail funnel: "fundamentals make checklist, technicals make
  checklist, management checklists and valuations make checklist." (RSSER
  01:36:35)
- Numeric thresholds are treated as starting points to investigate further,
  not hard cutoffs — e.g. on the D/E>1x rule he immediately softens it: "We
  do read the company at that time, but we think a little bit about the
  business" (RSSER 01:39:00).
- Valuation is explicitly NOT a static band check; it is a scenario model
  (bear/base/bull forward PE × forward PAT estimate built from management
  guidance and con-calls) cross-checked against a technical confirmation,
  demonstrated end-to-end on three real names (PG Electroplast, IIFL
  Securities/Capital, Alkyl Amines).
- A checklist is framed as a discipline device against human forgetfulness,
  not a scoring formula: "No matter how much an expert you are, a
  well-designed checklist can improve outcomes." (RSSER 01:33:24)

## 4. WHAT THE LADDER MISSES

(a) Qualifies capital_efficiency_gate-001 (ROC/ROE >= 15%, G3). This rule's
own provenance quote is cited to timestamp 01:36:54-01:36:57 in the
CONTEXT.md rulebook — that is THIS SAME LECTURE, so the >=15% bar itself is
already correctly captured, not a miss. But the instructor pairs ROC AND ROE
together in the same breath ("ROC and ROE above 15%", RSSER 01:36:57) where
the rule only encodes ROCE as the metric, and he immediately adds an
improving-trajectory escape clause the boolean rule doesn't encode: "if not
above 15% can it go above 15%" (RSSER 01:36:57) — i.e. a company below 15%
but visibly heading toward it is treated differently from one flat or
declining below 15%, a distinction the pass/fail gate cannot make.

(b) Qualifies leverage-001 (D/E <= 0.7, G1, hard gate). This lecture states
a different, softer number for the SAME concept in the SAME checklist —
"more than one time" (i.e. 1.0x, RSSER 01:38:56) triggers caution, not
rejection: "We do read the company at that time, but we think a little bit
about the business" (RSSER 01:39:00). The ladder's 0.7x is a hard binary
gate; this lecture's 1.0x is explicitly a soften-but-keep-reading threshold
from the same instructor in the same course. Both numbers are real and
sourced, but they are not the same rule — a company between 0.7x and 1.0x
D/E would be hard-REJECTed by leverage-001 while this lecture says to keep
reading it "cautiously."

(c) Qualifies peg_ratio-001 (PEG <= 1.5) and pe_context-001 (PE 15-35 band).
This lecture explicitly overrides both for durable multi-year growth
stories: "we are lenient with valuation if growth runways multi-year in such
cases rely on technicals for confirmation." (RSSER 02:51:39) The rules as
written have no growth-durability escape hatch and no notion of
technical-confirmation-in-lieu-of-valuation-discipline.

(d) No rule for margin-volatility-based rejection at all (b-type gap). This
lecture states a company with a long history of volatile margins is a
pre-screen disqualifier from even studying it (RSSER 01:37:22, 01:37:28) —
distinct from, and prior to, any threshold check. G2 (forensic veto) is
empty and this doesn't map to a forensic issue either; it is its own gap.

(e) No rule for promoter shareholding change at all (b-type gap). The
mTAR Technologies example (RSSER 01:48:51, 01:49:53) is a genuine stated
disqualifying pattern — promoter selling into a headwind — and nothing in
the 16 rules or the observations reads promoter holding trend.

(f) No rule for relative strength vs Nifty (b-type gap, see SIGNALS above).
This is explicitly distinguished from RSI and is a standing pass/reject
checklist item (RSSER 02:21:27) with zero counterpart.

(g) No rule for monthly-RSI-as-topping-signal (b-type gap). Distinct from
the ladder's existing weekly entry_rsi-001 gate; demonstrated specifically
and currently-relevant on Asian Paints (see SIGNALS and section 6 below).

(h) No rule for management legal/criminal history, related-party-transaction
complexity, dividend/capital-allocation behaviour (Avanti Feeds amber flag:
large cash/investment balance with no special payout), or moat-durability
discounting for policy-dependent ROCE (Borosil Renewables). These are the
entire "management checklist" and parts of "fundamentals checklist" the
lecture spends roughly 40 minutes on, and G2 (forensic veto) has zero rules,
so none of it has any counterpart in the ladder today. Most of these are
genuinely SOFT/JUDGE (need annual reports, con-calls, ChatGPT/Perplexity
research, Value Pickr threads) rather than screener-computable, so they are
correctly out of scope for a HARD gate — but they are exactly the kind of
disqualifying judgement G2 was designed to eventually hold, and this lecture
is the first source in this pass to state one outright (the criminal-case
disqualifier, RSSER 01:56:56).

(i) The bull/bear/base scenario valuation methodology itself (build a 3-4yr
forward PAT estimate from management guidance/con-calls, apply distinct
bear/base/bull forward PE multiples, compare implied market cap to current
price) is the instructor's actual valuation process, worked three times in
full (PG Electroplast, IIFL Securities/Capital, Alkyl Amines). G6
(valuation) is entirely empty in the ladder; pe_context-001 and
peg_ratio-001 are static single-point observations, not scenario models.
This is irreducibly JUDGE — it depends on reading con-calls and picking
scenario multiples — so it is not proposable as a gate, but it is the
central "what happens after the screen" content this lecture exists to
teach, and the ladder has no concept of it at all.

(j) One dated, one-off number should NOT be treated as a bar: "I am taking
the company at 11.5 times per ratio" [digits possibly ASR-garbled — could
be a PEG range rather than a PE multiple] (RSSER 02:52:10), stated only as
"today, because we are in the okay market" — an explicitly time-bound,
market-regime-conditioned calibration, not a universal threshold.
