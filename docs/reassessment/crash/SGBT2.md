## 1. CRUX

This is not a screen calibration lecture — it teaches how to find the sales/PAT growth number BEFORE you trust it: where structural growth triggers originate (industry super-cycles, corporate actions, product-mix change, capex, M&A, promoter change, demergers) so the investor can tell apart durable growth from a number that will pass a screen this quarter and reverse next quarter (SGBT2 00:24:40-00:25:34, 00:32:07-00:32:37).

## 2. MECHANISM

- Start top-down: industry-level "variant perception" triggers — super-cycles/structural demand-supply imbalance, new industry creation, regulatory change, industry consolidation — set the ceiling on how long a growth rate can persist (SGBT2 00:00:06-00:01:24, 00:05:05-00:05:14).
- Bottom-up, screen for the symptom first: run the buoyant-growth/"Explosive Growth" quarterly screens (>20% PAT growth) and a separate top-line-acceleration screen (>15% growth) purely to know "where to fish" — the screen is the starting point of research, not the verdict (SGBT2 00:18:10-00:18:19, 00:24:40-00:24:52, 00:54:02-00:54:05, 01:07:56-01:08:02).
- Attribute the growth to a specific trigger before trusting it: corporate actions (debt refinancing/deleveraging cuts interest expense and inflates PAT), capex (three ranked forms — greenfield, brownfield, debottlenecking), M&A, product-mix/premiumization, promoter change, backward integration, or demerger (SGBT2 00:05:35-00:05:41, 00:44:19-00:44:31, 00:51:36).
- Read the con-call/investor-presentation text to test whether management itself frames the driver as structural ("sustainably increasing," "structural in nature") or a one-off ("peak quarter") — this is the load-bearing quality-of-growth test and it cannot be done from ratios alone (SGBT2 00:31:48-00:31:54, 00:32:01-00:32:13).
- Match the comparison window to the business's seasonality: QoQ for seasonal industries (hotels, gold loans), YoY for non-seasonal ones (IT services) — using the wrong window misreads the growth number (SGBT2 00:27:28-00:28:14).
- Treat a base-effect reversal as the standard failure mode: an abnormally strong prior year (elevated base, "peak" quarter/order book) makes next year's YoY growth number collapse or turn negative even for a business nothing structurally wrong with it, and the same base effect can inflate this year's number off a depressed prior year (SGBT2 00:17:27-00:17:44, 00:32:01-00:32:37).

## 4. WHAT THE LADDER MISSES

(a) The ladder's canslim_sales-001 and canslim_pat-001 gates apply a flat >=15%/>=20% quarterly YoY threshold with `requires_attribute: {}` (no scope at all). SGBT2 directly conditions the SAME two numbers on comparison-window choice: "Shashank, sometimes companies are seasonal." (SGBT2 00:27:28), giving the hotel industry as the worked example — "the strongest quarter in the hotel industry is your Q3 quarter, now Q3 quarter will be compared with Q3, not Q2" (SGBT2 00:27:39) — against the explicit contrast case, "Now like IT services industry, there is not so much seasonality in IT services industry." (SGBT2 00:28:10), where the instructor says YoY is used instead: "So now we will see year on year, right?" (SGBT2 00:28:08). A flat YoY-only gate can misclassify a seasonal business's genuinely strong quarter, or fail to flag an IT-services company where YoY is the wrong lens to begin with. This is a real qualification the ladder has no rule for.

(b) A central point of this lecture — that a passing PAT-growth number can be entirely deleveraging-driven (falling interest cost, not rising revenue/margins) — has NO corresponding rule anywhere in the 16-entry rulebook. The ladder's G0 gate cannot distinguish a company clearing 20% PAT growth via genuine operating improvement from one clearing it purely because interest expense fell (the lecture's own worked examples: Usha Martin 74cr→27cr interest cost, Bellrise Industries 89cr→56cr YoY, at SGBT2 00:06:23-00:06:49). This is exactly the headline-number-vs-quality-of-growth gap the assignment asked to check for, and it is real and unaddressed.

(c) Similarly, acquisition-led (inorganic) growth inflating the headline sales/PAT number has no ladder rule at all — the lecture treats it as a standing category to check for (SGBT2 00:18:38-00:18:49, 00:58:50-00:59:11).

(d) On the specific claim the assignment flagged (that a CANSLIM-style screen fails often in a sideways market, per a different lecture) — SGBT2 makes NO such statement. It never characterizes market regime (sideways/trending) as a condition under which the growth screen fails or misleads. Its qualifications are all about comparison-window/seasonality and growth-quality attribution, not market regime. Do not attribute that claim to this REF.

(e) The lecture explicitly frames growth screening as step 1 of research, never a final verdict: "that is the idea that at least you can use this screen to observe pad growth If you come to observe pad growth, you will go and study it Then you will see whether it can last or not" (SGBT2 00:24:40; transcript has no internal punctuation in this run-on block). This matches the ladder's own architecture (G0 gate feeds into further gates, not a standalone buy signal) — so on THIS point the ladder is not missing anything; it's consistent with the instructor's stated design. Saying so plainly per the brief's instructions.

(f) No dated/one-company number in this lecture is being smuggled into the rulebook as a universal bar — the 15%/20% figures given here are stated as reusable screen settings: "So this is the screen which I use." (SGBT2 00:19:43), "At least every quarter, refresh this screen." (SGBT2 00:19:45) — not a single-company calibration. The Acutaas 25%/28-30% figures (SGBT2 00:31:05) and PG Electroplast's 95%→14% swing (SGBT2 00:17:27-00:17:44) are company-specific worked examples and must NOT be read as thresholds — they aren't currently in the rulebook, so there's no risk of that happening, but flagging it since a future pass might be tempted to mine them as bars.
