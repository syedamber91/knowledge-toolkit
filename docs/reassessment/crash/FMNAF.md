## 1. CRUX

The lecture is not actually a financial-modelling-mechanics lesson (no bull/base/bear
build, no IRR, no return hurdle appears anywhere in this transcript) — its real
argument is that the *choice of valuation ratio itself is sector-conditional*, and an
investor must diagnose which regime (market environment) and which business type
(bank / cyclical / grower / loss-maker) they are looking at before any multiple, DCF,
or scenario number can mean anything (FMNAF 00:46:24-00:47:00, FMNAF 01:01:26-01:01:42).

## 2. MECHANISM

- Start from the market environment (liquidity, currency stability, seasonality):
  bull markets pay up for growth/positivity and bear markets punish any one-off miss,
  so the same fundamentals draw a different multiple depending on regime
  (FMNAF 00:00:40-00:02:13, FMNAF 00:09:03-00:09:23).
- For banks, throw out P/E; use price-to-book driven by an ROA tree — net interest
  income + other income → OPEX → PPOP → provisions → PBT → PAT, then ROA × leverage =
  ROE — and track GNPA (loans/advances unpaid 90+ days) and cost-to-income as the
  levers that move ROE and therefore the P/B re-rating (FMNAF 00:26:45-00:26:53,
  FMNAF 00:35:41-00:36:04, FMNAF 00:31:15-00:33:04).
- For autos, P/E tracks market-share trend (and product mix/premiumization), not
  absolute growth — a share-gaining company re-rates, a share-losing one de-rates
  (FMNAF 00:42:04-00:42:11, FMNAF 00:44:32-00:44:40).
- For cyclicals/commodities, P/E is actively misleading — it goes to a high multiple
  right when earnings collapse at the trough — so use P/B or EV/EBITDA instead, and
  read the margin cycle (near a historical peak vs. near a historical trough) as the
  real diagnostic (FMNAF 00:49:12-00:49:38, FMNAF 00:51:50-00:52:06).
- For loss-making/no-clear-profitability-path businesses, no valuation applies at all
  — instructor invokes a Buffett anecdote about failing students who tried to value an
  unprofitable internet business (FMNAF 00:47:44-00:48:23).
- Corporate actions (mergers) get a separate accretion check: compare the merging
  entities' own P/E and EBITDA margin against the listed vehicle's, check the swap
  ratio, and see how much PAT/EPS the deal adds (FMNAF 01:05:41-01:06:21).
- The lecture explicitly sets up "bull bear based scenario" analysis as the next step
  (SJS, Manorama, Gravita India, done live from concalls) but the transcript **cuts off
  at 01:08:30** before that build is shown — no scenario math, no IRR, no hurdle rate,
  and no discussion of checking management's guidance track record appears anywhere in
  this file.

## 3. SIGNALS

[JUDGE] P/E is not a universal metric — it is a good tool only for mature, stable,
predictable-earnings companies and for like-for-like peer comparison; it actively
misleads for cyclicals (earnings evaporate), is a poor tool for high-growth
pre-profit companies, and is unusable for loss-makers.
Evidence: "in mature and stable companies, it's an excellent tool to use P-Ratio
because earnings are consistent, predictable" / "Cyclicly, in industries, PE ratios
cannot be used because earnings evaporate" (FMNAF 00:46:35-00:46:55).

[SOFT] Bank valuation runs on P/B + ROE + ROA (via an ROA tree), not P/E; GNPA
(90+-day overdue loans) trend and cost-to-income trend are the two levers to watch.
Evidence: "GNPA basically refers to total value of all loans and advances that has
stopped generating income for a bank meaning borrowers which haven't paid principal
or interest for 90 days or more" (FMNAF 00:35:41). Illustrated with SBI/ICICI Bank
(re-rating as ROE/ROA improved, FMNAF 00:25:20-00:26:30), Equitas SFB and CSB Bank
(de-rating as OPEX/provisions blew up, FMNAF 00:29:37-00:34:25), South Indian Bank
and Ujjivan SFB (re-rating as GNPA fell, FMNAF 00:36:54-00:38:21).

[JUDGE] A bank's ROE "more than 14-15% mark" is offered as roughly where a bank is
"doing well" — stated once, in passing, not built out as a screening bar.
"If a bank is doing well, then the net worth or the return on equity of a bank will
be more than 14-15% mark" (FMNAF 00:29:00).

[SOFT] Auto/ancillary businesses are valued primarily on market-share trend (and
premiumization/value-per-vehicle), which the market translates directly into P/E
re-rating or de-rating.
Evidence: "one thing which matters is the most is market share" (FMNAF 00:42:04);
Mahindra & Mahindra's P/E "reared from 13 times to almost all the way till today is
at 26 times" as it gained SUV market share (FMNAF 00:44:32); the hypothetical

losing market share" (FMNAF 00:42:52).

[JUDGE] For cyclicals, valuation comfort is read off P/B or EV/EBITDA at cycle
extremes, cross-checked against the margin cycle (is the company near a historical
peak or trough margin).
Evidence, Tata Steel: "whenever EV upon EBITDA has gone to less than 6 times, then
value comfort emerges" (FMNAF 00:52:00); "P/E ratio was 183" at the 2024 earnings

peak margins is a value trap, not a bargain.

[SOFT] AMC (asset-management) valuation tracks market-share trend and product mix
