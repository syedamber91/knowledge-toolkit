## 1. CRUX

This lecture is trying to replace a flat "cheap P/E = buy" instinct with a two-engine model of stock returns (price = P/E x EPS, and both legs move independently), paired with the insistence that the multiple itself is sector-specific rather than a single universal number — so "is this expensive" becomes a judgement about which metric applies and what will move it, not a lookup against one band.

## 2. MECHANISM

- Price is nothing but P/E times EPS, and the biggest returns ("double engine") happen when both legs move together — earnings growth AND a re-rating multiple — while chasing only one leg produces value traps (cheap but not growing) or growth traps (growing but overpaying on the assumption growth never ends): "You buy a stock because it is a cheap stock." / "You are not buying a stock because it is growing." (BVB 00:03:56 / 00:03:59).
- The P/E leg itself swings on a re-rating/de-rating cycle driven by named qualitative catalysts — re-rating: "operating leverage margin expansion management change sectoral tailwinds" (BVB 00:29:12), plus product-mix shift, narrative shift, de-leveraging, demand-supply mismatch; de-rating (the mirror set): "operating delevage pricing erosion, margin erosion demand de-escalation, sectoral headwinds bad capital allocation, oversupply denting the balance sheet for growth, corporate governance issues" (BVB 00:36:16) — the ratio moves because of a story, not on its own.
- His own working P/E range is explicitly personal and earnings-conditional, not a claimed law: "in my case I have taken PEMF between 15-35 if you get good earnings then it works well" [likely "PE band"] (BVB 00:07:58) — and P/E is not even the right tool for every business: NBFCs go to price-to-book, insurers to embedded value, hospitals to EV/EBITDA (BVB 00:48:52-00:49:37, detailed under SIGNALS).
- Whether an individual stock re-rates at all is gated by the broad market environment first — index-level normal-GDP growth and a 200-day-moving-average breadth "fear gauge" set the odds before any stock-specific story matters (BVB 00:19:16, 00:20:07).
- His personal portfolio rule ties the entry multiple directly to required growth via PEG, and states the ceiling as a range conditioned on how rich the entry multiple already is, not a flat number: "if I am buying a company with 50PEP then I have to make sure that earnings growth is around 35% to 40% so that the peg ratio of the company remains below almost 1.5 to 1.75 times below" (BVB 00:32:26).
