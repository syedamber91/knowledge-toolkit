# CRASH-SYNTHESIS — Crash Course reassessment (25 lectures, 4 modules)

Sources: 25 reader briefs in `briefs/`, all quote-gate-passed. Claims below cite (REF timestamp) via those briefs.

## 1. The through-line, and where the modules disagree

Across all four modules the method is one pipeline: **numeric screen → attribute the growth to a named trigger → read the con-call → technical confirmation of entry → valuation as an avoidance check → exit on thesis-break or technical breakdown**. The ladder encodes only the first step and half of the fourth. Everything the instructor treats as the *hard* part — trigger attribution, growth quality, exit — sits downstream of where the ladder stops; a screen hit is a research candidate, not a verdict (FESTF 00:32:50; SGBT2 00:24:40).

The modules were recorded years apart and the method drifts three ways:

- **Bottom-up → top-down.** The 2024 mastering-fundamental module is screen-first ("build your own screens out of 5000 names", FESTF). The 2025-26 TVGP module inverts this: "Stock picking is later" (SGSTS 00:00:35) and "if we catch the right theme, then stock picking is overrated" (SGSTS 01:14:22). The ladder implements only the older, bottom-up half; the newer material says sector choice dominates it.
- **Hard bars → conditioned bars.** Older material states clean numbers (D/E 0.7, ROCE 15, PE 15-30). The later material re-states the same numbers with scope conditions: D/E >1.0x is "treat cautiously", not reject (RSSER 01:38:56); the ROCE bar gets a turnaround carve-out (FESTF 00:42:09) and a trajectory escape ("if not above 15% can it go above 15%", RSSER 01:36:57); the PE band becomes "my personal habit, for differentiated names, growth-matched" (VALU2 01:13:02, BVB 00:07:58).
- **Valuation as a band → valuation as avoidance.** The dedicated valuation lectures explicitly reject the flat-band frame the ladder's `pe_context-001` embodies: "valuation should not be looked at in terms of P multiples" (VALUV 00:28:42); "valuation is a tool of avoidance rather than a tool of filtering stocks" (VALUV 00:45:02).

## 2. TVGP reconstructed — and what the corpus actually contains

**There is no four-part TVGP framework in the two lectures named TVGP.** TVGP is documented as Theme / Valuation / Growth / Promoter, but TVGPT (part 1) covers only Theme, and TVGP2 (part 2) is *also* Theme — monsoon-frog/cyclical-trap detection, the five-moat test, stage analysis, screening tools — and explicitly defers the rest: "the growth triggers class next Sunday" (TVGP2 01:16:32) and "the valuation and promoter will have to look at it separately" (TVGP2 01:17:53). Reading the module's dates, the deferrals were delivered as separate lectures: **G** = SGBTS/SGBT2 (28.12.25) plus SGSTS (20.12.25), **V** = VALUV (18.01.26), VALU2/BVB (25.01.26), plus FMNAF/FMNA2 (financial modelling, 01.02.26). **P (Promoter) has no dedicated lecture anywhere in this corpus** — the nearest content is scattered (DSFDO/PIWEP promoter-incentive material, RSSER management checklist).

No single enumerated "TVGP checklist" exists in the transcripts. The checklists that do exist, item by item:

- **FSNAF 7-point balance sheet** (02:00:11-02:00:33): strong/weak overall; capex expanding; working-capital situation; asset-light vs heavy; cash on balance sheet; debt burden (D/E < 0.7 "healthy", 01:52:26); strengthening or weakening over time. **Income-statement 7** (02:37:20-02:37:47): sales growth speed; gross margin; EBITDA margin; earnings growth recent/past quarters; interest vs EBITDA; depreciation trend; which segment drives growth.
- **FESTF combined screen** (00:23:47): quarterly sales >15% AND PAT >20% AND ROC >15% AND market cap >1000cr, optionally PE <40 — stated as ONE query, with the ROC leg removable for turnarounds.
- **RSSER four parallel tracks** (01:36:35): fundamentals, management, technicals, valuations — run together, thresholds as starting points, not cutoffs.
- **SMEC2 closing valuation checklist** (03:01:29-03:01:45): PEG <1.5-2x, P/S <3x, PE <35x, P/CF <22-23x, EV/EBITDA <20-22x — five ceilings in one sentence.
- **SESCS buying setup** (03:32:51): weekly RSI >50 AND RS >0 vs Nifty 500 AND ADX >20 AND rising 30-week EMA — four legs.

**Side-by-side against the ladder's 16 entries.** Matches: canslim 15/20 (FESTF is the MASTEC source; independently corroborated SGBT2 00:54:05/00:18:19), ROCE≥15, D/E 0.7 number, RSI/ADX legs, CFO bands, PEG≤1.5. Dropped by the ladder: the market-cap leg (stated in the same breath as the growth legs, every time); promoter pledge = 0 (FMFDF 02:13:27); ROE≥15 as a *separate* leg alongside ROCE (FMFDF, RSSER pairs "ROC and ROE"); the RS-vs-Nifty-500 and rising-30-week-EMA legs of the buying setup; the turnaround/trajectory carve-outs on ROCE; every exit rule; the "new catalyst" requirement; the entire sector stage. Added that he never says: nothing invented outright, but `pe_context-001`'s flat, sector-agnostic band contradicts his own scoping (below).

**`growth_trap_flag-001` — provenance defect, report to rulebook owner.** The rule's ref is `TVGPF 00:18:39-00:19:07`. TVGP2's reader grep-confirmed that timestamp range does not exist in that transcript (markers jump 00:18:33 → 00:18:48; the content there is Ather going private, not growth traps), and TVGPT's reader confirmed the quote is absent from part 1 — greps for "growth trap", "30-50", "supernormal" return zero in both. The `provenance.quote` also reads as polished written prose, unlike the ASR speech everywhere else — suggesting it may be the rulebook author's paraphrase rather than transcript text. **Genuine growth-trap content does exist in this corpus, at different places with different numbers:** "anything above 30-35p, if it is not growing, it is a potential that it might end up becoming a growth trap" (VALU2 01:01:08 — 30-35x, not 30-50x), "excessive P multiple is more than 50X" (VALUV 00:24:22), and the base-effect mechanism nearly verbatim to the rule's display text at SGBTS 00:18:30-00:19:23. None matches the quoted wording; the citation needs re-sourcing, not guessing.

## 3. What the ladder misses, ranked

1. **G6 (valuation) is empty while the corpus contains its best-corroborated rule material.** [HARD, gate-shaped] Two ceilings recur across independent lectures: **PE > 50 as an alpha-destroying zone** — a claimed ~4,000-instance historical study, "buying richly valued stock doesn't work especially of P 50 and above", worsening to ~-8% 3-yr alpha above 75x (VDVUV 00:22:05-00:22:11), echoed as ">50X excessive" (VALUV 00:24:22) — and **PEG ≤ 1.5-2x** (SMEC2 03:01:29; VDVUV 00:19:29 "the hard rule should be 1.5, you can go 2 times only when you are looking at a consumer business"; BVB 1.5-1.75 conditioned on entry multiple). These are real PASS/FAIL-shaped bars, not judgement calls — *but only with sector scoping*: P/E is declared unusable unadjusted for banks/real-estate/insurers (SMECS 02:17:50), misleading for deep cyclicals (SMEC2 02:31:17, FMNAF), and wrong when earnings contain one-offs or pre-monetized capex (SMECS 02:18:39). The ladder has `is_lender` scoping for D/E and ROCE but none on any PE-based entry. Six lectures independently demand that carve-out.
2. **No exit rule despite four computable triggers stated as rules.** [HARD] From SESCS: 10-week EMA break for peak-momentum names (02:35:39); price >80% above the 200-day EMA (02:39:45 — corroborated at 70-80% in DSFDO 00:04:59, self-contradicted numbers there); 30-week EMA breakdown + Volatility Stop(10,2) negative, "the ultimate way to exit" (03:15:42); weekly/monthly RS crossing below zero (01:41:47). Plus monthly RSI <50 as a multi-year-top signal (RSSER 02:23:32 — demonstrated live on ASIANPAINT). The shortlist already carries an ExitTriggers column, but no rule consumes it.
3. **The entry gate is an incomplete rendering of the instructor's own setup.** [HARD] G8 encodes RSI≥50 + ADX≥20 but omits the third and fourth stated legs: relative strength > 0 vs Nifty 500 and rising 30-week EMA (SESCS 03:32:51). RS-vs-index is separately his "holy grail" screen (FESTF 01:11:03) and a standing pass/delete checklist item (RSSER 02:21:27), and both FESTF and RSSER explicitly warn RS ≠ RSI. Computable from price series plus an index series.
4. **PAT growth quality — the G0 gate cannot tell real growth from three fake kinds.** Deleveraging-driven (falling interest cost inflates PAT with no operating improvement — SGBT2 00:06:23, worked on USHAMART: interest 74cr→27cr); acquisition-led/inorganic (SGBT2 00:18:38); tax-rate normalization (FSNAF 02:31:46); plus one-off spikes markets punish (SGBTS 01:00:37) and seasonality making YoY the wrong window for some industries (SGBT2 00:27:28). Interest-expense trend is screener-fetchable — a "PAT passed while interest cost fell sharply" flag is [HARD]; the rest is [SOFT].
5. **Promoter layer absent entirely.** Pledge = 0 [HARD, fetchable, stated as a screen leg — FMFDF 02:13:27]; promoter-holding trend and selling-into-headwinds [SOFT — RSSER 01:48:51 treats it as disqualifying; SESCS 00:46:56 flags a 10% one-shot sale]; fresh warrants as conviction signal [SOFT — FSNAF, PIWEP, TFMLT]. The behavioral module's one screenable output is exactly this: "always look at promoter holding and always look at where the incentives are" (DSFDO 00:28:06).
6. **Market-cap band.** [HARD, fetchable] "best returns are made in businesses between 1 to 15,000 crore market cap" — restated identically across four screens (FMFDF 02:13:27 etc.), floor of 1000cr in FESTF. No ladder rule; several 38-names sit far outside it. **Flag: this is a dated calibration** (as is TVGPT's ₹5-7k-cr "sweet spot", self-described as an 11-year observation) — encode as an observation with vintage, not a bar.
7. **Sector dimension.** [mostly JUDGE] The TVGP module's core claim — same growth number means opposite things in different sectors (SRF best-in-weak-sector 5-6% CAGR vs "PNR" okay-in-strong-sector 3-4x, SGSTS 00:39:31-47) — is encodable only at the margins: the **peer-breadth check** ("5 companies... 4 getting good profit → the sector is working", SGSTS 01:14:50) is computable from data the ladder already fetches *if* a sector-to-company mapping is added [SOFT]; sector RS and custom indices need index data [SOFT]; theme durability, moats, policy triggers are irreducibly [JUDGE]. Honest answer: a sector-relative context column is feasible; a sector *gate* is not, on current data.
8. **G2 (forensic veto): the corpus argues it should stay nearly empty — with one narrow exception.** JFSNJ, the dedicated cross-statement lecture, frames every red flag as an open question, "never as 'reject the company if X'" (reader's finding); CFSHC's own worked example (NeoGen at ~9% CFO/PAT) is treated as "will need external financing", not disqualified. The disaster pattern he narrates is a *combination* — multi-year ~zero CFO **plus** exploding debt **plus** receivable write-offs/negative reserves (Bartronics/Opto Circuit, CFSHC 02:03:49-02:04:16) — so a single-ratio veto would be unfaithful. RSSER supplies the only outright stated disqualifiers in 25 lectures: a criminal case against management (01:56:56), promoter selling into headwinds (01:49:53), volatile-margins-refuse-to-study (01:37:22), 90%-down-for-years broken trust (01:47:33) — all [JUDGE] or needing unfetched data. **Recommendation:** the authors' empty-G2 was right for single ratios; the defensible addition is a *compound* flag (3-yr CFO/PAT far below the B2B/B2C floor AND rising debt AND negative/deteriorating reserves; debtor-days trend as an early-warning observation — currently entirely unused despite being standard screener data).
9. **Rule qualifications on existing entries:** leverage-001 hard-fails at 0.7x where RSSER treats 0.7-1.0x as caution-and-keep-reading; cfo_to_pat-001's blended 80-95 band conflicts with the twice-stated B2B 60-70 / B2C 80-90 split (JFSNJ 02:04:16, CFSHC 02:04:16) — and the near-identity of those numbers to the CFO/EBITDA 60/70 split suggests cross-metric conflation in the corpus worth flagging; capex_expansion-001's "no stated preference" is contradicted by FMFDF ("the expansion is happening only then will the growth come", 02:29:17) and complicated by TFMLT (idle gross block + capex = bullish latent operating leverage); capital_efficiency_gate-001 lacks the turnaround and trajectory carve-outs.

## 4. Contradictions (not smoothed)

- **D/E:** 0.7x hard gate (ladder, FSNAF "healthy" descriptor) vs 1.0x soften-and-study (RSSER 01:38:56) — same instructor, same course family. A 0.85x company is REJECT-shaped in the ladder and "read it cautiously" in the lecture.
- **PE band:** 15-30 "where I start getting very excited" (FESTF 00:33:51) vs 15-35 personal, moat-and-growth-conditional (VALU2 01:13:02, BVB 00:07:58) vs **30-40x as the alpha sweet spot** in the statistical study (VDVUV 00:22:34) vs "reading PE as a level is the wrong frame" (VALUV 00:28:42). The ladder's 15-35 band (whose `ref` is null) is one voice among four, and the strongest-evidenced number in the corpus (the >50 ceiling) is a *ceiling*, not a band.
- **PEG ceiling:** 1.5 hard, 2.0 consumer-only (VDVUV 00:19:29) vs 2.0 for everything *except* consumer (SMEC2 01:07:54) — the carve-out points in opposite directions across two lectures — vs 1.5-1.75 tied to entry multiple (BVB 00:32:26) vs "lenient with valuation if growth runways multi-year, rely on technicals" (RSSER 02:51:39).
- **Growth-trap direction:** the ladder's only trap flag fires on high PE; SWCTS's APL case (15x → 65x) warns low multiples aren't safety, and VALU2's base rate — ~10-11 of 500 low-PE stocks beat the index over 3-5 years (01:12:14) — says cheap-without-growth is the *more common* trap. The ladder has no low-PE-plus-low-growth flag.
- **DCF:** `pe_context-001`'s provenance quote implies DCF yields intrinsic value; the instructor of the DCF lecture says three times he doesn't use DCF to value anything (VDVUV 01:49:38, 02:20:57, 02:39:38).
- **Screen-first vs sector-first** (Section 1) — a genuine method drift over time, not reconcilable as stated.
- **Valuation's own weight:** "valuations matter is 80 to 85 percent of the times true" (SMECS 01:15:16); doesn't matter over 12-18 months, matters over 2-3 years (VALUV 00:50:04). Any encoded G6 rule is knowingly an ~80%-true rule; the corpus never specifies the exception set.

## 5. Named-company evidence — his revealed criteria

Pooling ~200 named examples: his positive worked examples are overwhelmingly **₹1,000-15,000cr companies with one nameable trigger** — deleveraging (Usha Martin, in five lectures), capex-to-operating-leverage (Shilchar, Deepak Nitrite, Sandhar, PG Electroplast), new product/category (Eicher, Varun Beverages, Monster), management change (Kilburn), regulation-window (Venus Pipes, always with a mean-reversion warning). Entry multiples in praised cases cluster at 10-35x with growth matching. His negative set: quality-at-any-price (the 2018-19 ~50x basket that lagged the Nifty, VDVUV), cyclical upswings mistaken for moats (graphite, Borosil, Polyplex), governance (Rajesh Exports, Vakrangee, Kaynes). Revealed and stated criteria match closely, with one gap: **the 38-name shortlist barely overlaps his exemplar universe.** His model companies (Shilchar, RACL, SJS, Venus Pipes, KEI, Deepak Nitrite…) are mostly absent from the 38, while the 38 contains exactly the mega-cap 50x+ quality names his negative case studies are built from.
