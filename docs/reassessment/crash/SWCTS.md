## 1. CRUX

This lecture is trying to change one decision: stop letting reflexive, narrative-driven System 1 panic (or euphoria) trigger buy/sell decisions, and instead force every market judgment -- especially valuation, which the instructor insists can never be precise -- through slow, deliberate System 2 reasoning.

## 2. MECHANISM

## 3. SIGNALS

- [JUDGE] Macro/geopolitical panic narratives (war, elections, bank-crisis contagion, visa-policy fears) are not, by themselves, actionable sell signals -- evidenced by the instructor's whole opening list of narratives that did not play out as feared (SWCTS 00:00:00-00:02:14).
- [JUDGE] News coverage volume/urgency is not a proxy for informational content, because outlets have a structural incentive to manufacture urgency: "the work of the news is not to cover anything." (SWCTS 00:03:43). Not something a screener can compute.
- [JUDGE] Portfolio-level returns are inherently lumpy/non-linear even over long (10-15 year) horizons -- an expectations-management claim, not a testable metric: "no no market always goes up market they will always have these different realities of life it will always be drops and peaks and ups and downs" (SWCTS 00:10:31).
- [JUDGE] Precise point-valuation is impossible; valuation should be treated as a range/approximation, not a single number: "It is 50 60% art and only 40 50% science." (SWCTS 00:19:56). This is a stance on method, not a computable check.
- [SOFT] A stock's PE can re-rate far beyond what looks statistically "expensive" (e.g. beyond "5X of retail businesses" standard deviation, SWCTS 00:17:25) when demand/momentum is strong -- the instructor frames this as a reason NOT to mechanically flag "expensive" PE as a sell signal, which directly qualifies how any PE-band check should be read. Checkable only with sector-relative PE distribution history, which the ladder does not currently compute.
- [JUDGE] Sector selection should be done bottoms-up, at a local/sub-market level (e.g. Delhi vs Mumbai real estate, not "pan India"), because macro conditions diverge within a sector (SWCTS 00:24:33-00:25:39: "I don't want to get trapped in a pan India real estate," "I want to see Delhi based developers," "I want to see Mumbai based developers because the price appreciation is the highest in both areas"). Not computable from screener.in ratios -- needs geographic/sub-market classification data the ladder doesn't fetch.
- [JUDGE] System 1 (fast, automatic) reasoning systematically fails on exponential/compounding processes -- demonstrated by the lily-pad riddle, where most people wrongly assume linear progress: "So on 47th day it will be 50% covered and on the 48th day it will be 100% covered" (SWCTS 00:22:06). This is a general cognitive-bias teaching point (the human tendency to underestimate compounding), not a company-specific or computable rule.
