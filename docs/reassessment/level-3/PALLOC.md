## 1. CRUX

This lecture is not about which stocks to buy — it teaches how much to put into each stock once it has already passed selection, and how to manage that sizing over time, so an investor can "you can take n number of risks as well Without risk of blowing up" (PALLOC 00:00:10) instead of accidentally concentrating a portfolio into ruin through mental biases and unmanaged position drift.

## 2. MECHANISM

- Anchor on portfolio-level returns, not individual-stock returns — the instructor frames endowment bias, status-quo bias, loss aversion, anchoring bias and the disposition effect (selling winners early, holding losers) as the psychological failure modes that wreck allocation discipline before any stock-picking skill even matters (PALLOC 00:12:49-00:16:34).
- Diversification benefit flatlines empirically: "The answer is 20 to 30 stocks" (PALLOC 00:47:29) — beyond that, "there is no advantage of owning more than 25 to 30 stocks when it comes to the idea of diversification" (PALLOC 00:47:41), and standard-deviation-of-risk case studies show the curve goes flat past that point (PALLOC 00:49:37-00:49:45).
- Sector concentration is capped as a rule of thumb: "in a given sector don't have more than 15 to 20% weightage" (PALLOC 00:48:31), stretchable to ~25% only for very high conviction (PALLOC 00:48:50-00:48:56).
- Four conviction-to-size frameworks, mapped to cricketer analogies, trade churn/diversification for concentration: 2/4/6/8% tiered ("Virat Kohli approach") with a satellite (2%, experimental, high-turnover) vs core (4-8%, validated) split (PALLOC 01:06:44-01:16:14); equi-weighted 4-5% × 25 names or 5-6% × 20 names ("Sachin Tendulkar approach") for investors who want conviction removed from sizing (PALLOC 01:18:22-01:20:22); 10% × 10 names ("Rohit Sharma approach") for closely-tracked high-conviction books (PALLOC 01:22:01-01:25:14); 20% × ~5 names (the Sehwag-style, hit-or-miss sizing posture, explicitly "not suited for many people") for maximal risk tolerance (PALLOC 01:24:59-01:26:49).
- Promotion/demotion between satellite (2%) and core (4-8%) is triggered by the thesis showing up in reported numbers, not narrative: a live example moved a "Kavach"-related holding from 2% to 4-5% the same day quarterly margins hit 32% on unutilized capacity coming online (PALLOC 01:08:39-01:09:20); conversely a satellite position with "no changes on the ground" over several quarters gets switched out entirely (PALLOC 01:10:03-01:10:05).
- Rebalancing has two triggers: a can-you-sleep-at-night comfort test — if a position's current-value weight has drifted far above cost-basis weight and "it is giving me sleepless nights", trim it back down, but not necessarily all the way if the story is still intact (PALLOC 01:40:13-01:40:37, 02:03:11-02:03:48); and a calendar cadence — quarterly review for 2%/satellite positions (high churn), half-yearly for core (4-8%) positions (PALLOC 02:02:06-02:04:11).
- Time stop loss: if the fundamental thesis is unchanged but the stock price goes nowhere for roughly 8 quarters (~24 months) during a bull market, that stagnation itself is treated as an objective signal to cut or reduce, specifically to avoid multi-year value traps (PALLOC 01:34:58-01:37:11).

## 4. WHAT THE LADDER MISSES

(a) Rules the ladder encoded that this lecture conditions or qualifies:

- `canslim_pat-001` (G0, `pat_growth_yoy_pct >= 20`) — the ladder gates a company out of the CANDIDATE pool if quarterly PAT growth misses 20%, no exception. This lecture explicitly walks through the case where PAT growth is suppressed purely by depreciation/interest during a capex ramp-up and states the opposite of exclusion — hold and even increase the position — because "business within itself will become stronger" and the horizon is multi-year (PALLOC 01:33:32-01:34:58). The ladder's G0 gate has no capex-cycle carve-out; this lecture says one should exist.
- `entry_rsi-001` / `entry_adx-001` (G8, weekly RSI≥50 and ADX≥20 for fresh entries) — the ladder treats these as unconditional entry gates. This lecture qualifies technical-trend entries as regime-dependent: in the sideways/choppy market it describes as the current environment, "technicals are not going to work much" and moving-average/trend systems produce false chop-outs (PALLOC 00:39:43-00:40:14, 01:28:23). The G8 gate has no market-regime context — it applies the same RSI/ADX bar in a trending market and a chopping one.

(b) Central points of this lecture with no rule in the ladder at all:

- Position sizing itself — the entire subject of the lecture (the 2/4/6/8%, equi-weight, 10-on-10, 20-on-20 frameworks) has zero counterpart in the ladder. The ladder answers whether a company passes; it never says how much capital should go into it — which this lecture argues is the actual determinant of portfolio-level return (PALLOC 00:50:34-00:51:33).
- Portfolio-size ceiling and sector-weight cap — the ladder currently surfaces 38 CANDIDATE+WATCH names with no upper bound and no sector-concentration check, while this lecture states diversification benefit is exhausted at 20-30 names (PALLOC 00:47:29-00:48:08) and caps any single sector at 15-20% (PALLOC 00:48:31). Given the 38-name output skews heavily to pharma/API names (AJANTPHARM, AUROPHARMA, DIVISLAB, EMCURE, GLAND, GLENMARK, GRANULES, IPCALAB, LAURUSLABS, NEULANDLAB — 10 of 38), this is a directly actionable gap, not a hypothetical one.
- Single-name regulatory/idiosyncratic concentration risk — CONTEXT.md confirms gate `G2` (forensic veto) is EMPTY: "no rule -> nothing can ever be REJECTED." This lecture's IEX/Old Bridge/PPFAS worked example (PALLOC 00:52:36-00:54:52) is exactly a G2-shaped concern — a clean-looking business whose allocation size, not its fundamentals, determined the damage from a regulatory shock — and the ladder has nothing there.
- Time stop loss — no rule anywhere checks whether price has gone nowhere for roughly N quarters despite the market being up (PALLOC 01:35:36-01:37:11); this is a distinct concept from the existing `entry_rsi`/`entry_adx` gates, which are entry-only and say nothing about a stagnating existing holding.
- Promoter/corporate-group track record as a core-vs-satellite differentiator (PALLOC 01:32:51-01:33:27) — this data (group track record, years of promoter history) is not fetched by the ladder at all.
- Rebalancing/position-drift discipline (the can-you-sleep-at-night trim rule and quarterly/half-yearly cadence, PALLOC 01:40:13-02:04:11) — this is post-entry portfolio management, a category the ladder does not address; it only gates entry and (via observations) some exit-adjacent context.
- The opening third of the lecture (endowment bias, loss aversion, anchoring, disposition effect, PALLOC 00:12:49-00:16:34) is investor psychology, not company-level signal — genuinely out of scope for a company screener and correctly has no ladder counterpart.

(c) Dated, one-company thresholds mistaken for a universal bar: none found. Every numeric threshold in this lecture (20-30 stocks, 15-20% sector cap, 2/4/6/8% tiers, 8-quarter time stop) is presented as a general portfolio-construction rule of thumb, not derived from or pinned to one company's specific numbers.
