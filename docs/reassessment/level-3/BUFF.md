LECTURE: How Buffett Actually Invests? Valuation Methods    REF: BUFF    (transcript: 151841 chars, 1548 lines, covered: yes)

## 1. CRUX

This lecture is trying to replace two separate failures at once — buying without any price discipline, and selling on emotion — by giving the student a complete, mechanical loop for "when to buy, how long to hold, and when to sell" that combines a from-scratch financial model, sector-appropriate valuation multiples, DCF/reverse-DCF, and three chart-based tools (ADX, Parabolic SAR, Volatility Stop), so that a stock's exit is decided in advance rather than in the moment of panic or greed.

## 2. MECHANISM

- Buffett's own method is explicitly NOT a DCF: "our analysis is very similar to which we use in buying entire businesses" (BUFF 00:04:41) — "We first have to decide whether we can sensibly estimate an earnings range for 5 years out or more" (BUFF 00:04:58), and buy only "if it sells at a reasonable price in relation to the bottom boundary of our estimate" (BUFF 00:05:05); outside that circle of competence, "We have never forgotten an attractive purchase because of macro or political environment or the views of the other people" (BUFF 00:06:18) — i.e. decline, don't force a view.
- Build a bottom-up PNL model directly from management's own guidance (worked live on Neo-Gen Chemicals: capex, EBITDA margin history, depreciation schedule, guided debt/equity, blended tax rate) rather than from a generic template — the model gets re-run every time guidance changes (BUFF 00:07:00-00:36:46).
- Three chart tools split the buy/hold/sell decision into independent, rule-based jobs: ADX confirms trend strength — "If the ADX of a stock is more than 20 to 25, then means the stock is in a positive uptrend" (BUFF 00:41:20); Parabolic SAR times entries — "you have to buy when it starts trending and your ADX is above 20" (BUFF 00:49:25); Volatility Stop is exit-only — "volatility stock is purely and purely meant for exits" (BUFF 00:43:35) [likely "volatility stop"].
- Valuation multiples are sector- and value-chain-specific, not a universal PE band: cement is priced on EV/ton because "Cement business is a business of high debt and high cash flow generation" (BUFF 00:58:12), and "never ever look at price to earnings ratio, especially when it comes to your cement sector" (BUFF 00:58:20); agrochemical PE is set by position in the value chain (patented/MNC vs. generic/domestic), not growth alone.
- Conglomerates and holding companies need Sum-Of-The-Parts valuation, not a blended PE: "There is a conglomerate discount that is given to ITC precisely because of this" (BUFF 01:15:20) — segment EBIT mix drives the multiple, and a holding-company discount (Kama Holdings vs. SRF) can itself be the trade.
- DCF/reverse-DCF: intrinsic value is PMT/(rate−growth) for a growing perpetuity, discount rate is opportunity cost not "science fiction" cost-of-capital math — "I take 10% as the cost of capital or 10% as the discount rate" (BUFF 01:44:38) — and terminal value dominates: "That is 55 to 60% of a company's valuations" while "discount rates and growth in cash flows only account for the remaining 35 to 40%" (BUFF 01:46:42, BUFF 01:46:49). Reverse DCF inverts this: "instead of forecasting the growth, what if we can invert the DCF and actually get what is the expected growth built into this company at this point of time" (BUFF 02:15:25) — if the implied growth rate is unrealistic, that itself is a sell/avoid signal, executed by tightening the Volatility Stop multiplier.

- [JUDGE] Position sizing should scale with conviction, not be flat across a portfolio: "should get should get only 2% allocations or lower because you're seeing the business is emerging" (BUFF 02:24:57) for low-conviction names, up to 8% for highest-conviction ones, held to roughly "total miss 17 to 19 companies" (BUFF 02:28:58) [likely "total is 17 to 19 companies"] for diversification. ## 4. WHAT THE LADDER MISSES

(a) Rules the ladder encoded that this lecture conditions or qualifies:
- `pe_context-001` (stock PE read against a flat 15-35 band, ref f10-pe-band) is directly qualified twice over. First, by sector: "never ever look at price to earnings ratio, especially when it comes to your cement sector" (BUFF 00:58:20) — the ladder has no per-sector carve-out, so a cement name would get scored against a metric this lecture says is the wrong one to use at all. Second, by moat/value-chain position within a sector: routine "good" businesses sit far outside 15-35x — Nestle "78.2p" (BUFF 01:13:11), HUL "61p" (BUFF 01:13:05), Bayer Crop Science "35 to 40 xp ratio" despite low growth purely on brand stickiness (BUFF 01:04:35). None of the current 38 are cement names, so this doesn't flip a verdict today, but it is a structural gap in how pe_context-001 is framed as a universal observation.
- G8's entry-only technical gate (`entry_rsi-001`, `entry_adx-001`) is corroborated on the ADX side by this lecture's independent explanation of the same >20-25 threshold (BUFF 00:41:20), but this lecture's real technical content is the exit side — Parabolic SAR (entry timing) and Volatility Stop (exit/hold-duration) — which G8 has no equivalent of at all. See (b).

(b) Central points this lecture makes that the ladder has no rule for at all:
- G6 (valuation) is explicitly empty per CONTEXT.md ("no rule"), and this entire lecture is the SOIC method's own answer to exactly that gate: build a financial model, discount it (DCF), and cross-check the implied growth rate against the price (reverse DCF). None of it is in the ladder — not the DCF mechanics, not the reverse-DCF check, not the 50%-margin-of-safety bar (BUFF 02:13:01), not the terminal-value-dominance framing (BUFF 01:46:42). This is the single biggest gap the lecture surfaces: the ladder's most valuation-relevant course has contributed zero rules to the valuation gate.
- Volatility Stop and Parabolic SAR are a complete, independently-taught when-to-sell/how-long-to-hold system (BUFF 00:43:35, 00:49:25) that the ladder does not implement anywhere. The 38-shortlist table carries an "ExitTriggers" column with nonzero counts for a few names (HINDCOPPER=1, NATIONALUM=2, SPLPETRO=2), but no rule in the 16-entry rulebook defines what an exit trigger is or cites this lecture — meaning either an undocumented rule exists elsewhere, or the ladder is silently missing the one lecture that teaches a concrete, chartable exit rule.
- Sector-specific valuation metrics (EV/ton for cement, SOTP for conglomerates, holding-company discount for parent/subsidiary pairs) have no equivalent anywhere in the ladder, which only ever reads flat ratios (PE, PEG, ROCE, D/E) off screener.in.
- Position-sizing by conviction tier (2/4/6/8% or 3/6/9/12%, capped at "17 to 19 companies", BUFF 02:28:58) is outside the ladder's per-stock CANDIDATE/WATCH/REJECT scope, but it is worth flagging that the ladder currently hands back 25 CANDIDATE names with no allocation guidance at all, which this lecture treats as inseparable from the valuation call itself.

(c) A caution for future rule-mining from this lecture, not a finding against the current ladder: this lecture's own numbers (10% discount rate, 4% terminal growth for "faster growing" companies vs. 2-3% for mature ones, a 5% aggressive terminal growth used specifically for Asian Paints, a "50p" exit-multiple ceiling the instructor says applies to maybe "one or two companies... out of 5000 or 6000" (BUFF 02:22:00-02:22:20, paraphrased)) are all explicitly contextual choices tied to prevailing bond yields and company maturity, not universal constants. If a future pass mines this lecture for a G6 rule, hardcoding "10% discount / 4% terminal growth" as a fixed threshold would misapply the lecture's central point that these inputs move with the interest-rate regime and the specific company.
