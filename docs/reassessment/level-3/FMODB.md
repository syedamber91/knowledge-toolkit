## 1. CRUX

This lecture is not about screening — it is about how to price a company once you already like it: derive a forward bull/bear/base valuation by (a) picking the *right kind of multiple for the sector* (never a universal PE), (b) building a simple 4-5-line forward P&L (sales, EBITDA margin, other income, interest, depreciation, tax), and (c) sanity-checking the result against peers and PEG — explicitly instead of DCF.

## 2. MECHANISM

- No universal multiple exists across sectors or even across segments of the same company — the same rupee of EBITDA is priced differently depending on growth, capital intensity and "the time it possesses" (samosa vs. coffee-shop example, "[00:00:44] Coffee shop gets a PE of 20 times, Samosa gets a PE of 10 times"; SRF chemical peer Navin Fluorine trades at "46 times" EV/EBITDA and "84-85 times" PE vs. its packaging-film peer Polyplex at "4.7 times" EV/EBITDA (FMODB 00:07:56-00:08:08)).
- DCF is deliberately avoided: "in DCF, you get a false sense of superiority" (FMODB 00:02:37) — "you should reverse DCF" instead, i.e. back out what growth/margin the current price is already assuming.
- Multi-segment companies (SRF: chemicals, packaging film, technical textile; the Raymond-like "Raven Life" [likely "Raymond"] garmenting/suiting/apparel example) require sum-of-the-parts (SOTP) valuation, and the lecture's own maxim is "market rewards simplicity in the business structures" (FMODB 00:06:00) — more segments, harder to value, generally lower multiple.
- Capital-intensity determines the metric, not just the number: asset-heavy, high-depreciation businesses (hospitals) should be valued on EV/EBITDA, not PE, because "In hospitals business depreciation is overstated" (FMODB 00:20:08) — the KKR/HCG buyout is walked through live and lands at "22.2 times EBITDA" (FMODB 00:26:31), cross-checked against a peer set of listed hospital chains.
- For lenders, PE is not the operative metric at all — leverage ("The business of the company is almost 10x leveraged," FMODB 00:42:14) means ROE = ROA × leverage, and book value growth (a function of sustained ROE and asset quality) is what the market actually prices; "If a bank is unable to earn 14-15% ROE then it will never be able to earn other cycles so the bank will never get the valuation of the bank" (FMODB 00:45:09).
- Forward modeling itself is deliberately simple and conservative: forecast sales off management/industry growth guidance, EBITDA margin off stated guidance, depreciation off the annual report's asset-life note, and interest cost off a conservative (worst-case) incremental-debt assumption — "roughly right is better than precisely wrong" (FMODB 01:45:16) — then apply a bull/base/bear forward PE band to forward PAT, and sanity-check with PEG ("below 1.5 times peg is also interesting," FMODB 01:46:57). The balance sheet itself is explicitly NOT modeled: "it's a waste of time to model the balance sheet ... You just have to model the cash flow, CFO to EBIT and PNL" (FMODB 01:52:37-01:52:44).

## 3. SIGNALS

`[JUDGE] Sector/segment-relative multiples are not comparable across sectors — the same EBITDA rupee is priced 2x differently by growth/scalability.` Coffee shop (higher growth, harder to scale) gets 20x PE vs. samosa's 10x PE despite lower EBITDA margin (FMODB 00:00:44-00:01:53).

`[JUDGE] Asset-heavy/high-depreciation sectors should be valued on EV/EBITDA, not PE, because depreciation there is a non-cash expense that overstates as a % of true economics.` "In hospitals business depreciation is overstated" (FMODB 00:20:08); the industry-standard KKR/HCG deal price of "22.2 times EBITDA" is used as the reference multiple, not the reported PE, which is called out as misleading due to "116 impact" (Ind AS 116 lease accounting) (FMODB 00:26:31, 00:27:41-00:28:04).

`[HARD] A multi-quarter run of decelerating sales growth is associated with PE derating.` Illustrated with an "avenue of supermarkets"-type example (numbers garbled — "313 times"/"123 times" [likely decimal-point ASR errors, true multiples uncertain]) and with Asian Paints, whose PE fell from a peak of "119" to roughly "46 45 50" as growth flattened for "7 quarters in a row" after a new competitor's capacity came online (FMODB 00:34:22-00:37:33).

`[SOFT] Large new competitor capacity entering a category is a forward risk to incumbent margins/multiple, and is only visible in the entrant's own investor communications, not in the incumbent's screener ratios.` The Asian Paints example: a new entrant put up capacity "nearly 60 to 65% of Asian paints capacity in one go" (FMODB 01:14:52-01:16:00); similarly a ~₹1800cr-capex new entrant into cables/wires is walked through via its own conference-call commercialization timeline (FMODB 01:13:02-01:14:40).

`[SOFT] Trailing/TTM PAT growth (or its absence) can be an artifact of a one-off item and needs normalizing before it is trusted.` NATCO's PE looked cheap ("8 times") in one narrative but the prior year's earnings included a one-off drug-exclusivity windfall that had since rolled off; the instructor manually annualizes the "true" run-rate quarter ("132 crores per quarter") to get a normalized PAT before judging the multiple (FMODB 00:39:07-00:40:16).

`[JUDGE] Lenders (banks/NBFCs) should be valued on ROE/book-value growth trend, not PE — PE is not treated as the operative metric for this business model at all.` "the bank will never get the valuation of the bank" if it can't sustain 14-15% ROE (FMODB 00:45:09); HDFC Bank cited as the positive contrast ("their book value is always growing by 15-20%," FMODB 00:45:59-00:46:00) vs. IDFC First ("growth in their equity is only by fundraising," FMODB 00:46:05-00:46:11).

`[HARD] For a lender, ROE = ROA × financial leverage (leverage ≈ assets/equity), and both are computable from standard balance-sheet line items.` Worked numerically in the Siddhant-bank toy example: 10x leverage, 1.8% ROA → 18% ROE (FMODB 00:42:14-00:43:22).

`[SOFT] Multi-segment/conglomerate companies need sum-of-the-parts (SOTP) valuation; a single blended company-level multiple or ratio can misrepresent them.` SRF (chemicals / packaging film / technical textile, three different peer multiples) and the Raymond-like garmenting/suiting/apparel example are both walked through as SOTP cases (FMODB 00:03:59-00:09:25).

`[JUDGE] PEG below roughly 1.5x is a useful sanity check on a valuation, per Peter Lynch.` "below 1.5 times peg is also interesting" (FMODB 01:46:57) — this already exists in the ladder as `peg_ratio-001`; corroborating, not new.

`[SOFT] Depreciation/asset-life assumptions for forward modeling should come from the company's own annual-report fixed-asset note, not a flat guess.` RR Kabel's schedule (annual report page 183) gives buildings a 30-year life, plant & machinery 3-15 years, used to derive a ~10%-of-fixed-assets depreciation run-rate (FMODB 01:21:56-01:22:14).

`[SOFT] Conservative modeling convention: size incremental debt as (planned capex − 3-year average operating cash flow), not an optimistic full-accrual-funded assumption.` Applied to RR Kabel's ₹1450cr capex vs. ~₹400-500cr average annual CFO (FMODB 01:18:06-01:18:45); framed generally as "roughly right is better than precisely wrong" (FMODB 01:45:16).

`[JUDGE] Do not model the balance sheet at all — only P&L and cash flow are worth forecasting.` "it's a waste of time to model the balance sheet ... You just have to model the cash flow, CFO to EBIT and PNL" (FMODB 01:52:37-01:52:44).

## 4. WHAT THE LADDER MISSES

**(b) — G6 (valuation) is completely empty, and this is the valuation lecture.** Per CONTEXT.md, "G6 = valuation (no rule)." This entire lecture — sector-relative multiple selection, EV/EBITDA-vs-PE-vs-P/B choice by business model, SOTP for multi-segment companies, bull/bear/base forward-PAT scenario modeling, and the PEG sanity check — is exactly the content that would populate G6, and none of it is encoded anywhere in the 16-entry rulebook. The ladder currently has zero mechanism to say what a company is *worth*; it only screens quality/momentum gates (G0/G1/G3/G8) and a flat PE observation. This is the single largest gap this lecture exposes.

**(a) — `pe_context-001` (f10-pe-band, a flat 15-35 PE reference band applied to every company) is directly conditioned by this lecture's central thesis: multiples are not comparable across sectors, and for some business models PE is not the right metric at all.** The lecture states "market rewards simplicity in the business structures" (FMODB 00:06:00) and shows a chemical peer trading at "84-85 times" PE against a packaging-film peer at single-digit EV/EBITDA (FMODB 00:07:56-00:08:08) purely because of sector, not quality. More sharply: it explicitly says hospitals should be read on EV/EBITDA because "In hospitals business depreciation is overstated" (FMODB 00:20:08), and that lenders should be read on ROE/book-value growth because "the bank will never get the valuation of the bank" on PE terms alone (FMODB 00:45:09). `pe_context-001` has no `requires_attribute` scoping (unlike `capital_efficiency_gate-001` and `leverage-001`, which both correctly exclude `is_lender`) — so a bank in the 38-name universe would currently be read against a flat 15-35 PE band that this lecture says is the wrong metric for that business model entirely.

**(a) — `canslim_pat-001` (f25-canslim-pat-growth, raw `pat_growth_yoy_pct >= 20`) is qualified by the lecture's normalized-earnings warning.** The NATCO case shows that a raw YoY PAT growth number (positive or negative) can be an artifact of a one-off item rolling in or out of the comparison base, and that the instructor's practice is to manually annualize a "true" run-rate quarter before trusting the number (FMODB 00:39:07-00:40:16). The ladder's G0 gate takes `pat_growth_yoy_pct` at face value with no normalization step — this lecture is a direct warning that doing so can flag a company as failing (or passing) G0 on a number that isn't representative of its underlying earnings power.

**(b) — no rule anywhere handles multi-segment/conglomerate companies.** The ladder computes ROCE, D/E, and PE at the consolidated-company level for every ticker. This lecture's SOTP treatment of SRF (and the Raymond-like example) says a blended company-level ratio can misrepresent a conglomerate entirely — a segment with 40%+ margins and a segment with single-digit margins wash out into one number that describes neither (FMODB 00:03:59-00:09:25). None of the 38 shortlisted names are obviously multi-segment conglomerates by name, but the ladder has no mechanism to even flag when this caveat applies.

**(b) — no rule anywhere handles forward-looking competitive-capacity risk.** The Asian Paints case (a new entrant standing up "60 to 65%" of Asian Paints' own capacity in one go, FMODB 01:14:52-01:16:00) is presented as a first-order valuation input — the market had already de-rated the stock's PE from 119x to ~45-50x partly on this basis before the new capacity even commercialized. This is unavoidably a SOFT signal (only visible in the entrant's own conference calls/capex announcements, never in the incumbent's screener ratios), but it is worth naming explicitly as a category the ladder cannot see at all, not merely a missing threshold.

**(b) — lenders pass through the ladder with essentially no fundamentals screen.** `leverage-001` and `capital_efficiency_gate-001` both correctly exclude `is_lender`, which is right per this lecture (D/E and ROCE are meaningless for a bank) — but nothing replaces them. This lecture's entire banking section is built around ROE, book-value-growth trend, and leverage-adjusted ROA/ROE as the actual valuation drivers for a lender (FMODB 00:41:00-00:52:34) and none of that exists in the ladder as either a gate or an observation. A lender in the universe currently reaches CANDIDATE on G0 (sales/PAT growth) and G8 (RSI/ADX) alone, with G1 and G3 silently skipped and no substitute check in their place — exactly the blind spot this lecture's ROE/book-value framework was built to fill.
