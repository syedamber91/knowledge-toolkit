# L3 SYNTHESIS — What Level 3 says the ladder cannot

Sources: 10 verified briefs in `out/reassess_l3/briefs/` (all ≥80% verbatim-quote-verified). Citations below are the readers' citations; every quote traces to a transcript timestamp.

## 1. The through-line of Level 3

The ladder automates "what to buy." Level 3's collective argument is that **"what to buy" is the part of the method that cannot stand alone** — the course exists because "a lot of retail investors learn what to buy" but "they do not know when to buy and thirdly they do not know when to sell" (VINTRO 00:00:08-00:00:12). The ten lectures then build the four layers the ladder does not have:

1. **What it's worth** — and the answer is never one number. The multiple is perception ("It depends on industry growth… sentiments… liquidity", FMODA 00:28:05-00:28:18), it is sector-specific by construction, and it is dynamic across the capital cycle (ODDM). Valuation is done by picking the sector's load-bearing variable first and the multiple second (VACRA, VACRB), by forward modelling off management guidance with bull/base/bear exit multiples (FMODA, FMODB), and by reverse-DCF rather than DCF ("in DCF, you get a false sense of superiority", FMODB 00:02:37).
2. **When to buy** — starting valuations dominate multi-year returns ("It's not a bad business, but it was a bad stock" — Infosys at 206x, ODDM 01:07:16), and the safe entry is a company *under-earning* below its own margin band, while the danger zone is "peak margins plus peak P ratios" (ARTBV 01:54:13).
3. **How much** — position sizing (2/4/6/8 tiers, core/satellite, 20-30 stock ceiling, 15-20% sector cap) is framed as the actual determinant of portfolio outcome: "it is not about being right It is about how much money you make when you are right" (PALLOC 00:51:16).
4. **When to sell** — a mechanical exit layer (volatility stop, Parabolic SAR, reverse-DCF-tightened multipliers, 8-quarter time stop) decided in advance, "purely and purely meant for exits" (BUFF 00:43:35).

The unit of judgement throughout is the business and the price paid for it — never a scalar in isolation. Every threshold the ladder lifted from these lectures originally came wrapped in a sentence saying *which* businesses it applies to and *when*.

## 2. What the ladder misses, ranked

**M1 — `pe_context-001` (flat 15-35 PE band) is contradicted by five lectures independently, and not just in calibration — in polarity.** This is the single most wrong entry in the book. ARTBV: "the P ratio will not work in life insurance companies, will not work in banks, will not work in real estate companies" (ARTBV 00:40:20). BUFF: "never ever look at price to earnings ratio, especially when it comes to your cement sector" (BUFF 00:58:20), while quality FMCG *normally* sits far above the band (Nestle "78.2p", BUFF 01:13:11). VACRA: asset-heavy/high-depreciation and capex-phase businesses need EV/EBITDA (VACRA 00:15:27, 00:28:30); AMC/wealth PE is cycle-distorted. VACRB: real estate must not be valued on P&L multiples at all (VACRB 00:43:05-00:43:34), and in capital-markets cyclicals a PE near the band's *ceiling* is the **buy** signal ("when it goes to 30 to 32 PE ratio that is a very good signal to study the business well", VACRB 01:44:52-01:45:20). FMODB: hospitals on EV/EBITDA ("In hospitals business depreciation is overstated", FMODB 00:20:08), lenders on ROE/book-value. ODDM and FMODA further poison the band's *input*: headline PE must be stripped to "core" PE (one-off/other income out — MapmyIndia 66.4x headline vs far higher core, ODDM 01:45:58-01:46:26). Five contradicting lectures, two more attacking the input, zero defending the band. The evidence supports the lead in full. The band also fails empirically inside the ladder's own output: 27 of the 38 shortlisted names are marked P/E-ok = N, including names two lectures call normally-priced quality (NESTLEIND). Fix direction: scope it (per-sector attribute, as `leverage-001` already does with `is_lender`) or retire it; leaving it unscoped makes it wrong in both directions. HARD to scope; JUDGE to apply.

**M2 — The exit half of G8 is empty and three lectures teach the same computable exit rule.** ODDM, ARTBV and BUFF independently teach the monthly-candle ATR volatility stop (length 10, multiplier 2-2.5x, tightened toward 1.5-2x when reverse-DCF says the price is rich; "The time to sell is on the first red sign", ARTBV 01:58:29). It is computable from the same price series the ladder already fetches for RSI/ADX. **This is the one genuinely gate-shaped addition in all of Level 3**: consistent across three lectures, mechanical, backtested by the instructor on his own exits (Laurus 520-550 exit after an 8x ride, BUFF 00:50:44-00:51:41; Sequent exit at 232 before a fall to 80, ODDM 01:01:04-01:01:26). The one caveat is that the multiplier is horizon-dependent (3-3.5x for 5-15yr holders, ARTBV 00:36:26) — a parameter, not a universal constant. HARD. Note also the data anomaly: the 38-table already carries an `ExitTriggers` column (HINDCOPPER=1, NATIONALUM=2, SPLPETRO=2) that no rulebook entry defines or cites — either an undocumented rule exists or the column is orphaned (flagged by the BUFF reader).

**M3 — G0's PAT gate trusts a number four lectures say must be normalized first — in both directions.** One-off income inflates it: Aarti's PE 15x→32x purely on termination-income roll-off (ARTBV 00:13:33-00:14:35), NATCO's cheap PE was an exclusivity windfall (FMODB 00:39:07-00:40:16), Divi's growth was flagged by SOIC itself as one-molecule-driven (ARTBV 01:56:41-01:57:04). And capex suppresses it: a company mid-ramp "will not see any path growth [PAT] because interest, cost, depreciation will keep killing the profit and loss statement but even though business within itself will become stronger" (PALLOC 01:33:32) — the lecture's response is *hold or add*, the ladder's response is FAIL G0. So `canslim_pat-001` both admits dirty growth and rejects clean businesses. Full normalization is SOFT (concall/AR reading), but the ladder already fetches a partial HARD proxy: CFO/PAT and CFO/EBITDA — see M4.

**M4 — G2 (forensic veto) is empty, but one HARD rule is sitting in the ladder's own fetched data.** The honest assessment first: most of what the lectures offer G2 is not gate-shaped — "unforecastable business" flags (commodity oversupply/anti-dumping: "Now we cannot model such a company", FMODA 00:23:21), regulatory concentration (the IEX allocation lesson, PALLOC 00:52:40-00:54:52), key-person risk (ARTBV 01:24:03) all need data the ladder doesn't fetch and judgement it can't make. Leaving those out was defensible. But two items are computable today or nearly so: (i) **grossly failed cash conversion as a veto** — the ladder already computes 3-yr CFO/EBITDA and CFO/PAT and already knows SOIC's bars (60-70%, 80-95%); CPPLUS sits at CFO/EBITDA **-1.33%** and CFO/PAT **-48%** — reported profits backed by *negative* operating cash — and is a CANDIDATE because nothing can reject. A floor (e.g. CFO/EBITDA persistently near/below zero → veto) is HARD, provenance-grounded (MODULB), and would have fired here. (ii) **Float-cornering flag** — "whenever you see obscene valuations in companies always it's because of float cornering" (ARTBV 00:22:27); shareholding pattern is a standard screener field, not currently fetched. HARD-fetchable.

**M5 — G6 (valuation): mostly not gate-shaped, and the empty gate was partly the right call — but not entirely.** Sector-multiple selection, SOTP, forward modelling and reverse-DCF are irreducibly model-building exercises (SOFT/JUDGE); encoding "10% discount rate, 4% terminal growth" would violate BUFF's own warning that these move with the rate regime (BUFF section 4c). What *is* defensible: the **PEG ≤1.5x check is corroborated by four L3 lectures independently** (ODDM 01:56:09; BUFF 1.3x on Fluorochem, 01:10:12; FMODA 00:58:09; FMODB 01:46:57) — the best-attested valuation number in the entire corpus — but always conditioned on (a) a conservative/normalized growth denominator (ODDM 01:58:34) and (b) sector validity (meaningless for real estate, VACRB). Promoting PEG from observation to a *scoped* G6 gate (excluding real estate/lenders/negative-growth) is the maximum the lectures support. Anything beyond that would be manufacturing a gate.

**M6 — G3's point-in-time ROCE gate encodes the opposite of the entry-timing the lectures teach — and dropped a condition its own provenance carries.** The provenance quote is "ROC/ROE above 15% **or trending toward it**" (MASTED); the encoded check is `>= 15` point-in-time — the trend clause did not survive translation. Consequence: the ladder structurally *prefers* peak-economics companies (peak margins → high ROCE → PASS), which ARTBV names as the danger zone ("peak margins plus peak P ratios", ARTBV 01:54:13), and *demotes* under-earning recovery names, which ARTBV names as the margin-of-safety zone. This is visible in the output: the lecture-favoured names FLUOROCHEM (BUFF: PEG 1.3x, earmarked for research), CARTRADE (VACRA: anti-cyclical platform with margin inflection) and JUBLINGREA (ARTBV: "this is a business I will go and study deeply", 02:11:23) all sit at WATCH on G3 fails, while lecture-doubted peak-multiple names sit CANDIDATE.

**M7 — The sizing layer is entirely absent, and it is directly actionable at portfolio level.** PALLOC + VCONC + BUFF converge: 20-30 stocks maximum (PALLOC 00:47:29), 15-20% per sector (PALLOC 00:48:31), core (high ROC + track record + 3-4yr runway) vs satellite tiers (VCONC 00:04:02-00:04:29). The 38-name output has no ceiling and no sector check — and 10 of 38 are pharma/API names (~26%), already past the sector cap if bought as listed. A HARD portfolio-level check (count names, count sector weight, tag core-eligible vs satellite-only) needs no new data. Also PALLOC's 8-quarter time stop (01:35:36) is a computable holding-level rule with no counterpart.

**M8 — Lenders pass through with no substitute screen.** G1/G3 correctly exclude `is_lender` but nothing replaces them; FMODB's entire banking section (ROE ≥14-15% sustained, book-value growth, ROA × leverage — FMODB 00:45:09) is the missing content. No lender is in the current 38, so this is structural, not verdict-flipping today.

**M9 — Growth-quality (volume vs price) qualifies both G0 and `growth_durability-001`.** "the better quality of sales growth is volume growth" (ARTBV 01:30:53); a name can clear ≥15%/≥20% on price/mix alone (Marico's value-added volume at 5.4%, ARTBV 01:31:50). SOFT (volume data lives in presentations).

**M10 — G8's entry gate has no market-regime qualifier.** PALLOC: in the sideways market prevailing since Sept-24, "technicals are not going to work much here" (PALLOC 01:28:23) — weekly trend filters chop out. The RSI/ADX bar is applied identically in trending and chopping regimes. JUDGE/SOFT.

## 3. Contradictions

**DIVISLAB — three lectures, three claims; mostly dissolves, one residue.** VACRB praises the *business* (pipeline-justified premium, VACRB 00:08:07-00:08:16) — a structural claim. ARTBV doubts a specific *earnings print* (2022 molnupiravir one-off; SOIC's own exit ~4100, ARTBV 01:56:41-01:57:57) — a period claim. PALLOC warns about *position size* (FDA import ban, 1140→543 in six months, PALLOC 00:28:03-00:28:35) and its reader explicitly notes it is "not a doubt about DIVISLAB's current fundamentals." BUFF's "DVs" margins-collapsing remark (BUFF 01:29:56, low confidence) matches the same 2022 episode. So: good business ≠ good stock at every price ≠ safe at any size — three different axes, no timeless contradiction. **The residue the corpus does not resolve:** whether DIVISLAB's *current* 65.5% PAT print is clean recovery or base-effect off the post-one-off collapse. The lectures tell you exactly which question to ask; the ladder cannot ask it.

**LAURUSLABS — dissolves into the exact dimension the ladder lacks.** BUFF's cautionary tale is about the *price paid* ("a lot of people actually got stuck towards the peak", BUFF 00:01:17) and simultaneously shows the exit system worked (8x captured, exit 520-550). VACRA, recorded against the later depressed base, is about the *business trajectory* (CDMO 28% of revenue, +89% YoY, re-rating contingent on reaching 40-50% mix, VACRA 02:14:54). Same company, opposite verdicts, fully explained by cycle position and entry price — i.e., by G6 and the exit layer, both empty. The residue: today's row (PE 89.2, PEG 22.3, 3y sales growth 4%) looks like BUFF's setup, not VACRA's; and VACRA's tracking variable (CDMO mix) is invisible to the ladder.

**Corpus vs `growth_trap_flag-001` (PE<30 band).** FMODA treats KEI as a legitimate bull-case at 36-44x and catches Waaree's trap at 260x via a bear-case-model test, not a band (FMODA 00:54:59-00:56:32); VACRB makes ~30-32x a buy signal for CDSL. The lectures' actual trap test is "does even the bear case clear an acceptable return," which no static PE line encodes.

**PALLOC vs G0 and G8.** Direct conflicts, not nuances: PALLOC says hold/add through capex-suppressed PAT (G0 says FAIL) and distrust weekly technical filters in choppy regimes (G8 applies them unconditionally). The corpus does not resolve these; PALLOC simply operates at a horizon (3-4yr theses) the quarterly gates don't model.

**Nestle (apparent only).** BUFF uses 78x as a *normal* quality multiple; VACRA shows Nestle derating 30%+ on a growth stall (VACRA 01:07:02-01:07:43). Consistent once stated properly: the quality multiple is conditional on growth persisting — which is the growth-trap mechanism itself.

## 4. The named-company evidence

Pooling ~90 named examples: his **positives** share margin expansion in progress (Neuland, CCL, Vadilal), a nameable forward variable he can track (CDMO mix, order book quality, capacity commissioning, book-value growth), entry at reasonable PEG (~1-1.5x), and often *currently depressed* economics with thesis intact (Equitas, South Indian Bank, IIFL, Birla Corp at an operating loss). His **negatives** share peak-margin-plus-peak-multiple entries (Alkyl/Balaji Amines), one-off-inflated earnings (Aarti, NATCO, Divi's 2022), order-book decay (Tejas), and high starting valuations regardless of quality (Infosys 206x, Asian Paints, Pidilite, D-Mart).

The revealed criterion is **change and price** — inflections bought below their re-rating, not levels bought after it. The ladder screens levels. The starkest evidence: the overlap between the instructor's live positive/watch names across these lectures and the ladder's 26 CANDIDATEs is nearly empty, while several of his named research targets (FLUOROCHEM, CARTRADE, JUBLINGREA, Axis Bank, Equitas, Birla Corp) fail or sit outside the ladder's gates — mostly on the point-in-time ROCE bar (M6). The gates are not his selection function; they are closer to its inverse at the entry-timing margin.

One false lead, preserved deliberately: FMODA's negative "Chemplus" (PVC/anti-dumping, unmodelable) is **not** the shortlisted CPPLUS (CCTV). String-similar, different company, no doubt transfers (FMODA brief §6).

## 5. First cut at the 38

Two claim types are kept separate below: **[company]** = a lecture argues against/for this business or its price; **[rule]** = the lecture argues against the rule that admitted it, so the verdict is untrustworthy rather than the company bad.

**Weakest:**
- **ASIANPAINT [company]** — the convergence case. Four independent readers: return was mostly re-rating not EPS (ODDM 01:37:02); DCF overvalued even at a 7% discount rate, ~70% at 10% (BUFF 02:09:36-02:09:48); a competitor commissioning ~60-65% of its capacity amid 7 flat quarters (FMODB 01:14:52-01:16:00); four-year underperformer as a loved blue-chip (PALLOC 00:35:29). The ladder's own observations agree (PEG 25.95, 3y growth 1%) — and the verdict is still CANDIDATE because observations never gate. Also a data question: G0's SalesYoY 17.93% coexists with FMODB's "7 quarters in a row" of flat growth — check which quarter the snapshot reflects before trusting the pass.
- **CPPLUS [rule]** — no lecture mentions it (Chemplus ≠ CPPLUS). It is here because negative cash conversion (CFO/EBITDA -1.33%, CFO/PAT -48%) is precisely what SOIC's own cash-conversion bars exist to catch, and the empty G2 is the only reason it is a CANDIDATE.

- **DIVISLAB [company, conditional]** — interrogate the 65.5% PAT print for one-off composition (ARTBV 01:57:04) and cap the size (PALLOC); business quality itself is supported (VACRB).

The meta-finding of this section: the lectures' doubts cluster in the CANDIDATE column and their endorsements cluster in the WATCH column. That inversion — driven by the point-in-time ROCE gate and the absence of any valuation gate — is the single clearest sign that the 38-name split does not reflect the method the thresholds were mined from.
