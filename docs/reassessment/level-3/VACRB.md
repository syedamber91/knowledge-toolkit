LECTURE: Part 2 Finding Intrinsic Value Across Sectors    REF: VACRB    (transcript: 115104 chars, 1152 lines, covered: yes)

## 1. CRUX

The lecture is trying to change the decision from "apply one multiple (PE/PEG/EV-EBITDA in a fixed band) to every company" to "first identify which sector-specific variable the market is actually pricing (pipeline depth, imputed pre-sales EBITDA, embedded-EBITDA margin, subscriber/volume churn, end-customer order book, RevPAR, capital-cycle position) and only then pick the multiple that variable belongs to" — taught entirely through paired-company "which gets the higher valuation and why" thought experiments across ~14 sectors, never through a formula.

## 2. MECHANISM

- Teaching method is Socratic: he poses two or three hypothetical (or real, unnamed-then-named) companies in a sector, asks the chat "which gets the higher valuation and why," waits, then reveals the sector's real driver — repeated roughly 10 times across CDMO, generic pharma, infra/B2G, IT, real estate, wealth management, alcohol, capital goods, and auto ancillary.
- Central causal claim: a multiple is high when the market believes the *variable driving future earnings* (not trailing PAT) is durable and growing — "valuation at the end moves because the business moves... without understanding the business variable, how will you understand valuations?" (VACRB 00:27:34-00:27:46).
- He explicitly forbids reading a multiple in isolation from the industry cycle: "valuation is not a multiple... You can't understand valuation by not understanding what is happening in the industry" (VACRB 00:18:14-00:18:22), illustrated with Sun Pharma's six-year EV/EBITDA de-rating (31.4x in 2015 down through a "six year down cycle," VACRB 00:17:30-00:18:04) caused by three supply-side forces: rising US FDA plant audits, industry-wide oversupply from copycat capacity, and buyer consolidation from "9 distributors to 3 distributors" (VACRB 00:19:38-00:20:06).
- For businesses whose accounting doesn't match cash economics (real estate), he throws out P&L-based valuation entirely and substitutes an "imputed/embedded EBITDA" computed from pre-sales × historical margin, because the P&L shows a loss for years while cash is actually being collected (VACRB 00:42:47-00:43:34).
- For platform/subscription businesses he says the market prices a *leading* operating KPI (paying-customer count / churn), which can move the stock against trailing PAT growth entirely (IndiaMart case, VACRB 01:37:02-01:40:20).
- Recurring meta-point: business-model quality (branded vs. commodity, own-pipeline vs. contract, own-brand vs. distribution-only) sets the ceiling on margin durability, and margin durability — not one quarter's growth number — is what the multiple is actually pricing.
- Closing synthesis: the best long-run returns compound from *simultaneous* revenue growth and margin expansion ("twin phase bazooka" / operating leverage), not either alone (VACRB 02:00:40-02:01:04).

## 4. WHAT THE LADDER MISSES

(a) Rules the ladder encoded that this lecture conditions/qualifies:

- **pe_context-001** (f10-pe-band, PE read against a flat 15-35 reference band, applied identically to all 38 names spanning pharma, real estate, chemicals, capital goods, FMCG, paints, auto). This lecture's entire thesis is that PE has no sector-independent meaning: real estate is explicitly told not to be valued on P&L-derived multiples at all (VACRB 00:43:05-00:43:34); Radico Khaitan is cited trading at "70-80 times" PE as a *normal* branded-alcohol reading, not a red flag (VACRB 01:19:54-01:19:58); Divi's Labs' historically rich multiple is framed as pipeline-justified, not expensive (VACRB 00:08:07-00:08:16); and in capital markets, a PE *bottoming* near 30-32x (right at/above the ladder's 35 ceiling) is framed as the buy signal, not the sell signal (VACRB 01:44:52-01:45:20). The 15-35 band, applied blind to sector, would misread several of these as expensive or would give real-estate names a number that the lecture says shouldn't be trusted at all.

- **peg_ratio-001** / **cfo_to_pat-001** (PEG ≤1.5, CFO/PAT 80-95%, both P&L/PAT-derived, applied to LODHA in the 38-list): the lecture's real-estate section (VACRB 00:41:01-00:53:31) says PAT itself is structurally suppressed during a multi-year construction window ("for those 3 years, my accounting can[show] the sale zero... I have to show the expenses but I cannot show the income," VACRB 00:43:05-00:43:12), so a PAT-denominator ratio (PEG) or a PAT-based cash-conversion ratio (CFO/PAT) is reading a number the lecture explicitly says is not representative of the underlying cash economics for this business type. Note the 38-table itself already marks Lodha's CFO/PAT as unreliable-adjacent (82.06%, marked "Y"/pass) and PEG as 0.54 ("Y"/pass) — this lecture is the specific reason those passes deserve a skeptical second look for a real-estate name, not blind trust.

(b) Central points the ladder has no rule for at all:

- **Real-estate imputed/embedded EBITDA valuation.** No ladder rule (hard, soft, or observation) replaces P&L-based metrics with a pre-sales × historical-margin construct for real-estate names. This is the single most load-bearing miss given LODHA is a live CANDIDATE.
- **CDMO/pharma pipeline depth** (count of commercialized vs. pre-registration/phase-2 molecules) as the dominant valuation driver for contract-manufacturing pharma names (VACRB 00:10:14-00:10:32). Relevant to DIVISLAB, LAURUSLABS, NEULANDLAB, GLENMARK, AJANTPHARM in the 38-list — the ladder has nothing that reads con-call/PPT pipeline disclosure.
- **End-customer/end-market growth tracking** for ancillary/supplier businesses (auto ancillary, capital goods) — the ladder screens the supplier's own ratios but has no signal for customer concentration or end-market cyclicality (VACRB 01:29:44-01:31:29).
- **Platform-business leading indicators** (paying-customer count, churn) as a valuation driver that can run opposite to trailing PAT growth (VACRB 01:37:02-01:40:20). No name in the 38-list is a pure marketplace platform, but CARTRADE (an auto marketplace) is adjacent enough to be worth a mention — the ladder currently reads only its PE/D-E/ROCE, none of which would catch a churn-driven de-rating of this kind.
- **Sector-appropriate multiple selection itself.** The ladder's pe_context-001 is the only valuation-multiple rule and it is one number applied uniformly; this lecture's central and repeated claim across ~14 sectors is that this is the wrong methodology — the multiple to even look at changes by sector (EV/EBITDA vs. price/CFO vs. price/sales vs. embedded-EBITDA vs. PEG).

(c) Dated one-company worked examples being treated as a universal bar: none found. The lecture's specific numeric bars (real estate >12-13x embedded EBITDA, hospital <25x market-cap/cash-flow, hotel 15-20% ROE/ROC) are not currently encoded anywhere in the ladder's rules or observations, so there is no live risk of the ladder having over-generalized one of them yet — but they are exactly the kind of number that would need a "which sector does this apply to" guard if ever added.
