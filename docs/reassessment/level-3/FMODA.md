LECTURE: Part 1 Financial Modelling (Level 3 — How to Value a Company & Portfolio Creation!)    REF: FMODA    (transcript: 75550 chars, 470 lines, covered: yes)

## 1. CRUX

The lecture is trying to change one habit: stop reading a company's current PE/valuation as a static fact and start building a rough, forward (1-3 year) PAT forecast off management's own guidance — then apply bull/base/bear exit multiples to that forecast — before deciding a stock is cheap or expensive; a single trailing ratio, however extreme, tells you nothing on its own.

## 2. MECHANISM

- Core forecasting loop, taught first as a toy model (Sid's Samosa Shop) then scaled to real companies: take management's own guidance for sales growth and EBITDA/PAT margin, multiply forward Sales x EBITDA margin, then walk down through depreciation, interest, and other income to PBT, then PAT — "roughly right" round numbers, not a precise Excel build. "An idea is to be roughly right and not be precisely wrong." (FMODA 00:09:22)
- Apply an exit PE multiple to the forward PAT to get a forward market cap, and always run it as three scenarios (different exit multiples) rather than one number, producing a return/IRR spread across bear, base and bull cases. "This is the risk reward calculation When my bear case is also getting 10% return I think the risk reward graph is favorable" (FMODA 00:54:59-00:56:32)
- The exit multiple itself is not a fixed input — it is perception, which the instructor explicitly decomposes into three drivers: "The P ratio is very dependent upon perception What is the perception dependent on?" (FMODA 00:28:05), "It depends on industry growth." (FMODA 00:28:07), "Second perception is dependent on sentiments." (FMODA 00:28:10), "And C is dependent on liquidity." (FMODA 00:28:18) — so the same forecast PAT can support very different market caps depending on narrative, independent of the earnings model being right.
- A subset of businesses is explicitly ruled out of this exercise altogether — anything where the core input (selling price, order recurrence) is unknowable, such as commodity oversupply/anti-dumping exposure or one-off order-book-driven demand. "Now we cannot model such a company" (FMODA 00:23:21); "those who are not forecastable, we cannot make forecasts" (FMODA 00:24:14)
- Guidance is aspirational and must be checked against delivery over time before the forward model built off it is trusted further — margin delivery, debt paydown, etc., tracked quarter to quarter. "how do we know we believe the management or not, so what you have to do is that you have to see the results of every six months, the results of every quarter Whether the management is delivering or not" (FMODA 00:44:10)
- The PEG ratio (forward PE divided by expected PAT growth) is used as the instructor's own summary heuristic for whether growth priced into the stock is reasonable, but only after the full bottom-up forward model is built — not read off trailing screener numbers. "So below 1.5-1.6 peg ratio, it gets interesting" (FMODA 00:58:09-00:58:12)
- Fundamentals and technicals are framed as two separate jobs: "Technicals is the study of perception Fundamentals is the study of earnings Fundamentals tell you what to buy technicals tell you when to buy and when to exit." (FMODA 01:00:01-01:00:25)

## 4. WHAT THE LADDER MISSES

(a) Rules the ladder encoded that this lecture conditions or qualifies:

- **peg_ratio-001** (target <=1.5x, ref INSIGN 01:37:05, F38): FMODA independently reaches the same threshold — "So below 1.5-1.6 peg ratio, it gets interesting" (FMODA 00:58:09-00:58:12) — but computes it on a *forward*, guidance-derived PE and growth rate (built through a full bottom-up model: sales guidance x margin guidance -> EBITDA -> PAT -> forward market cap -> forward PE), not on the trailing/point-in-time numbers the ladder reads from screener.in. The threshold corroborates, but the method it's meant to be applied to is different from what the ladder computes — a company can pass or fail 1.5x on trailing data for the opposite reason it would pass or fail on the forward-modeled number this lecture insists on.
- **growth_trap_flag-001** (PE < 30 reference band, ref TVGPF 00:18:39-00:19:07): FMODA's own worked examples span PE ratios well below and well above 30 without treating 30 as a discriminating line — Wari Renewables is flagged as a trap at PE 260 via a drawdown-under-best-case test, not a PE-band test; Trill's re-rate/de-rate discussion happens around PE 72; KEI is treated as a plausible bull-case candidate at 26.6-27x forward and 36x on one calculation, never flagged as a trap despite crossing back and forth over 30. This qualifies growth_trap_flag-001: the actual test this lecture applies is to build a bear-case model and check whether even that case still clears an acceptable return (FMODA 00:54:59-00:56:32), not a static PE cutoff — a fixed <30 screen would both miss Wari Renewables' problem and mis-flag KEI.
- **pe_context-001** (15-35 reference band, provenance ref: null): the instructor's own personal buying range of roughly 20-30 PE with 15-25% growth (FMODA 01:07:36) sits close to this band, but he explicitly disclaims it as generalizable — "This is my case study, it can be not be yours also" (FMODA 01:09:33) — a caveat the ladder's static reference-band framing doesn't carry.

(b) Central points this lecture makes that the ladder has no rule for at all:

- The entire forward-modeling methodology — projecting PAT 1-3 years out from management's own guidance and applying bull/base/bear exit multiples to get an IRR spread (FMODA 00:29:43-00:30:40, 00:54:59-00:56:32). Every rule in the 16-entry ladder reads a trailing or point-in-time (or at most 3-year-historical) ratio; none forecast forward off stated guidance.
- Order-book / revenue-durability composition — whether growth is driven by a one-off bulk order versus recurring demand (FMODA 00:32:06-00:38:14). No rule or observation checks order-book age, concentration, or one-off vs recurring mix, even though the lecture treats this as decisive for whether reported growth is trustworthy.
- Guidance-delivery tracking over time — checking each quarter whether management is actually hitting its own previously stated guidance before trusting a model built off it (FMODA 00:44:10-00:45:17). No rule reads guidance-vs-actual delivery; the ladder's checks are all single-snapshot or trailing-window.
- Competitive-entry threat to the multiple, and certification/barrier-to-entry duration as a mitigant (FMODA 00:46:54-00:48:14). No rule captures new-entrant competitive intensity or moat duration.
- Peer-margin plausibility check — cross-referencing a company's guided target margin against an already-scaled peer's realized margin (FMODA 00:48:36-00:49:19). No rule does peer cross-referencing of any kind.
- Unforecastable-business flag for commodity oversupply / anti-dumping exposure (FMODA 00:23:21-00:24:14). This is a genuine candidate for **G2**, the forensic-veto gate CONTEXT.md flags as completely empty (no rule means nothing can ever be REJECTED under it) — this lecture is direct evidence of what a G2 rule should catch, but it is [SOFT]/[JUDGE] (needs conference-call or annual-report reading), not something screener.in exposes, so it cannot become a G2 rule without adding a new (non-screener) data source.
