LECTURE: Art of Business Valuation-Different Methods    REF: ARTBV    (transcript: 138480 chars, 1431 lines, covered: yes)

## 1. CRUX

This lecture is trying to change one decision: stop reading every stock through a single P/E lens, and instead (a) pick the valuation multiple that fits the sector's actual economics, and (b) run a disciplined, non-emotional exit system (technical + fundamental) so that "when to sell" stops being a gut call driven by fear or greed — the instructor frames this explicitly through the VVGP (person/thing/event/situation) behavioural-finance lens he opens with (ARTBV 00:03:25-00:08:58).

## 2. MECHANISM

- P/E breaks down in identifiable, recurring situations, not randomly: one-off/non-recurring income inflating trailing PAT (Aarti Industries: "your PE ratio which we saw last week was around 15x" (ARTBV 00:13:33) became "So that PE ratio has now gone to 32 times" (ARTBV 00:13:33) once "it was nearly Rs.600 crores of termination income, which was given to them by the contract cancelation" (ARTBV 00:14:35) unwound); turnarounds; a project just gone commercial before sales ramp, where "the depreciation ends up hitting the PNL and your OPEX also starts hitting your PNL" (ARTBV 00:19:21), so "the PE ratio might look artificially elevated as well" (ARTBV 00:19:33); and float-cornered stocks, where "whenever you see obscene valuations in companies always it's because of float cornering" (ARTBV 00:22:27).
- Different sectors need different multiples because their economics differ: "the P ratio will not work in life insurance companies, will not work in banks, will not work in real estate companies because there are different metrics to value them" (ARTBV 00:40:20), and "in deep pre-cyclicals, you have to look for price to book" (ARTBV 00:40:30). He then walks a full sector→metric table: banks/NBFC/insurance → P/B (or price-to-embedded-value for insurers), hospitals → EV/EBITDA or EV/cash-flow, IT/platforms/exchanges → P/E, P/CF, DCF, chemicals/building materials → P/E or P/CF, cyclicals/auto-ancillary-in-losses → P/B, FMCG → P/E or DCF (ARTBV 00:40:20-00:42:47).
- Banks get valued on P/B because they are ~10x-leveraged businesses where "price to book value is determined by how well they enhance the spreads and how well they contain their NPAs" (ARTBV 00:49:27); he cross-checks this by comparing HDFC/ICICI/Kotak/Axis Bank's P/B against their stated ROE and ROA (ARTBV 00:52:43-00:54:31).
- Hospitals get valued on EV/EBITDA or EV/cash-flow because "in hospitals, there is no working capital" (ARTBV 01:44:56) so "your EBITDA ends up becoming your cash flow" (ARTBV 01:45:08), while greenfield capex depresses reported earnings for years — "For a hospital to break even in a new geography, the average time taken is 6 to 8 years" (ARTBV 01:46:51).
- The exit mechanism is a monthly-candle, 10-month-lookback ATR-style "volatility stop" indicator on TradingView: "search for an indicator by the name of volatility stock" (ARTBV 00:24:01), where "you can use a multiplier of two if the valuations are still okay then you can let it be at a multiplier of 2.5 times" (ARTBV 00:24:29) for a 2-4 year horizon investor, widening to "a multiplier of three or 3.5 times" (ARTBV 00:36:26) for a 5-15 year horizon investor; "The time to sell is on the first red sign" (ARTBV 01:58:29).
- Fundamentally, buy discipline is governed by an over-earning/under-earning, margin-of-safety-vs-zone-of-danger frame: "over earning is when a company earns more than average profits due to some shortage or one of orders" (ARTBV 01:55:21), and the most dangerous buy point is when "peak margins plus peak P ratios" (ARTBV 01:54:13) coincide, because "only one negative surprise is required for the D rating to start" (ARTBV 01:54:09). The safer buy point is when a company is under-earning below its own long-run average margin band with the fundamental thesis intact.
- Re-entry/bottom-fishing discipline: never average into a falling stock — "If you go for bottom fishing in a freefall scenario, you will likely get killed" (ARTBV 02:11:38) — wait until "you wait for the stock to stabilize in a sideways trend" (ARTBV 02:07:08), i.e. a flattening weekly EMA base.

## 4. WHAT THE LADDER MISSES

(a) Rules the ladder encoded that this lecture conditions or qualifies:

1. **pe_context-001** (f10-pe-band, "a DCF (or reverse-PE at 15–35× band) gives intrinsic value") is qualified hard: this lecture says flatly that "the P ratio will not work in life insurance companies, will not work in banks, will not work in real estate companies because there are different metrics to value them" (ARTBV 00:40:20), and separately that cyclicals need P/B (ARTBV 00:40:30-00:42:30). Unlike leverage-001 and capital_efficiency_gate-001, which already carry `requires_attribute: {is_lender: "false"}`, pe_context-001 carries no such exclusion — so today the ladder would silently read a bank's or a cyclical's PE against a 15-35x band the lecture explicitly says is the wrong lens for those sectors.

2. **growth_trap_flag-001** (f29-growth-trap-pe-band) is qualified: the lecture operationalizes the "growth trap" idea with a concrete mechanism — reverse-DCF-implied required growth ("30% growth is stock to justify... for the next 20 years" (ARTBV 00:29:40)) — and pairs it with a companion exit rule (the volatility stop) rather than treating "PE below the trap threshold" alone as safe. It also generalizes the danger condition beyond growth-trap PE bands specifically to "peak margins plus peak P ratios" (ARTBV 01:54:13) in any sector, which growth_trap_flag-001's PE-only framing does not capture.

3. **growth_durability-001** (f18-sitting-duck-revenue-growth, sales_growth_3y_pct >= 20) is qualified: the rule treats all 3-year sales growth as equal, but the lecture insists "the better quality of sales growth is volume growth" (ARTBV 01:30:53) — a company can clear the 20% bar on price-led growth alone (Colgate's revenue growth vs. its "1.8% of volume" (ARTBV 01:31:26)) and still be the lower-quality growth story the instructor would discount.

(b) Central points this lecture makes that the ladder has no rule for at all:

1. **Sector-differentiated valuation multiple selection** is the organizing spine of the entire lecture (P/B for banks/cyclicals, EV/EBITDA or EV/cash-flow for hospitals, price-to-embedded-value for insurers) — and G6 (valuation) is confirmed empty in CONTEXT.md. None of it is encoded anywhere in the 16-rule ladder.

2. **The volatility-stop systematic exit indicator** (monthly, 10-month ATR trailing stop, 2x/2.5x multiplier) is this lecture's answer to "when to sell." G8 in the ladder is an entry-only gate (weekly RSI≥50, weekly ADX≥20); there is no corresponding exit rule anywhere in the ladder.

3. **The over-earning/under-earning, margin-band-position framework** — buy below a company's own long-run average margin with thesis intact, avoid buying at simultaneous peak-margin-peak-PE — has no analogue. The ladder's ROCE and cash-conversion observations read a level or a direction, never a company's current margin relative to its own historical band.

4. **Base formation / no-bottom-fishing-in-freefall** — wait for a sideways price base before adding to a beaten-down name — has no rule. G8's weekly RSI/ADX are fresh-trend entry filters, not a re-entry/averaging-down discipline for names already owned or already fallen.

5. **One-off/non-recurring income detection** (contract termination income, a single COVID-drug molecule, commodity/inventory gains) distorting PAT and hence PE — nothing in the ladder flags or adjusts for this, and `canslim_pat-001` (pat_growth_yoy_pct >= 20) is directly exposed to exactly the Aarti Industries-style distortion this lecture uses as its opening case study.

6. **Free-float / promoter-concentration check** — "obscene valuations... because of float cornering" (ARTBV 00:22:27) is a concrete, screener.in-computable signal (shareholding pattern) the ladder does not fetch or gate on anywhere.

(c) Dated or one-company worked examples that should not be read as universal bars:

1. The 2x/2.5x (or 3x/3.5x for longer horizons) volatility-stop multiplier is explicitly the instructor's personal calibration to his own 2-4 year holding horizon (ARTBV 00:36:08-00:36:29), not a fixed universal parameter — it should never be hard-coded as a single number if this signal is ever implemented.

2. The South Indian Bank (forward PE ~3x, forward P/B ~0.56x, "57% CAGR" projection, ARTBV 01:07:36-01:13:59) and IIFL Finance (book value target ₹329, "2.5 times" or "3 times" peer P/B → ₹824-989, ARTBV 01:18:59-01:19:26) models are single-company, con-call-guidance-driven point estimates for FY23/FY24 — a specific management's specific cost-to-income and credit-cost guidance from roughly 2022-2023 con-calls, now well over three years stale relative to today (2026-08-23). None of the specific multiples (2.5x, 3x P/B; 35-38% cost-to-income) generalize past those two companies at that moment.

3. The HDFC/ICICI/Kotak/Axis P/B-vs-ROE/ROA snapshot (3.3x/2.8x/3.4x/1.9x P/B, ARTBV 00:52:43-00:54:17) is a single point-in-time market-pricing comparison that moves with the rate and NPA cycle — not a durable P/B-to-ROE mapping.
