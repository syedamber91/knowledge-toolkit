## 1. CRUX

This lecture is not about picking stocks at all — it is trying to change how much money you put behind a stock you have already picked, by handing you two named sizing formulas (2-6-8 and 3-6-9) tied to a core/satellite split, so that a bad pick barely dents the portfolio and a great pick actually moves it.

## 2. MECHANISM

- Two allocation frameworks are offered as alternatives, not a progression the investor must follow in order: "first allocation framework is 268" (VCONC 00:00:28), later generalized upward to "3, 6 and 9" once "you start becoming more and more experienced as an investor" (VCONC 00:02:45-00:02:53).
- Every portfolio is split into a core portfolio and a satellite portfolio, using a cricket-team metaphor: core = the settled starting XI, satellite = "players which are on the Indian cricket team bench" (VCONC 00:01:00-00:01:22).
- Core portfolio membership is defined by three traits together: "your ROC is high, where your track record is established or where you see that the growth is very, very linear for next three, four years" (VCONC 00:04:02-00:04:12), i.e. "You already see a runway for growth." (VCONC 00:04:12-00:04:14)
- Satellite portfolio membership is the mirror image: "companies which have shaky business models or business models which are just evolving or your understanding of those business models isn't correct." (VCONC 00:03:53-00:04:02)
- Sizing is asymmetric by design so a satellite loser cannot hurt you but a core/high-conviction winner can meaningfully help you — proven with two of the instructor's own worked numbers: a 2%-allocation stock that fell ~70% cost the portfolio "barely 1.4%" (VCONC 00:01:34-00:01:56), while an 8%-allocation stock that rose ~400% added "almost 32%" (VCONC 00:02:01-00:02:23).
- The instructor explicitly ties the SIZE of a position to the STRENGTH of the thesis, not to enthusiasm alone: the 8% bet was justified because "I had a superior understanding of the business model. Valuations were cheaper. There were a lot of catalysts at play." (VCONC 00:02:23-00:02:30), naming the catalysts as "company was deleveraging, there was capex which was happening, company was going into downstream derivatives of chemicals." (VCONC 00:02:30-00:02:39)
- The whole framework closes on a behavioral instruction that measurement should happen at the portfolio level, not the position level: "you should as a retail investor always measure your returns on a portfolio level and do not get disturbed by particularly individual stock level fluctuations" (VCONC 00:04:39-00:05:14), summarized as "You have to water your winners... and you have to cut down the weeds which are losers" (VCONC 00:05:14-00:05:35).

## 4. WHAT THE LADDER MISSES

This lecture is almost entirely category (b): it is a stand-alone layer of the SOIC method — position sizing / portfolio construction — that the current 16-rule ladder has no representation of whatsoever. The ladder (per CONTEXT.md) only computes entry gates (G0 growth, G1 leverage, G3 ROCE, G8 technical entry) plus a set of non-gating valuation/quality observations; it stops the moment a company is classified CANDIDATE/WATCH. Nothing downstream of that classification — how much of the portfolio to put behind a CANDIDATE, or whether a CANDIDATE belongs in "core" vs "satellite" — exists anywhere in the current rulebook or observation set.

Specific gaps:
- No rule or observation encodes the core/satellite classification (VCONC 00:03:53-00:04:12) at all. This is a real, load-bearing distinction in the instructor's method — it is the difference between whether a stock gets 2-3% or 6-9% of the book — and the ladder currently outputs a flat CANDIDATE/WATCH verdict with no sizing dimension.
- No rule or observation encodes the sizing caps (satellite ≤10-20%, core ≥80-90%, VCONC 00:04:14-00:04:35). Even if the ladder cannot compute a live portfolio (it does not track a user's actual holdings), it could at minimum flag "this classifies as core-eligible" vs "satellite-only" per company, which it does not do today.
- No existing rule is conditioned or qualified by this lecture in the (a) sense — none of the 16 rules concern position sizing, so there is nothing here to narrow or override. The closest overlap is thematic, not mechanical: the core-portfolio ROC criterion here ("your ROC is high," VCONC 00:04:02-00:04:12) echoes the existing `capital_efficiency-001` observation (ROCE ≥20%, ref SOICA6), but this lecture does not restate a number — it only says ROC should be "high," so it adds no new threshold, just reinforces that ROCE quality gates a sizing decision, not only an admit/reject decision.
- Category (c) does not really apply here: unlike some other lectures, this one does not present a dated, one-company worked example AS a universal numeric bar. The two dollar-figure anecdotes (70% drawdown / 2% impact; 400% gain / 32% impact, VCONC 00:01:34-00:02:23) are illustrative arithmetic to justify the sizing logic, not proposed thresholds — the actual thresholds stated (2/3/6/8/9%, 10/20% cap, 80/90% floor) are explicit, general framework numbers, not back-fitted from the anecdote.
